# Records to write at implementation (015)

The product specialist's plan review (M1 – M9, 2026-10-30) supplied this copy; the records task pastes it, replacing "2026-11-xx" with the merge date and filling the figures and the SHA at the bless. Public spelling is "behavior".

## Guide: "Scheduled changes and the change log" (the section title; not "timed")

Lead paragraph:

> Since 0.16 a change of a live control (the route, the volume position, the playback speed level) can carry a **position**: the index of the output sample it is the first to affect, in the stream's own clock. The engine applies it on that sample whatever the host's block size, and reports every change a stream applies, scheduled or set plainly, as a **change entry** with the position it landed on. **Positioning a change on a sample is a feature of the emulator's API, not a device behavior: the recorder's own controls move whenever a hand moves them.** Nothing about the modeled sound changes, no assumption is registered, and with no scheduled change every output is bit-identical to 0.15. It is what a host needs to play a session back the same way at any buffer size, to apply a preset or a scripted sequence of positions, and to show what changed when from the engine's own record rather than from what the host sent.

Boxed note:

> **An emulator convention, not a device behavior.** The device has no way to schedule a control; the engine's positions exist so that a host's changes land on the same sample at every block size. A change entry records where the engine applied a change, not where a hand would have moved a knob.

Also in the guide: tag 0 is reserved for plain-setter changes, use tags from 1 (M3); the control index is the control manifest's, looked up by identifier at start-up, never assumed (M4; the C snippet and the smoke look it up with `rr_dr60_control`); the refill recipe for schedules longer than 64 (keep the list sorted; after each call, while `pending_changes < 64` and the list is not empty, schedule the next) and the drain rule (the next item's changes are scheduled only after `finished`) (M6); the log is a replayable schedule **given** the same recording and settings (playback) or the same input, seed and recording counter (one-pass), the record side being spec 017's (M7); an entry is reported by the call that produced the sample, never by an empty call (plan F1); the speed change's audible lag (M1: the asserted identity with `playback_latency_samples`, or the helper).

Wording to use: position, lands on, change entry, the stream's clock, scheduled, plain setter, bit-identical at every block size, checked, modeled. Avoid: provable/proof/proves, audit, forensic, evidence, record of truth, timestamp, "event" for a change entry, assistant/AI/automation, "fade" as a feature, "replays a session bit for bit" without its conditions, sample-accurate, verified, behaviour.

## CHANGELOG 0.16.0 (the 0.15.0 style)

> - **Scheduled control changes and the change log** (spec 015, 2026-11-xx; version 0.16.0; no audio change). A change of a live control (the route, the volume position, the playback speed level) can be **scheduled at a position** in a stream's own clock, the host output samples the stream has produced (the player's from `start_playback`, the drain included; the one-pass's from construction, `reset` and `reconfigure`, the clock the voice-activation events use), and lands on exactly that sample at any block size: `Pipeline::schedule_change(stream, position, control, value, tag)` / `rr_dr60_schedule_change` (the stream `Stream::{Playback, OnePass}`, `Record` reserved for the record-side values of spec 017; the control the control manifest's index; the route and the volume on both streams, the speed on the playback stream; a position already passed lands on the next sample produced). Each stream keeps a schedule of up to 64 pending changes (`SCHEDULE_CAPACITY`) in position order, reserved at construction; a 65th is refused (`ScheduleFull`, C status 27) and a control not on the stream (`ControlNotOnStream`, 28); `pending_changes`, `stream_position` and `clear_schedule` read and clear it; the playback schedule is kept by `start_playback` and cleared when a playback ends by any path. Every change a stream applies, scheduled or by a plain setter, is reported by the processing call that produced the sample as a **change entry** (`ChangeEntry` / `RrDr60ChangeEntry`, 40 bytes, no size field: on the pre-1.0 ABI-pass list with the play report): the landed position, the control, the values before and after (chained per stream) and the caller's tag (0 for a plain setter), through `process_with_events_and_changes` / `play_with_changes` (`MAX_CHANGES_PER_CALL` = 67 is always enough; `BlockInfo` and `PlayInfo` gain `changes`; in C the count is an out-parameter and the report structs are unchanged). The plain setters keep their meaning (the next call's first produced sample). `changes::speed_landing(P, rate)` / `rr_dr60_speed_landing` gives the device-rate index a speed change lands on (audible about one playback latency later); `recording_counter()` / `rr_dr60_recording_counter` reads the counter a one-pass replay needs beside the seed (spec 012, A-049). Positioning a change on a sample is **a feature of the emulator's API, not a device behavior**: the recorder's own controls move whenever a hand moves them; no assumption is registered, no model version moves, the control manifest's canonical text is unchanged (the three controls stay "live"), and with no scheduled change every output is bit-identical to 0.15 (the ten audio golden files, their SHA guards and every smoke hash unmoved); the stage manifest's canonical text was **re-blessed once** for the version line and `Pipeline::schedule_change` in the API items of stages 9 and 11. New golden `golden-changes-v1.json` (N entries: output digests and full logs over the schedules at seven rates; blessed once, SHA `…`), the C smoke's `--changes` case diffed against `examples/changes_rows.rs`, test hooks `ChangeMutation`, and `scripts/ab-timing.sh <rev> changes` (the idle cost against 0.15, asserted: 3 % on the development machine, 5 % on the CI runner's `ab-timing` label; the cost with one pending change per block reported). Cost on the development machine (solo A/B against `f1449c2`, 48 kHz, 4096 blocks, speaker route, codec bypassed, the fastest of 45 runs × 3 alternations): the player **+x.xx ns (+x.xx %)**, the one-pass **+x.xx ns (+x.xx %)**; with one change per block +x.xx ns (reported).
