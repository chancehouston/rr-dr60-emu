# Feature Specification: Optional Output Clamp (a host option at the route output)

**Feature Branch**: `010-output-clamp`

**Created**: 2026-10-15

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description (abridged; the full text is in the specify session's transcript): "An optional output clamp at the end of the playback chain (feature 010, queued by spec 009 on 2026-10-15). Since spec 009 the engine's default output is the selected route's output. The speaker route's stage 11 is bounded at full scale by the amplifier's clipper, and the speaker template's gain peaks at 1.0, but the template's resonance rings on transients and on clipped material: a full-scale square wave, or a full-scale tone at volume position 1, reads about 1.9 (+5.7 dB over full scale) after the template at 48 kHz (A-042). The earphone route passes the playback filter's reconstruction overshoot scaled by the position. Spec 009 kept this documented, not clamped, and the README tells hosts to limit or clip before converting to integer formats. A host-selectable clamp that bounds the route output to ±1.0 on both routes, after the route's last stage, OFF by default: the default output of 0.9 unchanged bit for bit, every golden file unmoved, no manifest row (an emulator convenience, not a device behavior). Applies at the route-output tap only; the per-stage taps stay unclamped. No state, no latency, no allocation; deterministic. A settings field in Rust and C; version 0.10.0."

## Overview

Since [spec 009](../009-output-chain/spec.md) the emulator's default output is the selected route's output: the signal after the speaker template on the speaker route, or after the earphone output on the earphone route. Each route's output is scaled so that a sample of 1.0 is that route's electrical full swing ([A-042](../../docs/hardware/assumptions.md)). The output is not bounded at that figure. On the speaker route the amplifier's clipper bounds stage 11 at full scale and the speaker template's frequency-response gain peaks at 1.0, so a steady tone below the knee never exceeds full scale, but the template's resonance rings on transients and on clipped material: a full-scale square wave, or a full-scale tone at volume position 1, reads about 1.9 after the template at 48 kHz (+5.7 dB over full scale). On the earphone route the playback filter's reconstruction overshoot and the coupling corners' own overshoot on square-edged material, scaled by the position, carry the output above full scale near the top of the travel. Spec 009 decided to keep this **documented, not clamped** (its US1 coach review): the overshoot is the model's own ringing, as a real driver's cone overshoots in its own way, and the README tells hosts to leave headroom or clamp before converting to 16-bit or other integer formats.

That leaves every integer-buffer host writing the same clamp, and the engine's own C smoke loop and parity checks cannot show a bounded output. This feature adds the **output clamp** as a host option: when a host turns it on, no route-output sample leaves the engine above +1.0 or below −1.0; a sample inside that range passes unchanged, so everything spec 009 measured below the knee is bit-identical with the option on or off.

The option is **off by default** and is **not a stage of the device**. The device bounds its electrical output where the model already does (the amplifier's clipper, A-031) and where a bound is registered but not modeled (the earphone follower's cut-off, A-032); the option bounds a number after the transducer templates, which corresponds to nothing in the device. So the default output of version 0.9 is unchanged bit for bit, the five existing golden files and their guards stay unmoved, the stage manifest's fifteen device stages gain no row and no changed string, and the option is described everywhere as an **emulator convenience**: "clamp" and "output clamp" name only the engine option, while "clip" keeps its spec 009 meaning (the amplifier's clipping, a device behavior). The register entry A-042 names the option as the host's alternative to leaving headroom.

Two things the option is not: it is not a limiter (no gain control, no time constants, no knee; a sample is either inside the bound or replaced by it), and it is not a change to any tap before the route output. The points after stage 11, after stage 12 and after stage 13 keep reporting the unbounded signal, so the overshoot stays measurable and a host that wants the raw figure selects that tap. The three choices the description left open were settled by the user at the specify step and are recorded under Clarifications.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-10-15 (specify: the three choices the description left open, answered by the user; the coach review C1–C17 applied)

- Q: Is the option a settings field (fixed at configuration) or an engine control turned at any time, like the volume and the route? → A: **A settings field**, in the output settings group beside the three stage bypasses, in Rust and in the C settings structure: a static property of the host's output format, not something turned during playback; a change needs a reconfigure, which resets the paths (spec 006). The user chose this "for now" and deferred a wider discussion of which settings should become live controls in the app (and of an AI-facing control surface) to a later consult; it is not part of this feature. The bypassed-chain preset keeps the option off, so 0.9 equivalence holds for every existing configuration.
- Q: A hard bound at exactly ±1.0, or a host-set ceiling, or a soft knee? → A: **A hard bound at exactly ±1.0** on each route's own scale (A-042): full scale is the integer-buffer boundary the hosts need, a host that wants headroom scales its own output, and anything softer is a second limiter with no device basis. Rejected: a host-set ceiling (a float field with its own refusals, duplicating host gain) and a soft knee (a limiter with distortion of its own and a design script to pin).
- Q: Does the bound also apply to the per-stage taps after stages 11, 12 and 13, and to the after-playback tap when the chain is bypassed? → A: **No on both counts.** Only the route-output tap is bounded, so the unbounded figures stay measurable at the per-stage taps; a host that bypasses the three stages and wants the bound selects the route output, where the option still applies (the chain's bypass does not switch the option off). Consequence (coach C13): with the option on, the route-output tap and the selected route's last per-stage tap (or the after-playback tap when the chain is bypassed) are no longer the same signal; the tap documentation says so.
- Q: Is the default on or off? → A: **Off.** The device has no such bound; on by default would change 0.9's default output and every host's audio without a host decision, and the register could not describe it as a device behavior. Rejected: on by default "because integer hosts need it", because the audiophile audience and the measurement harness want the unbounded figure and because the five golden files would all move.
- Q: A manifest row, a count or a string? → A: **No row, no count change, no entry string change.** The manifest describes the device's fifteen stages, and its "not modeled here" notes name device gaps, which an engine option is not. The canonical text's header carries the library version, so the bump to 0.10.0 moves it: **one recorded re-bless for the header's version only** (coach C2).
- Q: Which golden file? → A: **A new golden file for the option on**, at the seven host rates with the stimuli below, blessed once; the five existing files and their SHA guards are unmoved by rule.
- Q: Is a new assumption ID needed for "not a device behavior"? → A: **No** (coach C12). The bound's value is A-042's full scale and the code cites A-042; the default (off) traces to this spec's Clarifications; A-042's status line gains "amended by spec 010 (2026-10-15): the output clamp". A new ID would register an assumption with no hardware content and no verification method.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Bounded output for integer-buffer hosts (Priority: P1)

An app developer writes the engine's output into 16-bit buffers for playback or for a file. With the option on, the developer converts every output sample to 16-bit without headroom and without a clamp of their own, by scaling with 32767 (or with 32768 and saturating +1.0 to 32767), as the README states, on either route and at any volume position; the clipped and transient material that read about 1.9 on the speaker route in 0.9 now reads at most 1.0. With the option off, the developer gets exactly 0.9's output and the README's headroom advice. An audiophile keeps the option off, or reads the per-stage tap, to hear and measure the model's own overshoot.

**Why this priority**: this is the whole feature: the one output guarantee the integer-buffer hosts asked for since spec 009, delivered as an option the device-faithful default does not pay for.

**Independent Test**: can be fully tested with a fresh engine at each supported host rate. The **primary stimulus is synthetic input above full scale with every stage bypassed** (input above ±1.0 is accepted and kept exactly, spec 001): a 1 kHz sine of amplitude 1.5, which exceeds the bound at every host rate by construction and needs no stage's behavior (constitution VII). The **secondary stimuli** run in spec 009's output-chain-only configuration (every earlier stage bypassed, the three output stages on): a full-scale square wave at position 1 on each route, and a full-scale 1 kHz tone at position 1 on the speaker route, the material that overshoots today; their per-rate peaks are reported, not toleranced. The oracle for every stimulus is the **sample-wise identity** of FR-005: the option-on output equals the option-off output with each sample bounded. The memory, the export and the C API are not needed. P1 alone is a usable engine with the bounded output.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** every stage bypassed, the option **on**, **When** a 1 kHz sine of amplitude 1.5 is played, **Then** the route output equals the option-off output bounded per FR-005, sample for sample, no sample exceeds ±1.0 and at least one equals a bound, at the seven host rates and on both routes; the after-playback tap reads the unbounded signal (peak above 1.0).
2. **Given** the output-chain-only configuration at 48 kHz, the speaker route, position 1 and the option **on**, **When** a full-scale 1 kHz square wave is played, **Then** the route output equals the option-off output bounded per FR-005 and no sample exceeds ±1.0; **When** the same is played with the option **off**, **Then** the route output is bit-identical to version 0.9's and its peak exceeds 1.0 (the figure, about 1.9 at 48 kHz in this chain-only configuration and about 2.05 in the 0.9 default on a full-scale tone, is reported per rate, not toleranced, and never exceeds the speaker template's L1 norm).
3. **Given** the same configuration at each of the seven host rates and both routes, **When** the secondary stimuli are played with the option on, **Then** each route output equals the option-off output bounded per FR-005; the option-off peak per stimulus and rate is reported, and which of them exceed the bound at which rates is recorded by the plan as a verified precondition (a stimulus that does not exceed it at a rate is reported, and the identity still holds).
4. **Given** the option on, the speaker route at the reference position, **When** a full-scale 1 kHz tone is played, **Then** the route output equals the option-off output bounded per FR-005, and the count of option-off samples beyond ±1.0 is reported per rate (expected zero, as spec 009 states at 48 kHz: a steady tone below the knee).
5. **Given** the option on and a stimulus that overshoots, **When** the output is read at the tap after stage 11, after stage 12 (speaker route) or after stage 13 (earphone route), **Then** each reads the unbounded signal, bit-identical to the option off; only the route-output tap is bounded.
6. **Given** a bounded sample, **When** the host inspects it, **Then** it is exactly +1.0 or exactly −1.0 (the bound replaces the sample; no scaling, no knee, no time behavior), and the output has no new latency.

### Edge Cases

- **Samples exactly at the bound.** +1.0 and −1.0 pass unchanged; the bound is inclusive.
- **Non-finite values.** The route output is always finite (spec 001 sanitizes the input; spec 002 R-07 saturates the output to the largest finite value), so the clamp sees finite samples only, and a saturated sample is bounded like any other. A core unit check feeds the bound ±∞ and a not-a-number directly and asserts ±1.0 and 0.0.
- **Volume position 0.** The output is exactly zero from the next block as in spec 009; the option changes nothing (0.0 is inside the bound; bit-identical with the option on or off).
- **Output chain bypassed.** The option still bounds the route-output tap (the bypass is a measurement configuration; the bound is a host decision); the after-playback tap is never bounded.
- **The per-stage taps.** Never bounded (after stage 11, 12, 13 and every earlier tap), with the option on or off. With the option on, the route-output tap therefore differs from the selected route's last per-stage tap (and from the after-playback tap when the chain is bypassed) wherever a sample was bounded.
- **Route change mid-playback.** The option is route-independent; the bound applies to whichever route's output is selected at each block.
- **Reconfigure.** The option is read at configuration like the other settings; a change applies from the next processing call after the reconfigure, with the paths reset as spec 006 states.
- **Low host rates.** The overshoot figures are reported per rate, not predicted (a high-pass's square-wave overshoot does not shrink at low rates, and sampled ringing can read higher or lower); the bound's checks hold by FR-005 at every rate.
- **Recording.** Nothing plays; the option has no effect on the record path, the memory or the stored form.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: **The output clamp.** The host MUST be able to turn on an **output clamp** that replaces every route-output sample above +1.0 by +1.0 and every sample below −1.0 by −1.0, on both routes; a sample inside the bound MUST pass unchanged. The bound is inclusive and is each route's full scale (A-042).
- **FR-002**: **Default off.** The option MUST be off by default; with it off, every tap's output MUST be bit-identical to version 0.9's at every route, volume position and host rate (the five existing golden files and their guards unmoved).
- **FR-003**: **Where it applies.** The bound MUST apply at the route-output tap only, after the selected route's last stage (after the speaker template on the speaker route, after the earphone output on the earphone route) and after any output-chain bypass; it MUST NOT apply to the taps after stages 11, 12 and 13, to the after-playback tap or to any earlier tap, so the unbounded signal stays readable. The play calls always return the route output, so with the option on they MUST always be bounded whatever tap the settings name, and the documentation MUST say so. With the option on, the route-output tap and the selected route's last per-stage tap (or the after-playback tap when the chain is bypassed) are no longer the same signal; the documentation of the taps MUST say so.
- **FR-004**: **Not a stage.** The option MUST NOT add a row to the stage manifest, change its counts or change any entry string; every string describing it MUST call it an emulator convenience or a host option, never a device behavior, and MUST pass the claims policy and spec 007's denylist.
- **FR-005**: **No time behavior (the sample-wise identity).** For every stimulus, configuration, route, position, block partition and host rate, the route output with the option on MUST equal, sample for sample, the route output with the option off with each sample above +1.0 replaced by +1.0 and each below −1.0 replaced by −1.0; the bound MUST have no state, no gain control and no knee, and MUST add no latency and no reported latency change.
- **FR-006**: **Whole-engine behavior.** With the option on, record-then-play MUST equal the one-pass processing bit for bit under spec 008's equivalence rule on both routes and at two volume positions at the seven host rates, with the check shown to engage the bound at position 1; route and volume changes MUST take effect at the next block as in spec 009, the output through the change equal to the bounded option-off output; the stored form and the frame bytes MUST be unchanged, shown by identical export bytes with the option on or off and by an option-off export playing back bounded through an option-on engine.
- **FR-007**: **Determinism and real-time safety.** The bound MUST be bit-identical on every supported platform and block partition and allocation-free per block, and MUST add at most 2 ns per host sample (about 3 %) to the **codec-bypassed** speaker route's one-pass cost at 48 kHz, measured as US2 AS5 states.
- **FR-008**: **The surface.** The option MUST be a settings field in the output settings group, in the Rust public API and in the C API. The C settings structure MUST grow from 104 to 112 bytes (it is 8-byte aligned), so that version 0.9's size is refused with the existing size status; the 0.9 tail padding and this version's own tail MUST be explicit reserved fields (written 0) with assertions that the tail ends the structure, so that the next version can refuse this one's size; the header MUST be regenerated and the drift check clean.
- **FR-009**: **Records.** A-042 MUST be amended to name the option as the host's alternative to leaving headroom; to state every overshoot figure with its configuration (the chain-only speaker route about 1.9, the 0.9 default's speaker route up to about 2.05 on a full-scale tone at position 1, the speaker route never above the speaker template's impulse-response L1 norm of 1.75–2.29 by rate, the earphone route 1.37–1.52 chain-only and 1.04–1.07 in the default on a full-scale square at position 1), as the model's figures that move if A-010 or A-032 is revised; to state the earphone route's overshoot as the reconstruction overshoot and the coupling corners' overshoot on square-edged material; to state that on material above the bound the option replaces the excess with flat tops, adding distortion of its own that the device does not have, while typical material at the default position is untouched; with its hardware statement unchanged and its status line marked "amended by spec 010". The README's output-scale paragraph MUST lead with headroom scaling (0.42, −7.5 dB) as the fidelity-preserving choice and then state the option, its default, what it bounds (the play calls always), what it does not, that +1.0 maps to 32767 in a 16-bit conversion (a host scaling by 32768 saturates +1.0 to 32767), the added distortion, and that later resampling, filtering or mixing by the host can exceed full scale again; the README's record-path headroom paragraph MUST be rewritten with the corrected figures; the 009 overshoot test's doc comment ("only when the chain's input does") MUST be corrected. - **FR-010**: **Its own golden file.** The option on MUST have its own golden file at the seven host rates with the primary stimulus and the secondary stimuli on the overshooting configurations and one configuration that does not overshoot (the 0.9 default on the speaker route at the reference position), each entry recording how many samples sit exactly at the bound, blessed once, reproducing on every golden-matrix platform.

### Out of Scope

A host-set ceiling other than full scale, a soft knee or a look-ahead limiter (a second limiter without a device basis); any change to the overshoot itself (the speaker template, A-010, and the earphone corners, A-032, are untouched); the fixed-length volume ramp and the earphone follower's class-A cut-off (queued separately by spec 009); any bound on the record path beyond the codec's clamp of spec 005; dithering or the integer conversion itself (the host's); any report of bounded samples (a count or a flag in the block or play report), a later feature if an app needs a clip indicator; making the option (or other settings) a live control, deferred to the controls discussion the user queued.

### Key Entities

- **Output clamp (the option)**: a boolean host setting in the output settings group, off by default; when on, the route-output sample is held within [−1.0, +1.0] by replacement.
- **Route output**: the selected route's output after its last stage (spec 009), the only point the bound applies to.
- **Per-stage taps**: the points after stages 11, 12 and 13 and every earlier tap; never bounded.
- **Primary stimulus**: a 1 kHz sine of amplitude 1.5 with every stage bypassed; exceeds the bound at every host rate by construction.
- **Secondary stimuli**: in the output-chain-only configuration at position 1, a full-scale square wave on each route and a full-scale 1 kHz tone on the speaker route. A square wave has a half-period of round(rate ÷ 2000) host samples and starts at +1.0.
- **Overshoot figures**: the per-rate option-off sample peaks of the secondary stimuli (about 1.9 on the speaker route at 48 kHz), reported and documented, not bounded by this spec.
