# Feature Specification: Playback Speed (the device's five playback speeds, in the playback path)

**Feature Branch**: `013-playback-speed`

**Status**: Draft

## Overview

The RR-DR60 plays its recordings at one of **five speeds**. The owner's manual ([S-001](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)) describes a SPEED CONTROL switch, off when the unit is new, and five playback speeds: level 3 is normal, level 1 the slowest, level 5 the fastest, and the level can be changed only while a recording is playing. The service manual ([S-002](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)) shows the switch wired to the microcontroller, so a speed change is a command to the DSP's firmware, not a change of any analog part. Panasonic's 1999 US product page ([S-012](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)) gives the only figure: "Slow/Fast Play (+/-40%)". The 2026-10-17 research ([report](../../docs/hardware/research/reports/Noise%20reduction%20clock%20and%20power.md)) found that the codec cannot be slowed by lowering its clock within its stated operating range (its sync has a 6.0 kHz minimum and the device already runs at 6 kHz, [S-003](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)), so the slow speeds are a DSP process. Whether that process lowers the pitch (resampling) or keeps it (time-scaling the decoder's output) is unknown until someone captures a real unit's playback at each level (capture P1 in the hardware-evidence template); the research leans weakly toward a pitch-keeping device.

This feature gives the emulator the speed control as a **live playback control**, like the volume and the route. The host selects a level at any time; from the next device-rate sample the playback computes, the recording plays at that level's speed; a change during playback carries on from the current place in the recording. The modeled mechanism is the owner's labelled default (the research's mechanism (b)): the decoded stream is **resampled at the 6 kHz device rate** between the speech decoder (stage 9) and the codec's reconstruction filter (stage 10), so **pitch moves with speed**, and at the fast levels the content that would no longer fit below the device's 3 kHz Nyquist frequency is removed first. The emulator models a clean resampler (at least 60 dB of rejection); how clean the device's own process is, is unknown. The speed ratios follow the speed reading of "+/-40%": **0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2 and 1.4** (3/5 … 7/5) at levels 1 to 5, an assumption (A-054, low confidence) with the other reading (a duration change of ±40 %) registered beside it.

Level 3 is the identity: at level 3 the playback is bit-identical to version 0.12, every play report included, and every existing golden file is unchanged. The speed control applies only to the playback of stored recordings: the record path and the one-pass processing are untouched, as on the device, where the speed is selectable only during playback. Everything is modeled on the documents and the research's inference, not measured on a unit.

The main session's prototype (Kaiser, 70 dB, a 30 kHz prototype rate) meets it with 241 – 281 taps (49 – 57 per phase): worst spurious −66.9 dB (241 taps) and −71.7 dB (281 taps) at level 5, passband deviation at most 0.003 dB; the plan pins the design.

- Q (C4, D3, the coach's question 4): the rounding rule and the end-of-recording tail? → A: The rule is fixed in the spec: the place starts at 0 and advances by exactly r stored samples per device output sample (exact in fifths), and the recording's own part is the ⌈K ÷ r⌉ device samples (at a constant level) whose place is before K. **The speed control's tail is played**: after the place reaches K it keeps computing from silence past the recording's end until its response is spent (at most about 50 device samples at level 1), and those samples take the place of the drain's first silent device samples, so the drain's length is unchanged and the recording ends without a truncation click; the level is latched when the place reaches K. (The coach's alternative, stopping at K, cuts the last samples' response.) The pre-ring before place 0 is not played (recordings begin from the record path's quiet start).
- Q (C5, D6, the coach's question 3): the continuity oracle? → A: **Bit-exact.** The device-rate output at a place depends only on the place, the level and the stored samples; after a change to b, the output is bit-identical to a constant-level-b playback from the same place (the plan's independent harness reference, see the plan review below), the prefix bit-identical to the constant-a playback, and the sequence of places exact in fifths. The only discontinuity is the change of speed **and of its band** (each level has its own filter); the plan reports the size of that step.
- Q (D5): snapping forward into level 3 is a phase step (up to 0.8 sample, 96° at 2 kHz). → A: **The switch into level 3 waits for a whole place**: since every non-3 ratio is k/5 with k coprime to 5, the place becomes whole within at most 4 more output samples at the old ratio (at most 667 µs); from there the stored samples pass unaltered. No jump, exact identity afterwards.
- Q (D4, C7): reading ahead at level 3 would change the play reports. → A: **At level 3 nothing is read ahead** (a history of recent stored samples is kept); leaving level 3 fills the look-ahead at once (at most one extra frame decoded in that block, counted in the worst-block check). Every play report at level 3 is identical to 0.12's.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Slow and fast playback at a chosen level (Priority: P1)

An app developer adds the recorder's speed dial to the app. A user records a few seconds of speech, sets the dial to SLOW and plays it back: the recording plays at 0.6 times normal speed, about 67 % longer, and lower in pitch, as the modeled device plays it. At FAST it plays at 1.4 times, shorter and higher, and the content that would fold back above the device's band is gone rather than turning into new tones. At level 3 it sounds exactly as it does today. An audiophile comparing the levels hears the modeled device's speed change, not an artifact of the engine.

**Why this priority**: this is the feature: five playback speeds with the modeled device's sound. It is usable on its own, with the level set before the playback starts.

**Independent Test**: can be fully tested with a fresh engine at each supported host rate, importing synthetic stimuli (tones, an impulse, silence) or recording them with stages 1 – 5 bypassed, and playing them at a constant level. For the speed control's own response the codec, stage 10 and the output chain are bypassed, so the stored device samples are the stimulus itself, and FR-004 is asserted at the 6 kHz host rate, where the playback output is the device stream; at the other rates the moved tone's level is compared with level 3's playback of a stored tone at r × f and the spurious figures are reported (constitution VII). The oracles are the stimulus's known frequencies, length and samples and the same recording played at level 3. The live change, the C API and the records are not needed.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a recording of K stored device samples and a constant level with ratio r (0.6, 0.8, 1.2 or 1.4), **When** it is played to the end at each of the seven host rates, **Then** the recording's own part is exactly ⌈K ÷ r⌉ device samples, the speed control's tail of T device samples follows (at most 46), and the playback produces ⌈(⌈K ÷ r⌉ + T) · m ÷ l⌉ host samples (m ÷ l host samples per device sample) before the drain, whose length is unchanged, `finished` is reported exactly once at the end of the drain, and the length helper (FR-009) returns the total.
2. **Given** a stored steady tone at f from 100 Hz to min(2250, 2250 ÷ r) Hz (the codec, stage 10 and the output chain bypassed, 6 kHz), **When** it is played at each level, **Then** a least-squares sine fit at exactly r × f has an amplitude within ±0.1 dB of the stored tone's and a residual at least 60 dB below it.
3. **Given** a stored steady tone at f from 100 Hz to 2900 Hz (same configuration), **When** it is played at each level other than 3, **Then** every other component in the output between 20 Hz and 3000 Hz (images at the slow levels, aliases at the fast levels) is at least 60 dB below the stored tone's level, and at levels 4 and 5 a stored tone whose moved frequency r × f exceeds 3000 Hz leaves nothing in the output above −60 dB relative to its level.
4. **Given** any recording and any settings, **When** it is played at level 3, **Then** the output and every play report (produced, finished, frames, frame index, place) are bit-identical to version 0.12's on both routes and at every host rate; the record-then-play ≡ one-pass checks of specs 006 and 008 pass unchanged; every existing golden file is byte-identical and its SHA guard unchanged.
5. **Given** a stored tone at f and the full default playback path (the codec, stage 10 and the output chain on), **When** it is played at each level, **Then** the tone's frequency is r × f (the fit of scenario 2), and its level at r minus its level at level 3 equals H(r × f) − H(f) within ±0.2 dB, where H is the level-3 response of stage 10 and the output chain measured with the codec bypassed (reported per level and rate; the codec's own level error is common to both).
6. **Given** a stored impulse at sample j, **When** it is played at each level (the configuration of scenario 2), **Then** the response peaks at the device output nearest place j (at a half-sample tie the two outputs are equal within 1e-12) and is symmetric about the place; a recording that ends on a non-silent sample ends without a truncation of the speed control's response (its tail is played before the drain, FR-005).

### User Story 2 - Changing the speed while a recording plays (Priority: P2)

The user turns the dial while a recording is playing, from normal to SLOW, then to FAST, then back. The playback carries on from the same place in the recording at the new speed: nothing restarts, skips or repeats, and the only discontinuity is the change of speed and of its band. The app can turn the dial as often as it likes; the playback still ends exactly once, with every stored sample played, and the app's progress display (the place reported by each call) keeps tracking the place in the recording.

**Why this priority**: on the device the speed is changed during playback (S-001), so a live change is how users will use it; it also carries the real-time and API guarantees the structured engine needs. Separable from P1: P1 proves each level, P2 proves the transitions between them and the guarantees of specs 006 – 012.

**Independent Test**: can be tested with US1's configurations and the existing equivalence, determinism, allocation-free and timing checks, by changing the level at chosen block boundaries and comparing with constant-level playbacks of the same recording started at the same place (a test hook), which must match bit for bit.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a recording playing at level a, **When** the level changes to b (every ordered pair of distinct levels, with b ≠ 3), **Then** from the first device-rate sample computed after the change was set, the output is bit-identical to a constant-level-b playback started at the place reached; the output before it is bit-identical to the constant-level-a playback; the places advance by exactly r_a, then r_b, in fifths of a stored sample.
2. **Given** a change into level 3, **When** the playback continues, **Then** the old ratio holds until the place is a whole stored sample (at most 4 more device output samples), and from there the stored samples pass unaltered, bit-identical to a level-3 playback from that place; the place never moves backward or jumps.
3. **Given** a playback whose level changes any number of times, **When** it plays to the end, **Then** `finished` is reported exactly once, at the end of the drain; the recording's own part is the sum of the constant-level stretches by FR-005's rule; the speed control's tail is played before the drain; the reported place and frame index never decrease and reach the recording's end; the drain length is unchanged.
4. **Given** a level set while idle, while recording, or after the place has reached the end of the recording, **When** the next playback starts, **Then** the level applies from its first sample; a change after the place reached the end changes nothing in the current playback; the read-back level is always the host's last valid setting.
5. **Given** an invalid level (anything other than 1 – 5), **When** it is set, **Then** it is refused with a distinct error and the level is unchanged.
6. **Given** a reset, a reconfigure, a stop, a new playback, a route change, a volume change or an erase during playback, **When** the engine continues, **Then** the level survives reset and reconfigure (like the route and the volume), each playback starts the speed control from rest at the recording's start, and the speed control, the volume transition, the route and the output clamp act independently.
7. **Given** the one-pass processing and the record path, **When** any level is set, **Then** their output, the stored recordings and the export form are bit-identical to version 0.12's.
8. **Given** levels changing between blocks, **When** the per-block processing runs, **Then** it allocates nothing, its work per output sample is bounded, and its output is deterministic across platforms and, for a constant level, across block partitions; the cost per host sample at 48 kHz (codec bypassed, speaker route, a release run with the timing tests one at a time, each side the fastest of 45 strictly alternating runs) rises by at most **3 %** at level 3 against version 0.12 (a build A/B; a 5 % ceiling on the CI runner) and by at most **15 %** at levels 1, 2, 4 and 5 against level 3 in the same build; with the codec on, the cost per host sample at level 5 is at most 1.5 times level 3's, and the worst 64- and 4096-sample block at 6 and 48 kHz with the level changed before every block (the block that leaves level 3 included) stays below the block's real-time duration; nanoseconds stated beside each ratio, the CI runner's figures quoted beside the development machine's.

### Edge Cases

- **The level set before the first playback.** The playback starts at that level from its first sample; there is no glide between levels (the device's dial selects one of five discrete levels, S-001).
- **A change set during a call.** Controls are read when a play call starts; a change takes effect from the first device-rate sample computed after it was set. At 48 kHz a small call may compute no device sample; the change then takes effect in a later call.
- **Several levels set in one gap.** Only the last counts. **The same level set again**: not a change.
- **Into level 3.** The old ratio continues for at most 4 device output samples until the place is whole; then exact level-3 output. A change to another level during that wait applies at once from the current place, and the switch into level 3 is dropped. **Out of level 3**: the look-ahead fills at once (at most one extra frame decoded in that call).
- **Very short recordings.** A recording shorter than the speed control's reach still plays every stored sample at every level, its tail into the drain.
- **The end of the recording.** When the place reaches K the level is latched (before any pending change); the speed control's tail is computed from silence and played before the drain; a change after that has no effect on this playback.
- **Rapid changes.** Changing the level every block is allowed; each change takes effect as stated, the place stays continuous, and the work per block stays bounded.
- **Erasing the recording being played.** The playback stops, as in spec 006; the level is kept.
- **Codec bypassed.** The speed control applies to the stored device samples in the same way; the frame index stays 0 as in 0.12, and the place reports progress.
- **The one-pass taps.** The player honours no tap (spec 006); the speed control is never visible at the one-pass taps, which it does not affect.
- **The output clamp.** It bounds the route output as before.
- **DC and silence.** Each phase of the speed control has unit gain at DC, so DC and silence pass unchanged at every level.
- **Host rates.** The speed control runs at the 6 kHz device rate, so its behavior is the same at every host rate; the host-rate conversion after stage 10 is unchanged.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: **The control.** The engine MUST offer a **playback speed level**, an integer from 1 to 5, as a live control beside the volume and the route: settable and readable at any time (idle, recording, playing), the read-back always the host's last valid setting, **default 3**, kept by reset and reconfigure; any other value MUST be refused with a distinct error and the level left unchanged. No setting, and no change to the Rust settings or the C settings structure (it stays 120 bytes).
- **FR-002**: **The ratios.** Levels 1 to 5 MUST play at speed ratios **r = 3/5, 4/5, 5/5, 6/5 and 7/5** (0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2, 1.4; A-054: the speed reading of S-012's "+/-40%", linear spacing assumed; low confidence), published as read-only exact fifths (Rust and C). The duration reading (r = 5/7, 5/6, 1, 5/4, 5/3) is registered in A-054 as the alternative and is not offered.
- **FR-003**: **The mechanism.** At a level with ratio r, the playback MUST reproduce the stored device-rate stream as if read r times faster: a stored component at frequency f MUST appear at r × f, so **pitch moves with speed** (A-054's mechanism (b), the owner's labelled default). The speed control MUST sit at the 6 kHz device rate between the speech decoder (stage 9; or the stored samples when the codec is bypassed) and the codec's reconstruction filter (stage 10), so stage 10, the host-rate conversion and the output chain are unchanged. Its response MUST be centred on the place (linear phase at every level) and each of its phases MUST have unit gain at DC (within 1e-15).
- **FR-004**: **Band and spurious products.** With the codec, stage 10 and the output chain bypassed, at the 6 kHz host rate: (a) a stored tone at f from 100 Hz to min(2250, 2250 ÷ r) Hz MUST play at r × f with a least-squares fit amplitude within **±0.1 dB** of its stored level and a residual at least 60 dB below it; (b) for stored tones from 100 Hz to 2900 Hz, every other component in the output between **20 Hz and 3000 Hz** MUST be at least **60 dB** below the stored tone's level (spec 004 FR-003's converter stopband target); (c) at r > 1, a stored tone whose moved frequency r × f exceeds 3000 Hz MUST leave nothing above **−60 dB** relative to its level. At the other host rates the moved tone's level MUST agree with level 3's playback of a stored tone at r × f within ±0.1 dB, and the spurious figures are reported. The plan states the filter that meets these and its computed figures per level, designed with margin (70 dB).
- **FR-005**: **Duration, completeness and the tail.** The **place** MUST start at 0 at a playback's start and advance by exactly r stored samples per device-rate output sample, with no accumulated rounding. The recording's own part MUST end at the first device output sample whose place is at least K (K stored device samples): ⌈K ÷ r⌉ device samples at a constant level (K′ device samples in general; m ÷ l host samples per device sample). After it the speed control MUST play its own tail, computed from silence past the recording's end, T device samples (at most 46; 0 at level 3), and then the drain of `drain_samples()` host samples, unchanged: the playback produces ⌈(K′ + T) · m ÷ l⌉ host samples before the drain, and when the total is reached nothing more is pushed or emitted; the level MUST be latched as soon as the place reaches K, before any pending change is applied. Every stored sample MUST contribute to the output; `finished` MUST be reported exactly once, at the end of the drain. With level changes during playback, the recording's own part MUST be the concatenation of the constant-level stretches by the same rule.
- **FR-006**: **Level 3 is the identity.** At level 3 the stored samples MUST be passed unaltered and nothing read ahead: the playback output and every play report (produced, finished, frames, frame index, and the new place) MUST be bit-identical to version 0.12's at every route, host rate and settings combination; the existing golden files MUST be byte-identical and their SHA guards unchanged; the record-then-play ≡ one-pass checks of specs 006 and 008 and every existing check that does not set a level other than 3 MUST pass unchanged.
- **FR-007**: **Live change.** A level change MUST take effect from the first device-rate sample the speed control computes after the change was set (a play call that computes none does not count), continuing from the place reached, without restarting the playback, skipping or repeating stored samples (beyond what the change of ratio implies) or jumping in place. The device-rate output at a place MUST depend only on the place, the level and the stored samples, so the output after a change to b MUST be bit-identical to a constant-level-b playback from the same place, and the output before it bit-identical to the constant-level-a playback. A change into level 3 MUST keep the old ratio until the place is a whole stored sample (at most 4 device output samples) and pass the stored samples unaltered from there; a change to another level arriving during that wait MUST apply from the next computed device sample at the current place, the pending switch dropped. A change after the place reached the recording's end MUST have no effect on that playback. A playback start MUST begin the speed control from rest at the recording's start. The plan reports the size of the step a change of level makes (the change of speed and of its band).
- **FR-008**: **Scope.** The speed control MUST apply to the playback of stored recordings only: the record path, the stored recordings, the export form and the one-pass processing MUST be bit-identical to version 0.12 at every level. An impulse stored at sample j MUST peak at the device output nearest place j (at a half-sample tie the two outputs equal within 1e-12) with a response symmetric about the place, so the playback path's reported latency MUST be the same at every level; the drain length MUST be unchanged.
- **FR-009**: **Reports and the length helper.** The play report MUST gain the **place**: ⌊place⌋ in whole stored samples, capped at K, as reached by the last device-rate sample computed in the call (it leads the audible output by the playback latency, as the frame index does), in the Rust report and at the end of the C report structure; the frame index MUST be ⌊place ÷ 120⌋ with the codec on and 0 with it bypassed (0.12's values at level 3), held at the last frame during the drain; neither may decrease. A **length helper** MUST return a recording's playback length in host samples at a given constant level (the recording's own part and the tail by FR-005, plus the drain), in Rust and C.
- **FR-010**: **Determinism, real-time safety and cost.** Everything the speed control needs MUST be prepared at construction and reconfiguration; the per-block processing MUST allocate nothing and do bounded work per output sample at every level, including the codec's decoding at level 5 (1.4 times the frames per output second) and the look-ahead filled when leaving level 3; the output MUST be deterministic on every supported platform (no platform math function) and, at a constant level, independent of the block partition. Cost (48 kHz, speaker route, release, the timing tests one at a time, each side the fastest of 45 strictly alternating runs, spec 012's protocol and the 2026-10-17 chore's runner-ceiling pattern): codec bypassed, at level 3 at most **+3 %** against version 0.12 (a build A/B on the development machine; a **5 %** ceiling on the CI runner, run once before the merge), at levels 1, 2, 4 and 5 at most **+15 %** against level 3 in the same build; codec on, the cost per host sample at level 5 at most **1.5 times** level 3's, and the worst 64- and 4096-sample block at 6 and 48 kHz with the level changed before every block MUST stay below the block's real-time duration. Nanoseconds reported beside each ratio, and the 6 kHz cost reported.
- **FR-011**: **Traceability.** The speed control MUST trace to a new assumption **A-054** (the five levels and their ratios under both readings, the linear spacing, mechanism (b) as the labelled default and why, the clean resampler as the emulator's choice, mechanisms (a) and (c) and what S-003 excludes, the weak lean toward (c), the confidence, and capture P1 with its corrected decision rule as the verification) and to the sources S-001, S-002, S-003, S-012 and a new **S-018** (speed-change patents of the period, OKI EP0714089B1 and Lucent US5717823A, as secondary sources for the class, never for the device). The stage manifest's entry for **stage 9** MUST cite A-054, S-001, S-003, S-012 and S-018, name the speeds in its description, list the setter in its controls note (a list of API items within 120 characters; the getter and the identity are stated in the rustdoc and the README), name pitch-preserving playback in its "not modeled here" note, and move its model version to 0.13.0; stage 10's note MUST no longer list the speed control; the canonical text and the README's manifest table MUST be re-blessed once in the feature's first phase (a second only if a later review changes a string), each recorded in the CHANGELOG (spec 007 FR-015). The signal-chain table's row 9 MUST say "Modeled by" for the speed control.
- **FR-012**: **C API.** The C API MUST gain functions to set and read the level, the published ratios and the length helper, a status code for a refused level, the place at the end of the play report, and parity with the Rust API (bit-identical on an exactly representable stimulus). The settings structure MUST stay 120 bytes.
- **FR-013**: **Its own golden file.** The speed control MUST have its own golden file at the seven host rates: constant-level playbacks at levels 1, 2, 4 and 5 of a steady tone and of the voiced stimulus, with the codec on (both routes' outputs) and bypassed (the speaker route), plus a set with stage 10 and the output chain bypassed at 6 kHz that isolates the speed control; and playbacks with a level-change schedule (into and out of level 3, between two non-3 levels, and a change after the place reached the end). Each entry records its output digest, the recording's own length, the final place and the frames decoded; the bless asserts FR-005's counts and the length helper (level 3 is guarded by the eight existing golden files, so no entry plays at level 3 throughout). Blessed once, reproducing on every golden-matrix platform.
- **FR-014**: **Records.** The README (a playback-speed section), the API documentation (Rust and C), the CHANGELOG, the register (A-054, S-018), the signal-chain table and the stage manifest MUST state the levels, the ratios, the pitch behavior and the weak lean toward a pitch-keeping device, the playback-only scope, the live-change rule, the duration rule and the helper, and that level 3 is unchanged, as modeled, not measured. The version MUST be 0.13.0.

### Out of Scope

The SPEED CONTROL switch as a separate control (an app maps "off" to level 3; whether the device keeps the level while the switch is off is open, A-054); mechanism (a), a codec clock change, even for the fast levels only; pitch-preserving playback, mechanism (c) (a follow-up feature if a capture shows it or an app asks for it); the duration reading of "+/-40%" as a selectable alternative; an era-cheap resampler (linear interpolation) as an option; speed during recording or in the one-pass processing; the device's restriction that the level changes only during playback (the engine accepts it any time); a glide between levels; a tap after the speed control; the 10-second review and the beeps.

### Key Entities

- **Speed level**: the host's setting, 1 to 5, default 3; what reading the level returns.
- **Speed ratio**: r = k ÷ 5 for a level, k = 3 … 7 (A-054); device output samples per stored sample are 1 ÷ r.
- **Place in the recording**: the stored-sample position the speed control has reached, in fifths of a stored sample, advancing by k fifths per device output sample; whole at level 3; reported as whole stored samples.
- **Speed control (the stage)**: the device-rate resampling between stage 9 and stage 10 in the playback path, centred on the place; the identity at level 3.
- **Constant-level stretch**: the part of a playback between two level changes (or the start and the end).
- **The tail**: the speed control's response past the recording's end, T device samples (at most 46; none at level 3), played after the recording's own part and before the drain.

## Assumptions

- **A-054** (new): the device's SPEED CONTROL plays a recording at one of five levels, factory set to 3 = normal (S-001), selected by the DSP firmware on a command from the microcontroller (S-002). Ratios: the speed reading of "+/-40%" (S-012), 0.6 / 0.8 / 1.0 / 1.2 / 1.4 with linear spacing assumed; the duration reading (speeds 1/1.4 … 1/0.6, that is 0.714 … 1.667) is the registered alternative. Mechanism: unknown; a codec clock change cannot produce the slow levels within the codec's stated 6.0 – 10.0 kHz sync range (S-003; strong but not proof, since a recommended operating range is not a physical limit); the candidates are (b) resampling at the fixed 6 kHz clock (pitch moves) and (c) time-scaling in or after the decoder (pitch kept; the weak circumstantial lean, from the codec maker's 1994 patent and Panasonic's later marketing, S-018), with (a) a codec clock change still possible for the fast levels (under the duration reading the fastest level would put the codec at exactly its 10.0 kHz maximum). The emulator models **(b)** as the owner's labelled default (2026-10-17): it is well defined, testable and cheap; (c) is a follow-up. The device's resampler quality is unknown (a 1998 DSP might interpolate linearly, leaving images and aliases 25 – 40 dB down); the emulator models a clean one (at least 60 dB). Confidence low (ratios, mechanism, quality), high that the control exists and is firmware. Verification: capture P1: record 1 kHz and 2.3 kHz tones and a sustained vowel, play each at levels 1 – 5 into the earphone jack; the durations give the ratios; the tone plus its DAC image (f + f_image = 6 kHz at a fixed clock, r × 6 kHz under a clock change) tells a clock change from a fixed clock at every level, and the vowel's pitch tells (b) from (c). The 2.3 kHz tone at level 5: absent under (b) with anti-aliasing, a residue at 2.78 kHz under (b) with a weak resampler, 3.22 kHz under (a); at level 4 it plays at 2.76 kHz about 54 dB down under (b) as modeled (the speed control's band edge and stage 10; a weaker device resampler would leave more) against about 0 dB under (a), so attenuation, not presence, decides there.
- The speed control sits in the DSP's playback firmware, which the manifest's stage 9 (the speech decoder) represents; the codec's reconstruction filter (stage 10) runs at the fixed 6 kHz clock under (b), so its response does not move with the level. The codec's postfilter is upstream, so its tilt moves with r, as it would under (b).
- Stored content lies below about 2550 Hz in the default engine (the record band, A-027). At the slow levels the band's top moves to 1530 Hz (level 1) and 2040 Hz (level 2). At the fast levels stage 10 removes played content above about 2550 Hz, so the stored band that survives tops out near 2125 Hz at level 4 and 1821 Hz at level 5; the speed control itself removes stored content above 3000 ÷ r (2500 and 2143 Hz) before it can alias.
- Because the playback pulls stored samples as it needs them, the speed control can read ahead of the place it has reached (outside level 3); a response centred on the place is what lets a change of level leave the place where it is and keeps the playback latency the same at every level.
- The level changes at a device-rate sample computed after the change was set, as every control takes effect at the next block since spec 006; the device's dial selects five discrete levels (S-001), so a step between levels with no glide is the modeled behavior.
- The engine accepts a level change at any time, though the device accepts one only during playback (S-001); an app that wants the device's restriction applies it in its UI. Whether the device keeps the level when the switch is turned off is open (capture P1 notes it).
- The cost budget is stated as ratios against version 0.12 (about 66 ns per host sample on the speaker route, codec bypassed, on the development machine; spec 012's figures), with the protocol and the runner-ceiling pattern of the 2026-10-17 timing chore; the prototype's filter costs about 49 – 57 multiply-adds per device output sample, about 7 per host sample at 48 kHz.
- The version is 0.13.0; no release has been published. The feature branch builds on the records chore (`research/noise-clock-power`, PR #31), which registers S-012 – S-017 and the corrected rows the spec cites; the branch is rebased onto `main` once PR #31 merges.
