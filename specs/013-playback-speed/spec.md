# Feature Specification: Playback Speed (the device's five playback speeds, in the playback path)

**Feature Branch**: `013-playback-speed`

**Created**: 2026-10-17

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description (abridged; the full text is in the specify session's transcript): "Feature 013: the playback speed control (SPEED CONTROL, five playback speed levels) of the Panasonic RR-DR60, modeled in the emulator's playback path. The device has a SPEED CONTROL switch (S6) and five playback speed levels, factory set to 3 (normal), level 1 SLOW, level 5 FAST, selectable only during playback (S-001). S6 is a microcontroller input, so any speed change is a command to the DSP firmware (S-002). The only figure anywhere is Panasonic's 1999 US product page: 'Slow/Fast Play (+/-40%)' (S-012, marketing copy with known errors). The codec's 6.0 kHz minimum sync (S-003) rules out slowing by a codec clock change. The owner chose mechanism (b) as the labelled assumed default: a fixed-rate resampling of the decoded stream at the 6 kHz device rate between the speech decoder (stage 9) and the codec's reconstruction filter (stage 10), so pitch moves with speed, and at speeds above 1 content above 3000/r Hz is removed before it can alias. The pitch-preserving mechanism (c1) is a later follow-up. App creators want slow and fast playback live during playback, like the volume knob. Level 3 is exactly today's output; the control is live, survives reset and reconfigure, and a change continues the playback from where it is; playback of stored recordings only; ratios 0.6/0.8/1.0/1.2/1.4 (A-054, low) with the duration reading registered as the alternative; the whole recording is played in about length ÷ ratio; real-time safe; measurable checks of duration, pitch, aliasing, level-3 identity, continuity, determinism, work bounds and C parity; the manifest, the registers (A-054, S-018), the README and the C API gain the control. Out of scope: the switch as a separate control, mechanisms (a) and (c), pitch-preserving playback, speed during recording or the one-pass, the 10-second review and the beeps."

## Overview

The RR-DR60 plays its recordings at one of **five speeds**. The owner's manual ([S-001](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)) describes a SPEED CONTROL switch, off when the unit is new, and five playback speeds: level 3 is normal, level 1 the slowest, level 5 the fastest, and the level can be changed only while a recording is playing. The service manual ([S-002](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)) shows the switch wired to the microcontroller, so a speed change is a command to the DSP's firmware, not a change of any analog part. Panasonic's 1999 US product page ([S-012](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)) gives the only figure: "Slow/Fast Play (+/-40%)". The 2026-10-17 research ([report](../../docs/hardware/research/reports/Noise%20reduction%20clock%20and%20power.md)) found that the codec cannot be slowed by lowering its clock (its sync has a 6.0 kHz minimum and the device already runs at 6 kHz, [S-003](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)), so the slow speeds are a DSP process. Whether that process lowers the pitch (resampling) or keeps it (time-scaling the decoder's output) is unknown until someone captures a real unit's playback at each level (capture P1 in the hardware-evidence template).

This feature gives the emulator the speed control as a **live playback control**, like the volume and the route. The host selects a level at any time; from the next playback block the recording plays at that level's speed; a change during playback carries on from the current place in the recording. The modeled mechanism is the owner's labelled default (the research's mechanism (b)): the decoded stream is **resampled at the 6 kHz device rate** between the speech decoder (stage 9) and the codec's reconstruction filter (stage 10), so **pitch moves with speed**, and at the fast levels the content that would no longer fit below the device's 3 kHz Nyquist frequency is removed first. The speed ratios follow the speed reading of "+/-40%": **0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2 and 1.4** at levels 1 to 5, an assumption (A-054, low confidence) with the other reading (a duration change of ±40 %) registered beside it.

Level 3 is the identity: at level 3 the playback is bit-identical to version 0.12 at every tap, and every existing golden file is unchanged. The speed control applies only to the playback of stored recordings: the record path and the one-pass processing are untouched, as on the device, where the speed is selectable only during playback. Everything is modeled on the documents and the research's inference, not measured on a unit.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Slow and fast playback at a chosen level (Priority: P1)

An app developer adds the recorder's speed dial to the app. A user records a few seconds of speech, sets the dial to SLOW and plays it back: the recording plays at 0.6 times normal speed, about 67 % longer, and lower in pitch, as the modeled device plays it. At FAST it plays at 1.4 times, shorter and higher, and the content that would fold back above the device's band is gone rather than turning into new tones. At level 3 it sounds exactly as it does today. An audiophile comparing the levels hears the device's speed change, not an artifact of the engine.

**Why this priority**: this is the feature: five playback speeds with the modeled device's sound. It is usable on its own, with the level set before the playback starts.

**Independent Test**: can be fully tested with a fresh engine at each supported host rate, recording synthetic stimuli (tones, a sweep, silence) or importing them, and playing them at a constant level. For the speed stage's own response, stage 10 and the output chain are bypassed and the speech codec is bypassed (the stored device samples are then the stimulus itself); the oracles are the same recording played at level 3 and the stimulus's known frequencies and length (constitution VII). The live change, the C API and the records are not needed.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a recording of K device samples and a constant level with ratio r (0.6, 0.8, 1.2 or 1.4), **When** it is played to the end at each of the seven host rates, **Then** the output before the drain lasts K ÷ r device samples within the stated rounding rule (FR-005), the playback reports `finished` exactly once at the end of the drain, and the drain length is the same as at level 3.
2. **Given** a stored steady tone at f between 100 Hz and 2250 Hz (codec, stage 10 and output chain bypassed), **When** it is played at each level, **Then** the output is a tone at r × f, its frequency within 0.01 % of r × f and its level within ±0.1 dB of the stored tone's whenever r × f is at most 2250 Hz.
3. **Given** a stored steady tone at f between 100 Hz and 2900 Hz, **When** it is played at each level other than 3, **Then** every other component the speed control produces (images at the slow levels, aliases at the fast levels) that falls between 20 Hz and 2550 Hz in the output is at least 60 dB below the stored tone's level; at levels 4 and 5 a stored tone whose moved frequency r × f lies at 3450 Hz or above (that is, beyond the device's band after the speed change) leaves nothing in the output above −60 dB relative to its level.
4. **Given** any recording and any configuration, **When** it is played at level 3, **Then** the output is bit-identical to version 0.12's at every tap, host rate and route; every existing golden file is byte-identical and its SHA guard unchanged.
5. **Given** a stored tone at f and the full default playback path (codec, stage 10 and the output chain on), **When** it is played at each level, **Then** the tone's frequency is r × f within 0.01 % and its level differs from level 3's by no more than stage 10's and the output chain's own responses at the two frequencies explain (reported per level and rate).
6. **Given** a recording that ends in silence, **When** it is played at any level, **Then** the last stored sample is represented in the output (every stored sample contributes, none is cut), and the output after the recording's own part is the drain's settled silence as at level 3.

### User Story 2 - Changing the speed while a recording plays (Priority: P2)

The user turns the dial while a recording is playing, from normal to SLOW, then to FAST, then back. The playback carries on from the same place in the recording at the new speed: nothing restarts, skips or repeats, and the only discontinuity is the change of speed itself. The app can turn the dial as often as it likes; the playback still ends exactly once, with every stored sample played, and the app's progress display (the frame being played) keeps tracking the place in the recording.

**Why this priority**: on the device the speed is changed during playback (S-001), so a live change is how users will use it; it also carries the real-time and API guarantees the structured engine needs. Separable from P1: P1 proves each level, P2 proves the transitions between them and the guarantees of specs 006 – 012.

**Independent Test**: can be tested with the configurations of US1 and the existing equivalence, determinism, allocation-free and timing checks, by changing the level at chosen block boundaries and comparing with constant-level playbacks of the same recording (the oracle: the output after a change, at the device rate, equals a constant-level playback of the same recording read from the same place).

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a recording playing at level a, **When** the level changes to b between two blocks (every pair of distinct levels), **Then** from the first device-rate sample computed in the next block the playback continues at b's ratio from the place in the recording it had reached, with no stored sample skipped or read twice beyond what the change of ratio implies, and (with stage 10, the codec and the output chain bypassed) the device-rate output after the change agrees with a constant-level playback at b read from the same place within the plan's stated tolerance; the change takes effect no later than the start of the next block's output plus the playback path's latency.
2. **Given** a level change into level 3 during playback, **When** the playback continues, **Then** from the first whole stored sample at or after the place reached, the stored samples are passed unaltered (level 3's bit-identity), the place moving forward by less than one device sample (at most 167 µs of the recording) and never backward.
3. **Given** a playback whose level changes any number of times, **When** it plays to the end, **Then** `finished` is reported exactly once, at the end of the drain; every stored sample has contributed; the reported frame index tracks the frame of the recording being played and never decreases; and the total output before the drain equals the sum over the constant-level stretches within the plan's rounding rule.
4. **Given** a level set while idle, while recording, or during the drain, **When** the next playback starts (or the drain completes), **Then** the level applies from the next playback's first block (a change during the drain changes nothing audible, the recording's own part having ended) and the read-back level is always the host's last valid setting.
5. **Given** an invalid level (anything other than 1 – 5), **When** it is set, **Then** it is refused with a distinct error and the level is unchanged.
6. **Given** a reset, a reconfigure, a stop, a new playback, a route change, a volume change or an erase during playback, **When** the engine continues, **Then** the level survives reset and reconfigure (like the route and the volume), each playback starts the speed stage from rest at the recording's beginning, and the speed control and the volume transition, the route and the output clamp act independently.
7. **Given** the one-pass processing and the record path, **When** any level is set, **Then** their output is bit-identical to version 0.12 (the speed control does not apply to them).
8. **Given** levels changing between blocks, **When** the per-block processing runs, **Then** it allocates nothing, its work per output sample is bounded, and its output is deterministic across platforms and, for a constant level, across block partitions; the cost per host sample at 48 kHz (codec bypassed, speaker route, a release run with the timing tests one at a time, each side the fastest of 45 strictly alternating runs) rises by at most **3 %** at level 3 against version 0.12 (a build A/B) and by at most **15 %** at levels 1, 2, 4 and 5 against level 3 in the same build; with the codec on, the worst 64-sample block at level 5 stays below the block's real-time duration; nanoseconds stated beside each ratio, the CI runner's figures quoted beside the development machine's.

### User Story 3 - The control in the API and the records (Priority: P3)

A developer finds the speed control next to the volume and the route in the Rust and C APIs and the README, with the five ratios, the pitch behavior and the fact that it is a modeled assumption. A reader of the register and the stage manifest finds where the speed control comes from and how a real unit could settle it.

**Why this priority**: the project's traceability rule (constitution II) covers the ratios and the mechanism; app developers need the API and an honest statement of what is assumed. Separable: the records and the C parity can be checked without new audio measurements.

**Independent Test**: the C API parity checks, the manifest cross-check, the traceability audit and the wording checks run against the updated code and records; a reader test on the README.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the C API, **When** a host sets, reads and refuses a level and plays at each level, **Then** the results are identical to the Rust API's, bit for bit on an exactly representable stimulus, and the refusal has its own status code.
2. **Given** the register, the stage manifest and the signal-chain table, **When** the speed control's records are read, **Then** it traces to a new assumption A-054 (the five levels, both readings of "+/-40%", the linear spacing, mechanism (b) as the labelled default, the open question and the capture that would settle it) and to S-001, S-002, S-003, S-012 and a new secondary source S-018 (speed-change patents of the period, for the class); the stage manifest's entry for stage 9 cites them, names the control, and names pitch-preserving playback as not modeled; stage 10's "not modeled here" note no longer lists the speed control; the CHANGELOG records the change.
3. **Given** the README, **When** the playback section is read, **Then** it states the five levels and their ratios, that pitch moves with speed, that this is the modeled default pending a capture, that the level is live and applies to playback only, how the playback length changes, and that level 3 is the unchanged output, in under five minutes of reading.
4. **Given** every string this feature adds, **When** the wording checks run, **Then** they pass spec 007's denylist and the claims policy (neutral; "modeled on", never "measured" or "accurate"; no claim that slowing reveals anything in a recording).

### Edge Cases

- **The level set before the first playback.** The playback starts at that level from its first sample; there is no ramp between levels (the device's dial has detents, S-001 describes discrete levels).
- **A level change in the first block of a playback.** The first block runs at the level set before it; a change takes effect from the next block, like every control since spec 006.
- **Several levels set in one gap.** Only the last counts.
- **The same level set again.** Not a change; nothing happens.
- **Very short recordings.** A recording shorter than the speed stage's own reach still plays every stored sample at every level, followed by the drain.
- **A change at the very end of the recording.** If the recording's own part has ended (the drain is running), the change has no audible effect on this playback; the level holds for the next.
- **Rapid changes.** Changing the level every block is allowed; each change takes effect at its block, the playback stays continuous in place, and the work per block stays bounded.
- **Erasing the recording being played.** The playback stops, as in spec 006; the level is kept.
- **Codec bypassed.** The speed control applies to the stored device samples in the same way (it sits after the decoder, or in its place when the decoder is bypassed).
- **Stage 10 or the output chain bypassed, or a tap before them.** The speed control is part of the playback of stored recordings; the one-pass taps never see it.
- **The output clamp.** It bounds the route output as before; the speed control changes nothing about it.
- **Level 3 after another level.** The stored samples are passed unaltered again from the next whole stored sample, so a long playback that returns to level 3 resumes exact level-3 output (after the playback path's own filters settle).
- **Host rates.** The speed control runs at the 6 kHz device rate, so its behavior is the same at every host rate; the host-rate conversion after stage 10 is unchanged.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: **The control.** The engine MUST offer a **playback speed level**, an integer from 1 to 5, as a live control beside the volume and the route: settable and readable at any time (idle, recording, playing), the read-back always the host's last valid setting, **default 3**, kept by reset and reconfigure; any other value MUST be refused with a distinct error and the level left unchanged. No setting, and no change to the Rust settings or the C settings structure (it stays 120 bytes).
- **FR-002**: **The ratios.** Levels 1 to 5 MUST play at speed ratios **r = 0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2 and 1.4** (A-054: the speed reading of S-012's "+/-40%", linear spacing assumed; low confidence). The ratios MUST be published as read-only constants (Rust and C). The duration reading (r = 1/1.4, 1/1.2, 1, 1/0.8, 1/0.6) is registered in A-054 as the alternative and is not offered.
- **FR-003**: **The mechanism.** At a level with ratio r, the playback MUST reproduce the stored device-rate stream as if read r times faster: a stored component at frequency f MUST appear at r × f, so **pitch moves with speed** (A-054's mechanism (b), the owner's labelled default). The speed control MUST sit at the 6 kHz device rate between the speech decoder (stage 9; or the stored samples when the codec is bypassed) and the codec's reconstruction filter (stage 10), so stage 10, the host-rate conversion and the output chain are unchanged.
- **FR-004**: **Band and spurious products.** With the codec, stage 10 and the output chain bypassed: (a) a stored tone at f from 100 Hz to 2250 Hz MUST play at r × f within 0.01 % and, when r × f is at most 2250 Hz, within ±0.1 dB of its stored level; (b) for stored tones from 100 Hz to 2900 Hz, every other component in the output between 20 Hz and 2550 Hz MUST be at least **60 dB** below the stored tone's level (spec 004 FR-003's converter stopband target); (c) at r > 1, a stored tone whose moved frequency r × f is 3450 Hz or more MUST leave nothing above −60 dB relative to its level in the output. The plan states the filter that meets these and its computed figures per level.
- **FR-005**: **Duration and completeness.** A recording of K stored device samples played at a constant level MUST produce, before the drain, the device-rate output samples whose place in the recording is before its end, K ÷ r of them rounded by the plan's stated rule (within one device sample), and then the unchanged drain; every stored sample MUST contribute to the output; `finished` MUST be reported exactly once, at the end of the drain. With level changes during playback, the output before the drain MUST be the concatenation of the constant-level stretches by the same rule.
- **FR-006**: **Level 3 is the identity.** At level 3 the stored samples MUST be passed unaltered: the playback MUST be bit-identical to version 0.12 at every tap, host rate and route, the existing golden files byte-identical and their SHA guards unchanged, and every existing check that does not set a level other than 3 MUST pass unchanged.
- **FR-007**: **Live change.** A level change MUST take effect from the first device-rate sample the speed control computes in the next playback block, continuing from the place in the recording reached, without restarting the playback, skipping or repeating stored samples (beyond what the change of ratio implies) or jumping in place, apart from level 3's move to the next whole stored sample (FR-006; less than one device sample, forward only). The device-rate output after a change MUST agree with a constant-level playback of the same recording read from the same place within the plan's stated tolerance. A change during the drain MUST have no effect on that playback. A playback start MUST begin the speed control from rest at the recording's start.
- **FR-008**: **Scope.** The speed control MUST apply to the playback of stored recordings only: the record path, the stored recordings, the export form and the one-pass processing MUST be bit-identical to version 0.12 at every level. The playback path's reported latency MUST be the same at every level (the speed control adds none: the place in the recording at the playback's first output sample is its start), and the drain length MUST be unchanged.
- **FR-009**: **Reports.** The playback report's frame index MUST remain the index of the stored frame being played (the place in the recording) at every level and never decrease; the frames-decoded count MUST count the frames decoded in the call. No new field is required; the plan MAY add the level in effect to a report if a check needs it.
- **FR-010**: **Determinism, real-time safety and cost.** Everything the speed control needs MUST be prepared at construction and reconfiguration; the per-block processing MUST allocate nothing and do bounded work per output sample at every level, including the codec's decoding at level 5 (1.4 times the frames per output second); the output MUST be deterministic on every supported platform (no platform math function) and, at a constant level, independent of the block partition. Cost (codec bypassed, 48 kHz, speaker route, release, the timing tests one at a time, each side the fastest of 45 strictly alternating runs, spec 012's protocol): at level 3 at most **+3 %** against version 0.12 (a build A/B on the development machine, and once on the CI runner before the merge); at levels 1, 2, 4 and 5 at most **+15 %** against level 3 in the same build; with the codec on, the worst 64-sample block at level 5 at 48 kHz MUST stay below the block's real-time duration (1333 µs). Nanoseconds reported beside each ratio; a runner ceiling where the development-machine budget is tighter than the runner's noise (the 2026-10-17 chore's lesson).
- **FR-011**: **Traceability.** The speed control MUST trace to a new assumption **A-054** (the five levels and their ratios under both readings, the linear spacing, mechanism (b) as the labelled default and why, mechanisms (a) and (c) and what S-003 excludes, the confidence, and capture P1 as the verification) and to the sources S-001, S-002, S-003, S-012 and a new **S-018** (speed-change patents of the period, OKI EP0714089B1 and Lucent US5717823A, as secondary sources for the class, never for the device). The stage manifest's entry for **stage 9** MUST cite A-054, S-012 and S-018, list the control in its controls note, name pitch-preserving playback in its "not modeled here" note, and move its model version to 0.13.0; stage 10's note MUST no longer list the speed control; the canonical text and the README's manifest table MUST be re-blessed once in the feature's first phase (a second only if a later review changes a string), each recorded in the CHANGELOG (spec 007 FR-015). The signal-chain table's row 9 MUST say "Modeled by" for the speed control.
- **FR-012**: **C API.** The C API MUST gain functions to set and read the level and the published ratios, a status code for a refused level, and parity with the Rust API (bit-identical on an exactly representable stimulus). The settings structure MUST stay 120 bytes.
- **FR-013**: **Its own golden file.** The speed control MUST have its own golden file at the seven host rates: a constant-level playback at levels 1, 2, 4 and 5 of a steady tone and of the voiced stimulus, with the codec on and bypassed, and a playback with level changes (into and out of level 3, and between two non-3 levels), blessed once and reproducing on every golden-matrix platform.
- **FR-014**: **Records.** The README (a playback-speed section), the API documentation (Rust and C), the CHANGELOG, the register (A-054, S-018), the signal-chain table and the stage manifest MUST state the levels, the ratios, the pitch behavior, the playback-only scope, the live-change rule, the duration rule and that level 3 is unchanged, as modeled, not measured. The version MUST be 0.13.0.

### Out of Scope

The SPEED CONTROL switch as a separate control (an app maps "off" to level 3; whether the device keeps the level while the switch is off is open, A-054); mechanism (a), a codec clock change, even for the fast levels only; pitch-preserving playback, mechanism (c) (a follow-up feature if a capture shows it or an app asks for it); the duration reading of "+/-40%" as a selectable alternative; speed during recording or in the one-pass processing; the device's restriction that the level changes only during playback (the engine accepts it any time); a ramp or glide between levels; the 10-second review and the beeps.

### Key Entities

- **Speed level**: the host's setting, 1 to 5, default 3; what reading the level returns.
- **Speed ratio**: r for a level, 0.6 / 0.8 / 1.0 / 1.2 / 1.4 (A-054); output device samples per stored device sample are 1/r.
- **Place in the recording**: the stored-sample position the speed control has reached, advancing by r per output device sample; at level 3 a whole number.
- **Speed control (the stage)**: the device-rate resampling between stage 9 and stage 10 in the playback path; the identity at level 3.
- **Constant-level stretch**: the part of a playback between two level changes (or the start and the end).

## Assumptions

- **A-054** (new): the device's SPEED CONTROL plays a recording at one of five levels, factory set to 3 = normal (S-001), selected by the DSP firmware on a command from the microcontroller (S-002). Ratios: the speed reading of "+/-40%" (S-012), 0.6 / 0.8 / 1.0 / 1.2 / 1.4 with linear spacing assumed; the duration reading (speeds 1/1.4 … 1/0.6, that is 0.714 … 1.667) is the registered alternative. Mechanism: unknown; a codec clock change cannot produce the slow levels within the codec's stated 6.0 – 10.0 kHz sync range (S-003; strong but not proof, since a recommended operating range is not a physical limit); the candidates are (b) resampling at the fixed 6 kHz clock (pitch moves) and (c) time-scaling in or after the decoder (pitch kept; the weak circumstantial lean, from the codec maker's 1994 patent and Panasonic's later marketing, S-018), with (a) a codec clock change still possible for the fast levels (under the duration reading the fastest level would put the codec at exactly its 10.0 kHz maximum). The emulator models **(b)** as the owner's labelled default (2026-10-17): it is well defined, testable and cheap; (c) is a follow-up. Confidence low (ratios, mechanism), high that the control exists and is firmware. Verification: capture P1 (the template's priority captures): record 1 kHz and 2.3 kHz tones and a sustained vowel, play each at levels 1 – 5 into the earphone jack; the durations give the ratios; the tone plus its DAC image (f + f_image = 6 kHz at a fixed clock) tells a clock change from a fixed clock, and the vowel's pitch tells (b) from (c); the 2.3 kHz tone disappears at levels 4 – 5 under (b).
- The speed control sits in the DSP's playback firmware, which the manifest's stage 9 (the speech decoder) represents; the codec's reconstruction filter (stage 10) runs at the fixed 6 kHz clock under (b), so its response does not move with the level.
- Stored content lies below 2550 Hz in the default engine (the record band, A-027), so the fast levels lose the content between 3000 ÷ r and 2550 Hz (about 2500 – 2550 Hz at level 4 and 2143 – 2550 Hz at level 5) and the slow levels move the band's top to 1530 Hz (level 1) and 2040 Hz (level 2); with stage 4 and the codec bypassed, stored content up to 3000 Hz is handled by the same rule.
- Because the playback pulls stored samples as it needs them, the speed control can read ahead of the place it has reached; the plan uses this so that the speed control adds no latency and the place at the first output sample is the recording's start (FR-008).
- The level changes at a block boundary, as every control does since spec 006; the device's dial is detented (five discrete levels, S-001), so a step between levels with no glide is the modeled behavior; the abrupt change of ratio is the only discontinuity.
- The engine accepts a level change at any time, though the device accepts one only during playback (S-001); an app that wants the device's restriction applies it in its UI. Whether the device keeps the level when the switch is turned off is open (capture P1 notes it).
- The cost budget is stated as ratios against version 0.12 (about 66 ns per host sample on the speaker route, codec bypassed, on the development machine; spec 012's figures), the protocol and the runner-ceiling pattern of the 2026-10-17 timing chore; the decoder's own cost scales with the ratio, which only the codec-on worst-block check covers.
- The version is 0.13.0; no release has been published. The feature branch builds on the records chore (`research/noise-clock-power`, PR #31), which registers S-012 – S-017 and the corrected rows the spec cites.
