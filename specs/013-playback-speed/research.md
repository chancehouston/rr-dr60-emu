# Research: Playback Speed (spec 013)

Every figure here comes from `tools/playback-speed/speed_design.py` (written at plan time, 2026-10-17, and amended by the plan review; `uv run tools/playback-speed/speed_design.py --check`) unless marked as an architecture fact read from the code or a reviewer's measurement.

## R-01: Placement and structure

**Decision**: a new stage `stages/speed.rs::SpeedStage`, owned by `Pipeline` beside the player's decoder (`Pipeline { speed: SpeedStage, speed_level: u8 }`), run only by `Pipeline::play()`. The player's inner pull loop (`transport.rs`, `play()`: "Pull the next stored device sample (decoding a frame when the codec is on), or a zero of the drain") is refactored into a **source** that returns `Option<f32>`: the next stored device sample, or `None` once the recording's K stored samples are exhausted. The speed control takes the source as a closure, `SpeedStage::next(&mut self, src: &mut impl FnMut() -> Option<f32>) -> SpeedOut`, and the transport pushes each value into `PlaybackPath` (a new `push_device_f64`, the existing `push_wide`).

**Rationale**: architecture facts: playback is pull-based (the interpolator asks for a device sample when it needs one, `PlaybackPath::try_next_host`), the source (memory, decoder, cursor) is owned by `Pipeline`/`Transport`, and `PlaybackPath` is shared with the one-pass processing, which has no stored source. A pull interface lets the speed control read stored samples ahead of its place without adding latency (R-03), and keeps stage 10, the interpolator and the output chain unchanged (FR-003).

**Alternatives considered**: inside `PlaybackPath` (would need the memory and decoder, or a push interface that cannot read ahead); a push-model resampler with a fixed delay (adds latency, so the place at the first output would not be the start, and the level-3 identity would need the delay at level 3 too); the codec decoder producing speed-adjusted output (ties the control to the codec, but it must also work with the codec bypassed).

## R-02: The place in fifths, the level and the switch rules

**Decision**: the place is a `u64` count of fifths of a stored sample, `p5`, starting at 0, never derived from a floating-point ratio. Each device output is computed at the current place with the active level k (k = 3 … 7), then `p5 += k`. At a place p5 = 5i + ph (i whole, ph = 0 … 4):
- level 3 (k = 5, ph is always 0): the output is the stored sample x[i] widened exactly (`f64::from`);
- levels 1, 2, 4, 5: the output is Σ_{m=0}^{56} c_k[ph][m] · x[i + 28 − m] (R-03), x[j] = 0 for j < 0 and j ≥ K.

The host's level (`Pipeline::speed_level`, set any time) is copied into the stage at the start of each `play()` call (like the route and the volume, spec 009 FR-009), as the **requested** level. Before computing each output the stage first **latches** if the place has reached K (p5 ≥ 5K): from then on no request applies (plan review Q3: a pending switch into level 3 that would become whole exactly at K cannot drop the old level's tail, and a change set after the last own sample changes nothing). Otherwise it applies the request: a change to a non-3 level at once (the next output uses the new bank at the current place, the place then advancing by the new k); a change into level 3 **deferred** until p5 ≡ 0 (mod 5) (at most 4 outputs at the old ratio, because gcd(k, 5) = 1 for k = 3, 4, 6, 7: the script prints "at most 4" for every level); a different request during the wait applies at once and drops the pending switch (the spec's clarification).

**Rationale**: integer fifths make the place exact (no accumulated rounding, FR-005) and the five ratios exact; the duration rule follows directly: at a constant level the outputs with place < K number ⌈5K ÷ k⌉ = ⌈K ÷ r⌉ (the script's table: K = 120 gives 200 / 150 / 120 / 100 / 86). Because each output depends only on (place, level, stored samples), the output after a change equals a constant-level computation from the same place bit for bit (the continuity oracle, FR-007, R-08). The deferred switch avoids the phase step of a snap (D5: up to 0.8 sample, 96° at 2 kHz).

**Alternatives considered**: a fractional `f64` place (accumulates rounding; not exact); snapping into level 3 (a click); applying a change at the next whole sample for every level (needless; the banks work at every phase).

## R-03: The filter

**Decision**: per non-3 level, a linear-phase Kaiser-windowed sinc prototype of **281 taps** (10·D + 1, D = 28) at the 30 kHz prototype rate (5 × 6 kHz), **designed at construction** by the existing `resample::design::kaiser_lowpass(281, fc ÷ 30000, kaiser_beta(70))` and split by the existing `polyphase_split(h, 5)` into 5 phases of 57 taps, each normalized to sum to 1 (unit DC per phase, within 3.3e-16 in the script). Band edges, in the stored stream's frequencies:

| Level | k | r | Passband (Hz) | Stopband (Hz) | Cutoff (Hz) | Passband deviation (dB) | Worst spurious 20 – 3000 Hz (dB, at stored f) | Tail (device samples) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | 0.6 | 2550 | 3100 | 2825.0 | 0.0012 | −73.0 (2853 Hz) | 46 |
| 2 | 4 | 0.8 | 2550 | 3100 | 2825.0 | 0.0012 | −73.1 (2853 Hz) | 34 |
| 4 | 6 | 1.2 | 1875 | 2500 | 2187.5 | 0.0019 | −72.8 (2503 Hz) | 23 |
| 5 | 7 | 1.4 | 1607.1 | 2142.9 | 1875.0 | 0.0025 | −74.4 (2203 Hz) | 20 |

The slow levels' passband is the record band's top, 2550 Hz (the host converters' convention; plan review Q7: a 2250 Hz passband lost 0.15 dB at 2500 Hz and 0.68 dB at 2550 Hz, now 0.002 dB at 2500 Hz), and their stopband 3100 Hz (the image 6000 − f of a stored 2900 Hz tone, FR-004 (b)); levels 1 and 2 therefore share one prototype (three distinct banks). The fast levels' passband is 2250 ÷ r and their stopband 3000 ÷ r (the device Nyquist frequency after the move, FR-004 (b), (c)). The look-ahead is D = 28 stored samples (4.67 ms). Figures: a least-squares fit at r × f for the passband (tones every 50 Hz from 103 Hz up to min(2250, 2250 ÷ r)), a Kaiser-windowed (β 20) spectrum of the residual between 20 and 3000 Hz for the spurious (tones every 25 Hz from 103 to 2903 Hz; for r × f above 3000 Hz the whole output is the residual, FR-004 (c)).

**The dot product** uses **four partial sums in a fixed order**: s_q = Σ_{m ≡ q (mod 4)} c[ph][m] · x[i + 28 − m] for q = 0 … 3 (m ascending), y = (s₀ + s₁) + (s₂ + s₃). Any fixed order is deterministic (no fused multiply-add is generated without an explicit call); four independent chains cut the latency-bound loop's cost (plan review Q4: the single-accumulator 57-tap product measured 13.7 ns per device output on the development machine, 1.7 ns per host sample at 48 kHz, against a play-only base of about 24 ns).

**Pins**: the script prints per bank the cutoff in cycles per prototype sample, the energy Σ c² and the second moment Σ c · ((ph + 5m − 140) ÷ 140)² (levels 1/2: 0.094166666666667, 4.627700863476776, −1.029165517199091e-04; level 4: 0.072916666666667, 3.565198698361072, −1.304399895794038e-04; level 5: 0.0625, 3.044048562407605, 8.723903669859059e-05); the core tests pin the Rust banks to them within 1e-12, the spurious figures within 0.5 dB and the passband within 0.001 dB, and a core test pins a digest of each bank's `f64::to_bits` (from the Rust, a regression guard for the designer's code).

**Rationale**: the spec's prototype (`proto3.py`) showed 241 taps leaves level 5 at −66.9 dB and 281 taps at −71.7 dB; the per-level edges above give at least 12.8 dB of margin over the 60 dB floor at 281 taps. Designing at construction with the existing deterministic designer follows the host converters (004 research R-03; determinism across platforms by detmath) and costs 4 × 285 f64 ≈ 9 KB; this replaces the spec review's "generated by a design script with `--write`" (dated in the spec).

**Alternatives considered**: committed coefficients generated by the script as bit patterns (the hardware reviewer's D10; the host converters' runtime design is the closer precedent, and the pins above catch a change of the designer); per-level lengths (fewer multiplies at the slow levels; one length keeps the ring, the look-ahead and the tail rule uniform); a 60 dB design (131 taps; 5 dB margin, too thin, D8); linear interpolation (images 25 – 40 dB down; the spec chose a clean resampler).

## R-04: The ring, the look-ahead and the tail

**Decision**: the stage keeps the stored samples in a ring of 128 `f32` (a power of two above the 57-tap window plus two samples per output), `pulled` (stored samples pulled so far) and `end: Option<u64>` (K, once the source returned `None`). Reading x[j] for j ≥ pulled pulls from the source until `pulled > j` or the source ends; x[j] = 0 for j ≥ K. At level 3 only x[i] is needed (no look-ahead, FR-006); every pulled sample is written to the ring, so the history is there when the level leaves 3, and leaving level 3 at place i pulls up to x[i + 28] at once (at most 29 stored samples, at most one extra frame decoded in that call).

`SpeedOut` is `Own(f64)` while the place is before K, `Tail(f64)` while the kernel still reaches a stored sample (the lowest index read, i − 28 for ph = 0 and i − 27 for ph > 0, is at most K − 1), then `Silence`. At level 3 there is no tail (the first output at place K is `Silence`). **The tail is played after the recording's own part and before the drain** (plan review P1, Q2): the transport pushes `Own` and `Tail` values as device samples, and the drain (`drain_samples()`, unchanged) follows the tail, so stage 10, the interpolator and the output chain settle fully after it. The tail is T(K, k) device samples, at most **46** (level 1; 34 / 23 / 20 at levels 2, 4, 5; the script's worst case over K mod k), a pure function of K and k, which the length helper computes by the same rule (`stages::speed::tail_len`).

**Rationale**: the pull model makes the look-ahead free in latency; playing the tail avoids the truncation click of stopping at K (D3: the first tail samples reach 0.29 of full scale at level 5 on a recording ending on a full-scale sample, the hardware reviewer's probe); placing it before the drain works in every configuration, including those whose drain is shorter than the tail (0 samples at 6 kHz with stage 10 and the output chain bypassed; 80 at 8 kHz against a 61-sample tail).

**Alternatives considered**: the tail in place of the drain's first samples (the spec review's choice; no room where the drain is short); lengthening the drain at the levels other than 3 (the hardware reviewer's alternative; changes `drain_samples()` per level); stopping at K (cuts the last samples' response).

## R-05: Level-3 identity and the dynamic total

**Decision**: `Cursor::total` (host samples the playback produces in all) becomes **dynamic**: `u64::MAX` at `start_playback`, set to ⌈(K′ + T) · m ÷ l⌉ + drain the first time the speed control returns `Silence`, K′ the `Own` outputs and T the `Tail` outputs so far. **If `produced` has already reached the new total at that moment, nothing is pushed or emitted** and the call ends (plan review Q1: with a zero drain the 0.12 loop never requests device sample K; without this rule the new loop would emit one sample too many). The `place` reported by `play()` is min(⌊p5_last ÷ 5⌋, K), p5_last the place of the last output computed (0 before any); `frame_index` is min(⌊place ÷ 120⌋, F − 1) with the codec on and 0 with it bypassed.

**Proof of the level-3 identity** (architecture facts from `transport.rs` and `resample/up.rs`): at level 3 the stage pulls exactly one stored sample per output, in the same order and at the same moments as 0.12's loop (the interpolator's demand is unchanged), so every pushed device sample, every decode, `frames` and the output are identical; T = 0. Host sample n needs device sample ⌊n · l ÷ m⌋ (`PolyphaseUp::try_next_host`; at the identity rate the pending sample, n), so the first request for device sample K happens at n₀ = ⌈K · m ÷ l⌉, with `produced = n₀`; the stage returns `Silence`, the total becomes ⌈K · m ÷ l⌉ + drain = 0.12's precomputed one, and: (a) with a drain, the +0.0 is pushed exactly as 0.12 pushed its zero; (b) with a zero drain, `produced = n₀ = total`, so nothing is pushed or emitted, as in 0.12. 0.12's `frame_index` is the index of the last decoded frame, which is the frame of the last pulled sample ⌊i ÷ 120⌋ and F − 1 in the drain; the new rule gives the same. The existing tests `playback_produces_ceil_k_m_over_l_then_drain`, `play_reports_frames_and_index` and `drain_zero_when_stage10_off_at_6k` (`transport.rs`) must pass unchanged, and the eight golden files are the outer guard.

**Rationale**: a precomputed total cannot hold once the level changes mid-playback (C4); the dynamic one is exact for every schedule and reduces to 0.12's at level 3.

**Alternatives considered**: recomputing the total at every level change (needs the future schedule); counting device samples instead of host samples for `finished` (changes 0.12's `produced` accounting).

## R-06: The API

**Decision** (contracts/rust-api.md, contracts/c-api.md): Rust `Pipeline::set_playback_speed(level: u8) -> Result<(), Error>` (`Error::InvalidSpeedLevel { level }` for 0 or above 5, the level unchanged), `Pipeline::playback_speed() -> u8`, `Pipeline::playback_length_samples(number: u32, level: u8) -> Result<u64, Error>` (level checked first, then the number; ⌈(⌈5K ÷ k⌉ + T(K, k)) · m ÷ l⌉ + drain); `PlayInfo::place: u64`; the module `rr_dr60::speed` with `LEVEL_COUNT = 5`, `DEFAULT_LEVEL = 3`, `RATIO_DENOMINATOR = 5`, `RATIO_NUMERATORS = [3, 4, 5, 6, 7]`, `ratio(level) -> Option<f64>` (for display only; the engine never uses it), `LOOKAHEAD_SAMPLES = 28`, `MAX_TAIL_SAMPLES = 46`. C: status 25 `INVALID_SPEED_LEVEL`, `rr_dr60_set_playback_speed`, `rr_dr60_get_playback_speed`, `rr_dr60_playback_length_samples`, defines `RR_DR60_SPEED_LEVEL_COUNT`, `RR_DR60_SPEED_DEFAULT_LEVEL`, `RR_DR60_SPEED_RATIO_DENOMINATOR`, the numerators as five defines `RR_DR60_SPEED_RATIO_NUMERATOR_1 … _5` (cbindgen emits a define only for a literal const, 009 lesson; FR-012's "read the published ratios" is met by the defines), and `place` at the end of `RrDr60PlayInfo` (32 → 40 bytes on 64-bit targets; an **ABI break**: the struct has no size field, so a host built against the 0.12 header and linked against 0.13 is overrun, which the CHANGELOG states). The level is a `u8` like a C `uint8_t`; the control mirrors the volume's (spec 009 FR-003).

**Rationale**: the control surface of the volume is the established pattern; an integer level matches the device's detented dial; exact fifths let hosts compute lengths exactly.

**Alternatives considered**: an enum `SpeedLevel` (stronger typing, but a C mapping and five variants for a 1 – 5 integer); a speed setting in `Settings` (needs a reconfigure, which resets the paths: not live).

## R-07: Cost and timing protocol

**Decision**: three timing measurements, quoted from solo release runs with `--test-threads=1`:
- `timing::speed_cost` (in one build): the codec-bypassed playback of 60 s of −20 dB noise recorded with stages 1 – 5 bypassed, at 48 kHz on the speaker route in 4096-sample blocks, levels 1, 2, 4, 5 against level 3, each side the fastest of 45 strictly alternating runs; asserted ≤ 15 % on the development machine with a runner ceiling `RR_DR60_SPEED_COST_CEILING = 25` in ci.yml; nanoseconds per host sample printed, and the same at 6 kHz reported (where every host sample is a device output: 57 multiply-adds per host sample at the non-3 levels).
- `playback_timing` (a new test binary using only 0.12's API, `#[ignore]` so `cargo test` and coverage skip it): the same playback at level 3 (the default), printed in a parsable line; `scripts/ab-timing.sh` gains a **playback mode** (`scripts/ab-timing.sh 3a4eefb playback`) that copies `tests/playback_timing.rs` into the baseline worktree (`3a4eefb`, 0.12's merge) before building, and alternates the two builds **45** times; ≤ 3 % on the development machine; `.github/workflows/ab-timing.yml` gains the step, ≤ 5 % on the CI runner (the PR's `ab-timing` label).
- `timing::speed_codec_worst_block`: codec on, 6 and 48 kHz, blocks of 64 and 4096 samples, the level changed before every block through the cycle 3 → 1 → 5 → 2 → 4 → 3 …; the worst block against 1000 × block ÷ kHz µs (008's lesson); and the per-host-sample cost at a constant level 5 against level 3 (≤ 1.5× on the development machine, `RR_DR60_SPEED_CODEC_RATIO_CEILING = 2.0` on the runner).

Expected, against the play-only base of about **24 ns per host sample** at 48 kHz (the hardware reviewer's measurement; the 66 ns of spec 012 is the one-pass): the four-sum dot product about 0.5 – 1 ns per host sample, **+2 … +5 %** at the non-3 levels; under 1 % at level 3 (one branch and one store per device sample); with the codec on, level 5 decodes 1.4 frames per output second, inside 1.5×.

**Rationale**: the 2026-10-17 chore's lesson (in-build budgets need the 45-run estimator and a runner ceiling); 011's A/B lesson (a budget below the timing noise needs a build A/B); the baseline cannot have a test that does not exist in it, so the A/B overlays a test written against the old API (harness tests are auto-discovered).

**Alternatives considered**: the existing `split_costs_no_headroom` for the A/B (record and play together, the playback's share diluted); fastest-of-9 (run-order sensitive on this machine, 012's lesson).

## R-08: Oracles and measurement

**Decision**: `speed_checks.rs` with an **independent reference**: `reference_output(x, schedule) -> Vec<f64>` computes the speed control's device outputs from the decoded stream x (the level-3 playback in the same configuration, bit-exactly the stream), the published bank (`__test_hooks::speed_bank`), the four-sum order, and the places by the rule (R-02, its own integer arithmetic, the deferred switch, the latch), and the tail (R-04). Checks:
- **lengths**: `produced` minus `drain_samples()` at a constant level equals ⌈(⌈5K ÷ k⌉ + T) · m ÷ l⌉ and `playback_length_samples` minus the drain; `finished` once.
- **fit** (FR-004 (a)): at 6 kHz in `configs::speed_only` (codec, stage 10 and output chain bypassed), an imported stored tone; `analysis::sine_fit` at exactly r × f over the steady middle; amplitude ±0.1 dB, the residual's spectral peak ≤ −60 dB; at the measurement rates (`configs::measurement_rates()`) the level against level 3's playback of a stored tone at r × f within ±0.1 dB, **asserted** (the host converter's ripple common to both; plan review P9).
- **spurious** (FR-004 (b), (c)): `analysis::spurious_peak_db` (Kaiser β 20 window, 20 – 3000 Hz) of the fit's residual (or the whole output for r × f > 3000 Hz); asserted ≤ −60 dB at 6 kHz; pinned to the script's table within 0.5 dB at the table's tones; reported at the other rates.
- **US1 AS5** (the default path): tones at 500 Hz and 1 kHz; the fit checks the frequency and the amplitude (H(r × f) − H(f) ± 0.2 dB), not the 60 dB residual (the codec's own noise).
- **impulse** (FR-008): an imported impulse at stored sample 600 (levels 1, 2, 4) and 602 (level 5, so its place is a whole output: 430): the peak at the nearest output at 6 kHz; symmetric within 1e-12; at the half-sample ties (levels 2 and 4 at odd places) the two outputs equal within 1e-12.
- **completeness and tail**: a recording ending on a full-scale sample: the device outputs after the own part equal the reference's tail bit for bit, then the drain (or nothing, where it is 0).
- **continuity** (FR-007): for every ordered pair (a, b ≠ 3), two change points, and the schedules into level 3, the whole device output equals `reference_output` bit for bit, in `speed_only` and in `speed_only_codec` (codec on, stage 10 and the output chain bypassed, 6 kHz), the reported places matching the reference's; this is the spec's "constant-level playback from the same place" (the reference evaluated at the same places).
- **level 3**: every play report field against 0.12's rules (R-05), the record-then-play ≡ one-pass checks unchanged, the eight goldens.
- **scope**: the one-pass, the record path, the stored bytes and the export at every level against level 3 (bit-identical).

FR-004 is asserted at 6 kHz, where `play()`'s output is the device stream (C3), with the other-rate level comparison asserted at the measurement rates; the stimuli are imported (the samples form) or recorded with stages 1 – 5 bypassed, never the default record path (its hiss, spec 012). The comparison point of the bit-exact checks is the speed control's output (stage 10 and the output chain bypassed); with them on, the checks are the golden digests.

**Rationale**: constant-level runs and the stimulus itself are bit-exact oracles (011's lesson); an independent reference that shares only the published bank tests the place rule, the phase selection, the look-ahead, the tail and the switch rules (plan review Q5: a hook playback would share them with the system under test); a least-squares fit resolves 0.01 % where an FFT bin cannot (D1).

**Alternatives considered**: a `start_playback_at` hook (shares the implementation's place and phase logic, dropped); a tolerance-based continuity oracle; FFT frequency estimates.

## R-09: Golden file

**Decision** (contracts/golden-format.md): `golden-speed-v1.json`, generated through the public API with playback calls of **rate ÷ 50 host samples** (20 ms, 120 device samples, the same device time at every rate; plan review Q6) and each recording made once per (stimulus, rate, codec) and replayed at every level and route (P10): (a) the default engine (the codec on) recording `voiced_m20` and `tone_1k_m20`, played at levels 1, 2, 4, 5 on both routes at the reference volume, at the seven rates (112); (b) the codec bypassed, the speaker route (56; the route is downstream of the speed control, so the earphone adds nothing for it: a dated decision in the spec); (c) two schedules on the default speaker route (14): A: level 3 → 1 → 3 → 5 → 4 at calls 0, 20, 45, 65, 90; B: 2 → 5 → 1 at calls 0, 30, 55, then 3 after the place reached the end; (d) the speed-only configuration at 6 kHz on imported `stored_tone_1k` (a 1 kHz tone at −6 dBFS) and `stored_voiced` (`voiced_m20` at 6 kHz) at levels 1, 2, 4, 5 and the two schedules (12). 194 entries. Each entry carries the existing digest fields plus `speed_level`, `speed_changes` (`[call index, level]`), `place` (final), `own_length` (host samples before the drain) and `frames` (decoded, codec on), and `seed` and `counter` for the default-engine entries (012's format). The bless asserts the lengths against the length helper, and that schedule B's last change leaves the output identical to the schedule without it; no entry plays at level 3 throughout (the eight earlier files guard level 3). Estimated cost: 21 recordings of 2 s (7 rates × 3) and 182 playbacks of 1.4 – 3.4 s, about 30 – 40 s in a debug build, a few minutes instrumented (the codec dominates; 012's golden took a comparable time).

## R-12: Risks

- **Level-3 identity broken by the refactor** (the most likely defect): guarded by the three existing transport tests unchanged (R-05), the eight goldens checked in Phase 2 right after the core change, and a level-3 row in `speed_checks`.
- **Off-by-one in the tail, the latch or the total**: the independent reference; mutants without the tail, with the latch after the switch, with the look-ahead 27, without the drain-0 stop.
- **The drain's tail bound** (spec 009 FR-008): the drain now follows the tail, so the bound holds as before; re-checked at every level.
- **Cost on the runner**: the x86-64 runner reads 2 – 3× the development machine's in-build ratios for some code (010's clamp); the runner ceilings follow the 2026-10-17 chore.
- **Codec decode at level 5 in a 64-sample block**: at most one frame per 120 stored samples; at 48 kHz a 64-sample block computes at most 8 device outputs (11.2 stored samples), at 6 kHz at most 64 outputs (≤ 118 stored samples with the look-ahead filling), so at most one decode per block plus at most one when leaving level 3.
- **The rebase**: the branch is stacked on `research/noise-clock-power` (PR #31, the corrected registers it cites); before the PR it is rebased onto `main` if PR #31 has merged, otherwise the PR targets the research branch.
