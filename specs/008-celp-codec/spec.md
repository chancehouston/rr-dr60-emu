# Feature Specification: Speech Codec (CELP, 4 kbit/s)

**Feature Branch**: `008-celp-codec`

**Created**: 2026-10-12

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description (abridged; the full text is in the specify session's transcript): "Speech codec: model the RR-DR60's speech compression, signal-chain stages 7 (speech encoder) and 9 (speech decoder), assumption A-004. The service manual (S-002) gives the sourced facts: a CELP-type coder at 4 kbit/s on the Panasonic MN1931712BB DSP, fed by the codec's 6 kHz µ-law PCM stream. The device's own algorithm is unknown and no firmware or capture exists, so the fidelity target is a class match, not an algorithm match: a 4 kbit/s CELP-class encoder on the record path after the VAS and the matching decoder at the head of the playback path, modeled on a published CELP design of the period matched to the manual's 4 kbit/s and 6 kHz figures, with every unknown parameter registered as a low-confidence assumption. Asserted and measured: the exact bit rate, the codec's algorithmic latency, band and level transparency on speech-like stimuli, a noise and distortion floor, frame-periodic structure, determinism and a bounded per-block cost. A recording becomes a sequence of encoded frames; the exported byte form becomes version 2; recordings end on whole frames; record-then-play stays equivalent to the one-pass processing; the codec is on by default with one bypass for the pair and a new tap after the decoder; frames encoded and decoded are reported per block; manifest rows 7 and 9 flip to assumed; the earlier specs' checks run with the codec bypassed. Out of scope: noise reduction, µ-law quantization and idle noise, the sensitivity setting's digital gain, playback speed and the 10-second review, item locking, file I/O and app UI. Wording follows the claims policy."

## Overview

Everything the emulator does so far happens before the recording is stored or after it is read back: the limiter, the codec's band-limit and clamp, the voice-activated pause, the memory's accounting, the playback filter. The one thing the RR-DR60 does *to the recording itself*, the step expected to have the largest audible effect on its playback, is not modeled: the service manual ([S-002](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)) says the DSP compresses the 6 kHz stream with a **CELP-type speech coder at 4 kbit/s** ([A-004](../../docs/hardware/assumptions.md)), and the memory today keeps the engine's own device-rate samples instead ([spec 006](../006-record-playback-split/spec.md), which said this feature would change that).

This feature adds the two codec stages of the signal chain: the **speech encoder** (stage 7), which turns the post-VAS device-rate stream into frames of a fixed number of bits, and the **speech decoder** (stage 9), which rebuilds a device-rate stream from those frames at the head of the playback path. A recording becomes a sequence of frames, the memory's reservation shrinks from the 86 MB of 32-bit samples to the order of the device's own 16 Mbit flash, and the exported byte form moves to its second version.

What the feature can honestly claim is bounded by what is known. The manual gives the coder's class, its bit rate and its sampling rate; it does not give the algorithm: frame length, look-ahead, sub-frame structure, codebooks, bit allocation and post-processing are all unknown, and no firmware, recording or capture of a real unit exists. The target is therefore a **class match**: a 4 kbit/s CELP-class coder at the device's rate, modeled on a published design of the period and matched to the manual's figures, with each unknown parameter registered as a low-confidence assumption with a way to verify it (frame-periodic structure in a real unit's recordings gives the frame length, for example). The spec asserts and measures only what such a coder must do regardless of which CELP it is: the exact bit rate, the frame timing and latency, that speech-band material comes through at its level and spectrum within stated tolerances, a floor on the noise and distortion it adds, that the output is organized in frames, that it is deterministic, and what it costs. The *character* of the sound (the buzzy, watery rendering of everything, speech or not, through a speech model) is described, not claimed.

Nothing already checked changes: the earlier specs' checks run with the codec bypassed, as specs 001 and 002 run with the later stages bypassed, so the three golden files and their SHA guards hold; the codec gets its own golden file. The default output does change, because the codec is on by default as it is on the device, and the CHANGELOG records that as the adoption of A-004.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Record through the codec and hear it back (Priority: P1)

An app developer records microphone audio and plays it back. The recording passes through a 4 kbit/s speech coder on the way into the memory and through its decoder on the way out, so what comes back has a compressed, speech-shaped character modeled on the device's 4 kbit/s coder rather than a clean band-limited copy. The developer can read how many frames were encoded, what one frame is in time and bits, and can bypass the codec to hear the same material without it. The one-pass processing (microphone to speaker in one call) does the same thing in line, so everything the developer learned from it still applies.

**Why this priority**: the codec is the stage expected to have the largest audible effect on playback; without it the emulator reproduces the device's band and dynamics but not its compression. It is also the stage whose output is stored, so it must exist before any recording a user keeps.

**Independent Test**: can be fully tested with a fresh engine and the harness's deterministic speech-like stimuli (FR-005): record, stop, play at each supported host rate; compare record-then-play with the one-pass processing in the same run; measure bit rate, latency, level and spectrum transparency, noise floor and the frame structure against FR-003 – FR-007; check the frame read-outs. The memory's limits, export and the C API are not needed. P1 alone is a usable engine with the device's codec in the loop.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a freshly created engine at any supported host rate, **When** the host records a speech-like stimulus (FR-005) for 10 s, stops, and plays the recording back, **Then** at default settings the recording's length is a whole number of frames and the stored bits divided by the stored device time equal 4000 bit/s exactly (FR-003); and in the codec-only configuration (FR-015) at the decoder tap the output's level, long-term spectrum, log-spectral distance and segmental signal-to-noise ratio are within the tolerances of FR-005 and FR-006 of the same stimulus taken before the codec.
2. **Given** the same stimulus, **When** it is run through the one-pass processing at the default tap followed by the silence the stop fed (FR-009), **Then** the output equals the record-then-play output under the prefix-and-length rule of spec 006 FR-007 as restated by FR-009, bit-exactly, last frame included.
3. **Given** a recording in progress, **When** the host reads the block report after each record call, **Then** it shows the frames encoded in that block and in the recording so far, and the totals agree with the recording's length in frames when it stops; **Given** a playback in progress, **Then** each block report shows the frames decoded in the block and the index of the frame being played.
4. **Given** the codec bypassed, **When** the same stimulus is recorded and played back, **Then** the output is bit-identical to spec 006's record-then-play of that stimulus (the stored form is then the device-rate samples, FR-012), and the frame read-outs report zero frames.
5. **Given** two post-VAS streams (the codec-only configuration) that are identical up to the end of frame *k* plus the look-ahead (FR-004) and differ afterwards, **When** both are recorded, **Then** their first *k* frames are bit-identical, and the smallest offset past the end of frame *k* at which a change leaves frame *k* unchanged equals the reported look-ahead (frame causality; the look-ahead measured exactly).
6. **Given** any input, **When** it is recorded and played in blocks of different sizes, or on a different supported platform, **Then** the stored frames and the playback output are bit-identical (determinism, FR-016).

### User Story 2 - A memory of frames: capacity, limits and the stored form (Priority: P2)

The memory holds frames instead of samples. Its capacity is still 60 minutes and 99 recordings, counted in recorded device time, but the store it reserves is the size of the frames, about 1.8 MB instead of 86 MB. Recordings end on whole frames: at stop the record path runs on silence to the frame boundary, so the last frame is whole; at the limit a recording stops on a frame boundary. Exported recordings carry the frames in version 2 of the byte form and come back bit-identical; recordings written by an earlier form version are refused with a reason, and from the first release on, every released form stays decodable as it was written.

**Why this priority**: the stored form is user data once an app ships. Getting the frame granularity, the capacity rule and the versioning right before any recording is kept is cheaper than any later migration; the footprint matters for low-end phones.

**Independent Test**: can be fully tested with a small configured capacity and the harness's stimuli: record to the limit and check the stop point in frames; stop mid-frame and check the padding; export, alter, truncate and re-import; construct engines and read the footprint. No C host and no listening test needed.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an engine at the default capacity with the codec on, **When** the host reads the memory's reservation, **Then** it is at most 2.5 MB (SC-007) and the capacity still reads 3600 s and 99 recordings.
2. **Given** a memory configured for a small capacity (for example 2 s), **When** a recording runs past it, **Then** it stops at the last frame boundary that fits, the stop reason is "memory full", the recording is kept with a length that is a whole number of frames, and the remaining time reads exactly zero (capacity and recordings are both whole frames, FR-013).
3. **Given** a recording stopped by the host after 1234 device samples' worth of input (not a whole number of frames), **When** its length is read, **Then** it is the smallest whole number of frames that holds every input sample after the record path's pre-encoder flush, the frame completed by running the record path on silence (FR-013), and the stop report says how much silence was fed.
4. **Given** an exported recording, **When** its bytes are imported into the same or another engine of this version at any host rate, **Then** the recording has the same length in frames and bit-identical playback output; the bytes declare form version 2 and the codec's model version (FR-010).
5. **Given** a byte sequence in form version 1 (spec 006's device-rate samples), **When** it is imported, **Then** it is refused with "unsupported form version" and the memory is unchanged (FR-011); **Given** a version 2 sequence whose codec model version this release cannot decode, **Then** it is refused with "unsupported codec model version".
6. **Given** the harness's corruption set (truncations, single-byte alterations, foreign sequences, a frame count that does not fit), **When** each is imported, **Then** each is refused with the stated reason and the memory is unchanged (FR-010).
7. **Given** VAS splices during a recording (drop mode), **When** the events are read, **Then** their positions are counted in the recording's device samples exactly as in spec 003 and spec 006, whatever frame they fall inside (FR-014).

### Edge Cases

- **A recording shorter than one frame.** Input of fewer samples than a frame (after the pre-encoder flush) yields one frame, completed by running the record path on silence; a recording to which no input was fed is still discarded (spec 006 FR-012).
- **Stop exactly on a frame boundary.** No padding frame is added; the length is the whole frames stored.
- **Silence and near-silence.** The coder codes every frame the same way; there is no silence detection, comfort noise or mode switching in this version (A-041), so digital silence in gives a low but non-zero output whose level FR-006 bounds, and the encoder is defined on all-zero input. The device's coder never sees digital silence (the codec's idle noise is near −75 dBFS, A-030). The VAS, before the encoder, is what removes quiet passages, as on the device.
- **Non-speech input.** Tones, noise and music go through the same speech model; FR-005's transparency tolerances apply to the speech-like stimuli only, and the spec makes no transparency claim for other material. The frame-structure, bit-rate, determinism and latency requirements apply to every input.
- **Clipped input.** The clamp (spec 005) sits before the encoder; a frame of clipped input is coded like any other.
- **VAS pause inside a frame.** In drop mode the post-VAS stream is simply shorter and the frames are cut from it; a splice can fall anywhere inside a frame and its event position is unaffected (FR-014). In mute mode the muted samples are zeros that the encoder codes.
- **Capacity that is not a whole number of frames.** A configured capacity in seconds whose device-sample count is not a multiple of the frame length is rounded down to whole frames; the reported capacity is the rounded figure (FR-013). The default capacity is checked to be a whole number of frames.
- **The codec bypassed, then enabled again.** Reconfiguring the codec control while recordings are present is refused, so one memory never holds frames and samples at once; a host erases first (FR-012).
- **Import of frames into a bypassed engine, or of samples into a codec engine.** Refused with the reason "encoding differs from the engine's configuration", distinct from "damaged" and "does not fit" (FR-012).
- **A version 2 sequence from a later release.** Refused as "unsupported form version" by this release if its form version is higher; a later release imports this release's form unchanged (FR-011).
- **Record and playback at different host rates.** The frames depend on the input, the settings, the seed and the record host rate only; the decoder runs at the device rate, so playback at any host rate decodes the same frames (spec 006 US1 scenario 5 carries over, with the codec's output as the compared material).
- **The one-pass processing's trailing partial frame.** When the input ends mid-frame, the one-pass processing has not yet produced that frame; a host that wants the whole output feeds the same silence the stop would (FR-009, FR-013), as spec 003's lesson already says for the latency.
- **A frame with an unproducible bit pattern.** Every bit pattern decodes deterministically to finite output; if the design leaves code points unused, import refuses a frame that uses one as damaged (FR-010).

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**Stages 7 and 9**

- **FR-001**: The engine MUST model signal-chain stage 7 as a **speech encoder** that turns the post-VAS device-rate stream (6 kHz, A-026) into **frames of a fixed number of bits**, and stage 9 as the matching **speech decoder** that rebuilds a device-rate stream from those frames, with the encoder at the end of the record path after the VAS (stage 5) and the decoder at the head of the playback path before the codec output filter (stage 10). The coder is of the **CELP class at 4 kbit/s** (S-002, A-004), modeled on a published CELP design of the period matched to the manual's rate and bit rate; the plan names the design and registers every parameter the manual does not give (frame length, look-ahead, sub-frame structure, codebooks, bit allocation, post-processing) as assumptions A-040 and A-041 with low confidence and a verification method. When the design is published for 8 kHz, matching it to 6 kHz MUST conserve the durations (frame, sub-frame and look-ahead in milliseconds), the pitch range in hertz and the bandwidth expansion and weighting in hertz, and re-derive the sample counts, the predictor order (three formants fit the 225–2550 Hz band) and the bit allocation to the frame budget of FR-003; the plan states each derived figure. The documentation MUST say "modeled on a 4 kbit/s CELP-class coder of the period" and MUST NOT say or imply that it is the device's own coder.
- **FR-002**: The encoder and decoder MUST be **on by default** (the device has no bypass) and MUST be **bypassed together by one setting**; when bypassed, the record path stores the post-VAS device-rate stream as spec 006 does and the playback path reads it back, so the earlier specs' checks and golden files run unchanged with the codec bypassed. The documentation MUST say that a bypassed codec is a combination the device cannot produce. Turning the codec on by default is a user-visible change (constitution Principle II) recorded in the CHANGELOG as the adoption of A-004.

**Measurable properties (the class-match claims)**

- **FR-003**: **Exact bit rate.** Each frame MUST hold the same whole number of bits *B* for a frame length of *L* device samples with *B* × 6000 ÷ *L* = **4000 exactly** (S-002), so a recording's stored bits divided by its device time are 4000 bit/s for every whole-frame length; *L* MUST be a multiple of 12 so that *B* = 2*L*/3 is a whole number of bytes (10, 20, 30 or 40 ms give 40, 80, 120 or 160 bits). The 4000 bit/s is **frame payload**; the device's framing overhead, if any, is unknown (A-038's margin). The frame length (in device samples and in milliseconds), the bits per frame and the algorithmic delay MUST be readable constants.
- **FR-004**: **Latency.** The codec's algorithmic delay *D* (one frame plus the look-ahead, in device samples) MUST be a design constant of at most **300 device samples (50 ms)** (engineering bound; the plan's figure is expected near 20–30 ms). *D* is attributed **wholly to the record path and to the one-pass processing**: the record path's reported latency (spec 006 FR-016) becomes its pre-encoder latency plus *D* ("host input samples until the frame holding the corresponding device sample is stored"), the one-pass latency grows by *D*, the decoder adds no delay (a frame decodes as it is read) and the playback path's reported latency is unchanged, so the sum rule of spec 006 FR-016 still holds within one host sample. The look-ahead MUST be measured exactly by the causality probe (US1 scenario 5), so *D* = *L* + look-ahead by construction; the waveform alignment MUST be checked by the lag that maximizes the cross-correlation between the voiced stimulus (FR-005) and the codec's output in the codec-only configuration at the 6 kHz host rate, equal to *D* within **±2 device samples**, and at the other host rates within the same tolerance after adding the boundary's reported delay (the 999/1001 Hz group-delay method of spec 004 is undefined through a frame coder and is not used). Spec 001 FR-013's 20 ms default-latency budget is **restated to apply with the codec bypassed**, and a new bound applies with the codec on: the default one-pass latency MUST be at most **50 ms plus the codec-bypassed latency** at every supported host rate, both figures stated in the README.
- **FR-005**: **Transparency on speech-like material.** The harness MUST have at least two deterministic **speech-like stimuli**: a synthetic voiced signal (a pulse train through time-varying vocal-tract-like resonances, with a continuous pitch glide over at least one octave, about 90–220 Hz, so that every third-octave band from 315 Hz is populated, and an aspiration-noise component about 20 dB below the harmonics, so that it is not the one signal a CELP model fits perfectly; deterministic from a seed) and a synthetic unvoiced-to-voiced sequence. The measurements run in the **codec-only configuration** (FR-015) at the decoder tap, so that no other stage is attributed to the codec, at **three levels: −10, −20 and −40 dBFS** (the device's speech sits 10–35 dB below full scale, A-029). At each level the codec's output MUST have an RMS level within **±1.5 dB** of its input's; its long-term third-octave band levels at the centres from 315 to 2000 Hz MUST be within **±3 dB** of the input's, with the 2500 Hz band (which straddles the 2550 Hz band edge) reported only; and the mean **log-spectral distance** between the input's and the output's LPC envelopes (order 8, 20 ms frames, in dB) MUST be reported with a tolerance set at plan time. The tolerances are engineering targets **calibrated at plan time** by running the same stimuli, resampled to 8 kHz, through a documented reference coder of the same density (0.667 bit per sample: ITU-T G.723.1 at 5.3 kbit/s), each tolerance being that coder's figure plus a stated margin; the reference coder is used by a plan-time tool only, never at build or run time. No sample-wise or tone-level transparency is claimed: speech coders are not waveform-transparent, and tones are not speech.
- **FR-006**: **Noise and distortion floor.** On the voiced stimulus at each level of FR-005, the codec's **segmental signal-to-noise ratio** (20 ms segments, input against delay-aligned output, each segment clamped to −10 … +35 dB, segments below −50 dBFS excluded, averaged in dB) MUST be at least **4 dB** (engineering floor, calibrated like FR-005's tolerances; 4 kbit/s coders of the period reach 5–10 dB; the figure is reported per level). The encoder MUST be well defined on all-zero input (finite output, no failure), and on digital silence the codec's output MUST stay below **−60 dBFS** RMS after the first frame (a bound on the model, tied to the gain quantizer's lowest step; the device's coder never sees digital silence, A-030). On the unvoiced-to-voiced stimulus the output level MUST follow the input's within ±3 dB over 100 ms windows (no pumping or dropouts).
- **FR-007**: **Frame structure.** The encoder MUST be **frame-causal**: a frame's bits depend only on the post-VAS stream up to the end of that frame plus the look-ahead and on the encoder's own state, so two streams identical up to that point give identical frames up to that frame (US1 scenario 5, codec-only configuration). Frame boundaries MUST be fixed relative to the start of the recording (frame *n* covers device samples *n·L* to *(n+1)·L − 1* of the post-VAS stream for frame length *L*), so the frame grid is the same for every block partition of the input. The frame-periodic structure is the only artifact this feature asserts.

**Record, playback and the one-pass processing**

- **FR-008**: **Per-block reports.** Every record block report MUST carry the number of frames encoded in the block and in the recording so far; every playback block report MUST carry the frames decoded in the block and the index of the frame being played at the block's end; the one-pass block report MUST carry the frames encoded in the block. With the codec bypassed these read zero. No pitch, voicing, gain or codebook parameter of the coder MAY be reported (product requirement 4).
- **FR-009**: **Equivalence with the one-pass processing.** Spec 006 FR-007 MUST hold with the codec in the loop: the one-pass processing runs the encoder then the decoder in line at the point where the recording is taken, and the playback of a recording, before the drain, MUST be bit-identical to the same-length prefix of the one-pass output of the same input followed by **the silence the stop fed** (FR-013: the pre-encoder flush, then to the frame boundary) **plus the look-ahead**, with spec 006's slack of one device sample's worth of host samples; the recording's last frame is thereby reproduced bit for bit, since the padded samples are the record path's own output on silence in both. With the codec bypassed spec 006 FR-007 applies verbatim. Everything else in spec 006 FR-007 (dependence on input, settings, seed and record host rate only; cross-rate playback) carries over.
- **FR-010**: **Stored form, version 2.** A recording MUST be stored as its frames; the exported byte form MUST become **version 2**, carrying the form version, the encoding of the recording (frames, or device-rate samples when the codec was bypassed, FR-012), the codec's **model version** (the library version that last changed the coder, so an importer can tell which decoder the bytes need), the frame count or sample count, the data, and the same redundancy as version 1 (any truncation, any single-byte alteration and any foreign sequence in the harness's set is refused with a reason: unknown form, unsupported form version, unsupported codec model version, damaged, does not fit, encoding differs); the samples encoding carries no codec model version. **Every bit pattern of a frame MUST decode**, deterministically, to finite output within the device-rate range, so no imported frame can fail the decoder; if the design leaves code points unused, import MUST refuse a frame that uses one as damaged. Export MUST be deterministic and the round trip bit-exact at every host rate (spec 006 FR-014 carries over with the frames as the stored unit).
- **FR-011**: **Form versions over time.** This release MUST refuse form version 1 with "unsupported form version" (spec 006's device-rate samples; nothing has shipped) and MUST refuse any higher version the same way. From the **1.0 release** on, the engine MUST decode every stored-form version and codec model version it has ever released, bit-identically to the release that wrote it (a decoder is frozen per released codec model version and kept), so recordings a user made stay playable when the model improves; before 1.0 a form or codec model version MAY be dropped with a CHANGELOG entry, and a sequence that needs a dropped decoder is refused as "unsupported codec model version". The contract document MUST state this rule.
- **FR-012**: **The codec bypassed.** A recording made with the codec bypassed MUST be stored as device-rate samples (spec 006's stored content) and exported in form version 2 with its encoding declared, so a host can label it as not a device recording. The store MUST be reserved for the configured encoding (frames, or samples when bypassed); **one memory MUST NOT hold both encodings**: a reconfigure that changes the codec setting while recordings are present MUST be refused with the reason "stored recordings do not fit the new configuration" (the existing reason of spec 006 FR-019, generalized), and an import whose encoding differs from the engine's configuration MUST be refused with the distinct reason "encoding differs from the engine's configuration". Two consequences MUST be documented: a recording made with the codec bypassed cannot be imported into a default-configured engine, and because the one-pass processing shares the settings, comparing material with and without the codec through it needs an empty memory (the taps of FR-015 need no reconfigure). The alternative, a store sized for both encodings, was rejected in the clarify session because it gives up the 1.8 MB footprint.
- **FR-013**: **Whole frames.** A recording's length MUST be a whole number of frames: at stop, the record path **runs on silence until the frame boundary**: first its pre-encoder flush (the conversion and band-limit delay of spec 006 FR-005, so every input sample reaches the encoder; the codec's delay is not flushed as input), then whatever completes the frame, so the padded samples are the record path's own output on silence, as the device's DSP codes its stream until the frame ends; the stop report MUST say how many device samples of silence were fed; a recording with no input is still discarded. The memory's capacity MUST be a whole number of frames (a configured capacity is rounded down to whole frames and the rounded figure reported; the default 3600 s MUST be a whole number of frames for the chosen frame length), and a running recording MUST stop at the last frame boundary that fits with the reason "memory full" (spec 006 FR-012 with the frame as the stored unit). Lengths MUST still be reported in device samples and seconds; the memory's accounting stays in recorded device time (A-038).
- **FR-014**: **VAS events and the frame grid.** VAS event positions MUST keep their meaning (counted in the recording's post-VAS device samples, spec 003 FR-005 and spec 006 FR-006), whatever frame they fall inside; the encoder MUST NOT move, merge or align splices to frame boundaries.
- **FR-015**: **Taps.** The one-pass processing MUST offer a tap **after the decoder** ("the codec's output", before stage 10), and the existing after-VAS tap MUST keep its meaning as "before the codec". The **codec-only** configuration (limiter, VAS and the two band-limit stages bypassed, decoder tap) MUST be a supported case so a host can compare the same material before and after the codec. The default tap stays after stage 10.
- **FR-016**: **Determinism and real-time safety.** The encoder and decoder MUST produce bit-identical frames and output on every supported platform and for every block partition (constitution Principle IV; the golden matrix and the determinism tests extend to the codec); all arithmetic in the search and synthesis MUST be deterministic across platforms: the codec MUST use no platform math-library function, and ties in any search MUST resolve to the lowest index (a one-ulp difference would flip an index and every later frame through the adaptive state). The per-block record, playback and one-pass calls MUST NOT allocate, lock, perform I/O or do unbounded work; the codec's worst-case work per frame MUST be bounded by design (a fixed search), and the per-sample cost MUST be reported (SC-006).
- **FR-017**: **Playback by frame is not precluded.** The stored form and the decoder MUST allow a later feature to start playback at any frame and to play frames at another rate or by time-scaling (the device's SLOW/FAST speed control and 10-second review, S-001; the codec family's sync range starts at 6 kHz, S-005, so SLOW is plausibly time-scaling in the DSP, a lead for that feature) without a stored-form change; starting at frame *n* reproduces the from-start output only once the decoder's state has converged, so the stored form allows it and the output identity is not promised. This feature does not build either.
- **FR-018**: **State.** The encoder's and decoder's state MUST reset per recording and per playback (A-037), and a recording's frames MUST be decodable from the first frame without any state from another recording.

**Interfaces, registers and documentation**

- **FR-019**: **C API.** Every capability of FR-001 – FR-018 MUST be reachable through the C API with the same meaning: the bypass control and the new tap value in the settings structure (the only layout change, recorded in the CHANGELOG), the frame counts in the block reports (a layout change if the report structures grow, recorded likewise), the frame constants, form version 2 in export and import, each new refusal as a distinct status code ("encoding differs", "unsupported codec model version"), and spec 006's reconfigure refusal keeping its status code with the broadened meaning; no Rust-only types, caller-provided buffers, no panic across the boundary; the header regenerated and drift-checked.
- **FR-020**: **Manifest and registers.** The manifest's stage 7 and 9 entries MUST read status "assumed", confidence "low" (bounded by A-040 and A-041; A-004's headline level is high for the class and rate), model version equal to this feature's library version, with coverage notes naming the device's unidentified frame length, look-ahead and codebooks, and controls notes naming the bypass control, the decoder tap and the frame-count report fields; stage 8's row and coverage note MUST be rewritten for the frame store (model version 0.8.0; the stored unit becomes the frame in A-038 and A-039); the header counts MUST read 8 not modeled / 7 assumed; the signal-chain table rows 7 and 9 MUST gain the "Modeled by" marker with this spec's link; A-004's status MUST become adopted (its Status cell not beginning with "verified", since the algorithm stays assumed); A-040 and A-041 MUST be registered (rationale, confidence low, verification), including the density inference (0.667 bit per sample, that of a 5.3 kbit/s coder at 8 kHz) and the payload-versus-overhead note; the Matsushita CELP literature (the 1994–95 patent line, the November 1996 patents and the 2002 multi-dispersed-pulse paper) and ITU-T G.723.1 as the equal-density reference MUST be registered as **secondary** sources for the class, not for the device, dated honestly; the canonical manifest text and the README table MUST be re-blessed once with a CHANGELOG entry (spec 007 FR-015); the cross-check and the traceability audit MUST pass.
- **FR-021**: **Golden files.** The three existing golden files MUST stay byte-identical and their SHA guards unmoved; the earlier specs' harness checks run with the codec bypassed. The codec MUST get its own golden file (stimuli × configurations × the seven host rates, including the codec-only and default configurations, the frame counts and a digest of the **frame bytes** as well as of the audio), blessed once at the end of implementation; the cross-platform golden matrix compares the frame bytes too.
- **FR-022**: **Documentation.** The README MUST gain a codec section (what the stage does, the class-match statement and its basis, the frame length and bits per frame, the measured figures of FR-004 – FR-006 as reported, the default-latency restatement, the bypass and its "not the device" caveat, the stored form's version and the frozen-decoder rule, the footprint); the CHANGELOG MUST record the new stages, the default change, the C layout changes, the form version and the version bump to 0.8.0; the contract document for the byte form MUST describe version 2.
- **FR-023**: **Wording.** Every string and document this feature adds MUST follow the claims policy and spec 007's denylist: "modeled on a 4 kbit/s CELP-class coder of the period", never "the DR60's codec", "the same codec", "measured" (except of the harness's own figures), "closely matches" or "verified"; the artifact is described as a speech-shaped rendering of all input ("buzzy, watery compression" is acceptable in the README), with no statement about what recordings contain.

### Out of Scope

Noise reduction (stage 6, A-009); µ-law quantization and idle noise at the codec input and output (A-030: the device's coder reads an 8-bit µ-law stream; the engine's reads its 32-bit stream, and the register says so); the sensitivity setting's digital gain (A-033); the playback speed control and the 10-second review (S-001; FR-017 keeps them possible); item locking; any file or disk I/O; app UI and localization; per-stage meters other than the frame counts (E-A); the clock's ±0.5 % pitch deviation (A-011); identifying the device's actual algorithm from a real unit (the verification methods of A-040 and A-041 say how).

### Key Entities

- **Frame**: the stored unit: a fixed number of device samples (the frame length *L*, a design constant) coded into a fixed number of bits *B*, with *B* × 6000 ÷ *L* = 4000.
- **Speech codec** ("the codec" in this spec): stages 7 and 9 together, the encoder and the decoder; distinct from the PCM codec of stages 4 and 10, which is always named with its stage.
- **Codec constants**: frame length (device samples, milliseconds), bits per frame, algorithmic delay (device samples); readable by hosts; cited to A-040.
- **Encoding** of a recording: frames (the codec on) or device-rate samples (the codec bypassed); fixed per engine configuration; declared in the exported form.
- **Recording** (revised): a number, a length in whole frames (reported in device samples and seconds), its frames; exportable as form version 2; importable if intact, of the engine's encoding, and fitting.
- **Stored form, version 2**: form version, encoding, codec model version (frames only), frame or sample count, data, redundancy; refuses version 1 and higher versions with "unsupported form version" and an undecodable model version with "unsupported codec model version".
- **Block reports** (revised): record adds frames encoded (block, total); the stop report adds the device samples of silence fed; playback adds frames decoded (block) and the current frame index; one-pass adds frames encoded (block).
- **Taps** (revised): after-VAS = before the codec; new after-decoder = the codec's output; after-playback stays the default.
- **Refusal reasons** (added): encoding differs from the engine's configuration; unsupported codec model version; "stored recordings do not fit the new configuration" generalizes spec 006's reconfigure refusal under its existing status code.
- **Assumptions**: A-004 (adopted), A-040 (frame length and look-ahead), A-041 (coder structure, bit allocation and post-processing modeled on the published design; no silence handling).

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: For 100 % of the harness's recordings at the seven host rates, the stored bits divided by the stored device time equal 4000 bit/s exactly, and every recording's length is a whole number of frames.
- **SC-002**: The reported record, playback and one-pass latencies obey the sum rule within one host sample at all seven host rates; the causality probe gives exactly the reported look-ahead; the cross-correlation lag in the codec-only configuration equals the reported delay within ±2 device samples at every host rate; the codec's algorithmic delay is at most 300 device samples; and the default one-pass latency with the codec on is within the bound of FR-004.
- **SC-003**: On the voiced stimulus at −10, −20 and −40 dBFS in the codec-only configuration, the codec's output level is within ±1.5 dB and its third-octave band levels at the 315–2000 Hz centres within ±3 dB of the input's, the log-spectral distance is within its plan-time tolerance, at all seven host rates, and every figure (the 2500 Hz band included) is reported.
- **SC-004**: The segmental SNR on the voiced stimulus is at least 4 dB at each of the three levels, digital silence decodes below −60 dBFS RMS after the first frame without failure, and the unvoiced-to-voiced stimulus's output level follows the input within ±3 dB over 100 ms windows; all figures reported.
- **SC-005**: 100 % of the determinism cases (block partitions, the record-then-play case, the C-versus-Rust case) and 100 % of the golden-matrix platforms give bit-identical frames and output; the frame-causality scenario (US1 scenario 5) passes for every tested split point.
- **SC-006**: The one-pass cost with the codec on, measured in the release timing job on the same machine as the codec-bypassed figure, is at most **8 µs per device sample** (equivalently 1000 ns per host sample at 48 kHz, about 5 % of real time; engineering budget, the figure reported at 6 and 48 kHz; today's one-pass is about 60 ns per 48 kHz host sample); the **worst single block** at 48 kHz with 64-sample blocks (the block that completes a frame does the frame's search) is reported and lies below the block's duration of 1.33 ms with the margin stated; and record-plus-play stays within 25 % of the one-pass (spec 006 SC-006 carries over).
- **SC-007**: An engine at the default capacity with the codec on reserves at most **2.5 MB** for the recording store (the frames of 60 minutes at 4 kbit/s are 1.8 MB), the figure is stated in the README, matches the reported reservation within ±10 %, and does not grow during recording or playback; with the codec bypassed the reservation is spec 006's.
- **SC-008**: The three existing golden files are byte-identical to their committed form and their SHA guards unchanged; the codec's golden file is blessed once; 100 % of its entries reproduce, frame bytes and audio, on every golden-matrix platform.
- **SC-009**: 100 % of the export round trips reproduce length and playback bit-exactly at all seven host rates; 100 % of the corruption set, the version 1 sequence, the unsupported-model-version sequence and the encoding-mismatch case are refused with the stated reason and leave the memory unchanged.
- **SC-010**: The manifest cross-check, the wording tests and the traceability audit pass; the header reads 7 assumed stages; the README table and the canonical text are re-blessed exactly once.
- **SC-011**: An app developer can find, using only the README and the CHANGELOG, what the codec models and on what basis, the frame length, the latency with and without it, how to bypass it, how to read the frame counts, and what the stored form's version means, in under 5 minutes.

## Assumptions

- **A-004** (adopted by this feature): the coder is of the **CELP class at 4 kbit/s** on the 6 kHz stream (S-002, high confidence for the class, rate and bit rate; low for everything else). The model is a published 4 kbit/s CELP design of the period (the plan names it) matched to those figures; **it is not the device's algorithm**.
- **A-040** (new): **frame length and look-ahead.** Unknown for the device; the model's figures are the chosen design's durations kept in milliseconds (expected 20 ms frames with a few milliseconds of look-ahead), with the frame length a multiple of 12 device samples so that the frame's bits are whole bytes. Inferences from S-002, registered with the assumption: 4 kbit/s at 6 kHz is 0.667 bit per sample, the density of a 5.3 kbit/s coder at 8 kHz, so an 8 kHz design run unchanged on 6 kHz samples would stretch its frame by 4/3 (a 160-sample frame becomes 26.7 ms) and the device's frame may be a round sample count rather than a round millisecond count; the 4000 bit/s is frame payload, the framing overhead unknown (A-038's 14 % margin could be exactly that: 4660 bit/s stored would fill 60 minutes). Low confidence. Verify: frame-periodic structure in a real unit's playback of a sustained vowel or of a click train (the period of the recurring excitation pattern gives the frame length); the recording-length quantum of a real unit (the shortest item it keeps) gives it independently.
- **A-041** (new): **coder structure, bit allocation and post-processing** follow the chosen published design (spectral envelope, pitch, excitation and gains per frame, bits summing to the frame budget); every frame is coded the same way, with no silence detection, comfort noise or mode switching (the manual lists no such function; the VAS removes quiet passages before the encoder). Low confidence. Verify: a real unit's rendering of white noise and of tones against the model's (the pitch and excitation behaviour on non-speech is where CELP designs differ most audibly); Panasonic literature on the MN1931712BB.
- The Matsushita CELP literature is the nearest documented relative of a coder Matsushita shipped on its own DSP in 1997–98 and is registered as a **secondary source for the class**, dated honestly: the 4 kbit/s multi-dispersed-pulse CELP paper (Ehara, Yasunaga and others, IEICE 2002; 20 ms frames; an ITU-T 4 kbit/s candidate in a study that produced no Recommendation) is *later* than the device, the patents with November 1996 priority are contemporary, and the 1994–95 patent line is closer in time. ITU-T G.723.1 at 5.3 kbit/s is the documented coder of equal density and the calibration reference of FR-005. None of it is evidence of what the RR-DR60 runs, and the spec does not require the model to be any one of these designs; the plan picks by density and documented behaviour on non-speech, the fidelity priority being the class character over parameter plausibility (clarify session). The DSP's ADPCM pins are unconnected (S-002), consistent with a software coder.
- The engine codes its own 32-bit device-rate stream, not an 8-bit µ-law stream (A-030 is out of scope); the register row for stage 7 says so, and the difference is expected to be far below the coder's own noise.
- The transparency and noise tolerances (FR-005, FR-006) are engineering targets set from the published performance of 4 kbit/s coders of the period and reviewed at plan time; they bound the model's behaviour, not the device's.
- Form version 1 is dropped without a migration because no release has shipped; the frozen-decoder rule (FR-011) starts at 1.0.
- The one-pass default latency grows by the codec's delay; spec 001's 20 ms budget is restated as the codec-bypassed figure and a 50 ms bound on the codec's addition is a requirement.
- The codec bypass is a Workbench feature in the app and the frames read-out is free (product decision, clarify session); the engine exposes both regardless.
- No release has been published; the CHANGELOG records this feature as version 0.8.0.
