# Feature Specification: Record and Playback Paths with a Modeled Memory

**Feature Branch**: `006-record-playback-split`

**Created**: 2026-10-11

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description (abridged; the full text is in the specify session's transcript): "Split the emulator's single microphone-to-speaker pass into the two paths the real RR-DR60 has and the app's core loop needs (record, stop, play, share; product decision 2026-10-11): a record path that turns incoming audio into a recording held in a modeled memory, and a playback path that plays a stored recording out through a selectable output route. The record stages (signal-chain stages 3–5) run as today and their device-rate, VAS-spliced output is what gets stored; recording stops by itself when the memory is full or the recording count is reached, and the caller is told why. Capacity is counted the way the device reports it (60 minutes, 99 recordings; S-001, S-002), configurable at construction; recordings can be listed, selected and erased; a recording can be exported as opaque bytes and re-imported bit-identically; the stored form is the record path's output today and will become encoded frames when the codec is modeled (A-004) without changing the interface. Playback runs a selected recording through stage 10 toward one of two routes, speaker or earphone, which sound identical until stages 11–13 are modeled; the device mutes the speaker while recording (S-002), so the two paths never run at once and the state is readable. Record-then-play must be bit-identical to today's one-pass processing (the golden files remain the oracle), the one-pass processing stays available, and every existing guarantee (block-based, real-time safe, deterministic, taps, settings, C API parity) is kept. Out of scope: the codec, stages 11–13, SLOW/FAST and the 10-second review, A-033, file formats and disk I/O, live monitoring, the stage manifest (feature 007)."

## Overview

Today the emulator is one pass: audio goes in at the microphone end and comes out at the speaker end in the same call, which is right for "what would this sound like through an RR-DR60" but not for how the device is used. The RR-DR60 **records** into a flash memory (99 items, 60 minutes in total; S-001, S-002) and later **plays** a chosen item back through its speaker or its earphone jack, stopping at the beginning of the next item (S-001). When either limit is reached, "FULL" flashes and recording will not start; items are erased one by one or all at once, and "any items after the erased item move down to take its place; there are no missing numbers" (S-001, read 2026-10-11 for this spec). While recording, the power amplifier is muted (S-002: the amp's mute pin reads standby during recording), so the device never records and plays at the same time. The app built on this engine lives in that loop: record, stop, play, share (product decision 2026-10-11, `docs/product/decisions.md`), and the app's inside view wants to light a record path and a separate playback path (`docs/product/front-and-inside.md`).

This feature splits the engine into those two paths and puts a modeled memory between them:

- the **record path** is signal-chain stages 3–5 as they exist (the limiter, the codec input band-limit with the full-scale clamp, the VAS), ending in a **recording** stored in the memory at the device rate with the VAS splices already applied;
- the **memory** holds up to 99 recordings and 60 minutes of recorded time by default (configurable), reports what is used and what remains, and lets a host list, select, erase, export and import recordings;
- the **playback path** plays a selected recording through stage 10 (the codec output band-limit) to a selectable **route**, speaker or earphone, at any supported host rate.

Nothing in the sound changes. Playing a recording back produces exactly the samples the one-pass processing produces today, so the three golden files stay the oracle, and the one-pass processing remains available as a convenience. What the host gains is the device's structure: a state that is idle, recording or playing; a memory that fills and can be emptied; recordings that outlive the call that made them; and a route that the later speaker and earphone features (stages 11–13; A-031, A-010, A-032) will give different sounds to. Those stages are not modeled here; the route exists so that the interface does not change when they are.

The stored form of a recording is today the record path's device-rate output. When the speech codec is modeled (A-004, a later feature), the stored form becomes encoded frames and the memory's accounting in recorded time stays the same, because that is how the device reports its capacity.

### Session 2026-10-11 (clarify)

### Session 2026-10-11 (hardware-emulation review of the spec)

- B1 (critical, verified by a probe): the one-pass engine carries the device-rate stream in 64-bit precision from the decimator to the output, so any 4-byte store quantizes before stage 10 and record-then-play would differ from the golden files by up to one 32-bit LSB (≈ −150 dB) in about a third of the samples. **Decided:** the one-pass processing first narrows the after-VAS device-rate stream to 32-bit precision as a documented change (the device's ADC is 8-bit µ-law at that point, A-003), the three golden files are re-blessed once with the measured delta in the CHANGELOG, and the split matches the re-blessed files bit-exactly with a 32-bit store (FR-007, FR-009, SC-002, SC-007). The 64-bit store (≈ 173 MB) and a 1-LSB tolerance were rejected.
- B2 (critical, by arithmetic): one latency of drain cuts a loud recording's tail at about −20 dBFS (the interpolator's response runs two conversion delays past the last device sample; stage 10 settles in 112 device samples at −100 dB). **Decided:** the drain runs until the playback path is settled, no opt-out (FR-008).
- B3: the playback length is exactly the stored count converted to the host rate, rounded up, at every rate, and the one-pass output can be up to one device sample's worth of host samples shorter; the oracle is lengthened by that amount and the prefix rule kept (FR-007).
- B4: both latencies are reported in host samples, each rounded once (FR-016, SC-008).
- B5: the VAS starts unpaused with a 1 s hang and the stop flush stores zeros, so a "silent" recording is never empty; an empty recording is one to which no input was fed, and stop then skips the flush (FR-005, FR-012, US2 scenario 7, A-039).
- B6: A-037 over-read S-002 (a switched rail, not a rail known to be off while stopped); confidence lowered, the reset recorded as an engine decision required by FR-007, a capture defined.
- B7: A-038's arithmetic stated once (2²⁴ bit ÷ 4000 bit/s ≈ 69.9 min; A-004's row uses 16 × 10⁶ and says 66.7, to be made consistent), the item-count dependence noted, FR-012's "exact device sample" generalized to the stored unit.
- B8: the exclusive route is sourced (S-001: "sound will not be heard from the speaker" with an earphone connected; FR-015); the amplifier's idle state is not sourced (FR-003).
- B9: the cross-rate tone check moved below the knee (−20 dBFS) and measured by a least-squares fit, so spec 004's aligned-phase rule does not apply (US1 scenario 5).
- B10: imported samples must be finite and in range (FR-014); the timing criterion runs in the release timing job with a 25 % bound (SC-006); the footprint is the engine's reported reservation (SC-007).

### Session 2026-10-11 (plan)

- Stage 10's settling length at −100 dB relative to its own impulse-response peak is **121** device samples from the committed coefficients (the review's 112 was an estimate against a different reference); FR-008 restated. The record and playback latencies, each rounded once to host samples, sum exactly to the one-pass figure at all seven rates (research R-06), so SC-008 has margin.
- The one-pass processing keeps its own path instances; the recorder and player are separate instances reset at every start (plan Complexity Tracking row 1). The route is held on the engine, not in the settings, and survives reset and reconfigure (FR-015).
- The exported form is version 1 of a 32-byte header plus little-endian 32-bit samples plus a CRC-32 (contracts/recording-form.md); "within the record path's output range" is defined as finite and non-subnormal, since with every stage bypassed the record path passes any normal input (FR-014).
- Capacity settings live in `Settings` (ranges 1 ..= 86 400 s and 1 ..= 9 999 recordings, engineering targets); a capacity the host cannot reserve is an error at construction, not an abort (FR-017).

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Record, stop, play (Priority: P1)

An app developer feeds microphone audio into the engine's record path, stops, and plays the resulting recording back through the playback path. What comes out is exactly what the one-pass processing would have produced for that audio, so everything already verified about the sound (specs 001–005) carries over unchanged. The developer never sees the two paths run at the same time, and can read at any moment whether the engine is idle, recording or playing.

**Why this priority**: it is the app's core loop and the reason for the feature. Without it the app cannot be built on this engine; with it alone, an app can record and play at the device's fidelity, even before the memory's limits and persistence exist.

**Independent Test**: for every stimulus in the three golden files whose configuration reaches the default tap, record it, stop, play it back at the same host rate, and compare with the stored golden output sample for sample; also compare record-then-play against the one-pass processing of the same stimulus in the same run at all seven host rates. Check the reported state before, during and after each step. The memory stays at its default capacity and its limits are not exercised (that is US2); no persistence or route feature is needed. P1 alone is a usable engine: record, stop, play, state.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a freshly created engine at any supported host rate, **When** the host starts a recording, feeds a stimulus block by block, stops, and plays the recording back at the same host rate block by block until the engine reports the end, **Then** the playback output, up to the end-of-recording drain, is bit-identical to the one-pass output for the same stimulus followed by the record path's own latency of silence, at the same settings and seed.
2. **Given** the default settings, **When** the host records the golden stimuli of specs 001, 002 and 003 that use the default tap and plays each back, **Then** each playback reproduces its golden output bit-exactly (within the comparison rule of scenario 1), without any golden file being re-blessed.
3. **Given** an idle engine, **When** the host starts recording, **Then** the state reads "recording" and "speaker muted"; **When** the host stops, **Then** it reads "idle"; **When** the host starts playback of the recording, **Then** it reads "playing" with the selected route; **When** the recording ends, **Then** it reads "idle" again.
4. **Given** the engine is recording, **When** the host asks to start playback, **Then** the request is refused with the reason "recording in progress" and the recording continues unaffected; **Given** the engine is playing, **When** the host asks to start recording, **Then** the request is refused with the reason "playback in progress".
5. **Given** a recording made at 48 kHz, **When** it is played back at 44.1 kHz (or any other supported host rate), **Then** playback succeeds; the recording's length and exported bytes are unchanged by the playback rate; the output is bit-identical to the playback at 44.1 kHz of the same bytes imported into a fresh engine; and a 1 kHz tone at −20 dBFS (below the knee, so the host-rate limiter plays no part), 5 s long with the VAS bypassed, recorded at 48 kHz and played at 44.1 kHz, has the same level and frequency as the one-pass processing of that tone at 44.1 kHz within ±0.1 dB and ±0.1 Hz, both measured by a least-squares sinusoid fit over the last second (phase-independent, so no aligned start is needed; the two outputs differ only by the record-side conversion, expected below 0.02 dB).
6. **Given** an input of any length and block pattern, **When** it is recorded in blocks of one size and played back in blocks of another, **Then** the output does not depend on either block size (bit-identical across partitions, as today).

### User Story 2 - A memory that fills and empties like the device's (Priority: P2)

The host records several times and the engine behaves like the device: recordings are numbered in the order made, the memory reports time used and remaining and how many recordings exist, recording stops by itself when the memory is full or the 99th recording is complete and the host is told why, and recordings can be erased one at a time or all at once. A host that needs less memory, or (later) more than the device, sets the capacity when it creates the engine.

**Why this priority**: the device's limits are part of its character ("60 minutes, 99 items") and the free tier of the app is defined as the device with its limits (`docs/product/monetization.md`); the Workbench's expanded memory is the same mechanism with a larger figure.

**Independent Test**: with a small configured capacity (so tests stay fast), record until the time limit and until the count limit, check the stop reasons and the exact sample at which recording stopped, erase one and all recordings and check the numbering and the figures. Uses the record path only; no playback or persistence is needed.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the default configuration, **When** the host reads the memory's figures before recording anything, **Then** it reads 0 recordings, 0 s used, 3600 s (60 minutes) and 99 recordings remaining.
2. **Given** a capacity of 10 s of recorded time, **When** the host records 25 s of continuous speech-like audio with the VAS bypassed, **Then** recording stops by itself at exactly 10 s of stored device time (60 000 device samples at 6 kHz), the block that crossed the limit reports how many of its samples were stored and the reason "memory full", the partial recording is kept with length 10 s, and the memory reports 0 s remaining.
3. **Given** a capacity of 10 s and the VAS on in drop mode, **When** the host records 25 s of audio of which the VAS pauses 20 s, **Then** the recording stops only if the stored (unpaused) time reaches 10 s; paused time costs no memory. **Given** the same in mute mode, **Then** the recording stops at 10 s of input, the muted regions having been stored as silence.
4. **Given** 98 recordings exist and a 99th is made, **When** it stops, **Then** the memory reports 0 recordings remaining, and a further start is refused with the reason "no recording slots left" while the memory still has time left.
5. **Given** five recordings numbered 1–5, **When** the host erases recording 3, **Then** the memory holds four recordings numbered 1–4 (the former 4 and 5 become 3 and 4), the erased recording's time returns to "remaining", and the others' content is unchanged; **When** the host erases all, **Then** the memory reads as in scenario 1.
6. **Given** the memory is full, **When** the host starts a recording, **Then** the start is refused with the reason "memory full"; **When** the host erases a recording and starts again, **Then** recording proceeds.
7. **Given** the host starts a recording and stops it without having fed any input, **When** it stops, **Then** the stop flush is skipped, no recording is added, the stop reports that the recording was empty and discarded, and the count is unchanged. (A recording of silence is not empty: the VAS starts unpaused with its hang time, A-025, and the flush stores zeros.)
8. **Given** an engine created with a capacity above the device's (for example 600 minutes and 999 recordings), **When** the host records beyond 60 minutes, **Then** recording continues up to the configured figures; the documentation states that such a configuration is not the device's.

### User Story 3 - Recordings that survive the session and can be shared (Priority: P3)

The host takes a recording out of the memory as a block of bytes, stores or sends it however it likes (a file, a share sheet, a network), and later puts it back into a memory, in the same or a later engine, where it plays back exactly as it did before. The host never needs to know what the bytes contain.

**Why this priority**: an app must survive being closed and reopened, and "share a recording" is in the app's loop; but a session-only memory (US1 and US2) is already a usable engine.

**Independent Test**: record a stimulus, export it, create a new engine, import the bytes, compare the imported recording's length and the playback output against the original engine's; mutate or truncate the bytes and check the refusal. No route or capacity feature beyond the import's own accounting is needed.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a recording in the memory, **When** the host exports it, **Then** it receives a byte sequence whose length is reported beforehand, so the host can size its buffer, and whose content is identical every time the same recording is exported.
2. **Given** an exported byte sequence, **When** it is imported into a different engine instance (same engine version, any host rate), **Then** the imported recording has the same length, and its playback output is bit-identical to the original's at every supported host rate.
3. **Given** a byte sequence that is truncated, altered, or produced by something other than this engine, **When** the host imports it, **Then** the import is refused with a reason that names the problem (unknown form, damaged, or does not fit) and the memory is unchanged.
4. **Given** the memory has 30 s of time or no recording slot remaining, **When** the host imports a recording of 40 s, or any recording when no slot is left, **Then** the import is refused with the reason "does not fit" (time or slot) and the memory is unchanged.
5. **Given** a byte sequence exported by an engine of this feature's version, **When** a later engine version imports it, **Then** the import succeeds and plays back identically, or is refused with the reason "unsupported form version"; the form is versioned so that this can always be told.

### User Story 4 - The same from C (Priority: P4)

A Swift or C developer does everything in US1–US3 through the C API: start and stop recording, feed blocks, read the state and the memory's figures, list, erase, export and import recordings by number, select the route, and play back. Errors come back as codes with the same reasons; nothing panics across the boundary.

**Why this priority**: the C API is the integration surface for the iOS app (Constitution I); it is last only because its behaviour is fully defined by the stories above.

**Independent Test**: a C smoke test that records a tone, reads the figures, exports, imports into a second engine, and plays back, comparing against the Rust path's output; plus the existing parity checks extended to the new calls.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the C header, **When** a C program performs the record, stop, play loop of US1 scenario 1, **Then** its output is bit-identical to the Rust path's output for the same input and settings.
2. **Given** any error case in US1–US3 (refused start, refused import, memory full), **When** it occurs through the C API, **Then** a distinct error code with the same meaning is returned, buffers the caller provided are left in a defined state, and the engine remains usable.
3. **Given** an app that only ever used the one-pass processing call, **When** it is rebuilt against this version, **Then** it needs no source change for that call, and its output is unchanged.

### Edge Cases

- **Memory fills inside a block.** The block's samples are stored up to the exact capacity boundary (in device samples), the remainder is discarded, the recording stops, and the block's report says how many were stored and why it stopped. The next block fed while idle is refused (not recording).
- **Count limit reached by a recording that is still running.** The 99th recording runs to the host's stop or to the time limit; the count limit only prevents a further start.
- **Stop while the VAS is paused.** The recording ends at the last stored sample; no pause event is left open; the paused time after the last stored sample is not part of the recording.
- **Stop flush.** When the host stops, the record path finishes the samples that are still inside its conversion and filters (its reported latency), so the recording contains everything the host fed up to the stop (see FR-005). If the memory fills during this flush, the flush is cut at the boundary like any other block.
- **Playback drain.** When the stored samples are exhausted, the playback path drains until it is settled (FR-008): stage 10's settling length of device-rate silence, then the conversion's full remaining response; the drained samples are deterministic and are not compared with the one-pass processing. Playback stopped early by the host ends at once, without draining.
- **Playback of an empty memory or a bad number.** Starting playback of recording number 0, or above the count, is refused with the reason "no such recording".
- **Erase during playback.** The device erases an item "while playing the item to be erased or stopped" (S-001): erasing the recording being played, or erasing all, stops playback first and then erases; erasing a different recording does not disturb playback but renumbers the others, and the engine reports the playing recording's current number.
- **Erase and import during recording.** Both are refused with the reason "recording in progress" (the device cannot do either while recording either).
- **A block fed in the wrong state.** A record call while not recording is refused with the reason "not recording"; a playback call while not playing with "not playing"; neither changes anything.
- **The recording in progress has a number.** It is the count plus one while it runs; if it is discarded as empty (FR-012) the number is free again.
- **Tiny or zero capacity.** A configured time capacity below one second or a count below one is refused at construction or reconfiguration with the reason "capacity too small".
- **Import of an empty or out-of-range recording.** A byte sequence that decodes to zero stored samples, or to any sample that is not finite or lies outside the record path's output range, is refused as "damaged" (FR-014).
- **Reconfigure while recording or playing.** Refused with the reason naming the activity; reconfigure is allowed when idle and keeps the memory's contents (the memory is data, not state), unless the new capacity is smaller than what is stored, in which case reconfigure is refused with the reason "stored recordings exceed the new capacity".
- **Reset.** Resetting the engine returns it to idle with cleared path state and leaves the memory's contents unchanged; "erase all" is the only way to empty the memory.
- **Route change during playback.** Allowed; it takes effect at the next block and is reported. Until stages 11–13 are modeled it does not change the sound.
- **Host rate change between record and playback.** The recording is at the device rate, so playback at any supported host rate is allowed; the playback host rate is the engine's configured host rate at the time playback starts.
- **The one-pass processing while a recording or playback is in progress.** Refused with the reason naming the activity; the one-pass processing has its own path state and is otherwise unchanged.
- **Device rate in the stored form.** The device rate is 6 kHz (A-026). A recording's length in time is its stored device samples divided by 6000; the figures the memory reports are exact integers of device samples and derived seconds, never rounded in a way that lets the sum of recordings exceed the capacity.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**Paths and state**

- **FR-001**: The engine MUST offer a **record path** consisting of signal-chain stages 3 (limiter), 4 (codec input band-limit and full-scale clamp) and 5 (VAS) exactly as specs 003–005 define them, including every setting, bypass and default, fed with host-rate input and producing device-rate samples with the VAS splices applied (drop mode) or the muted regions applied (mute mode); those samples are appended to the recording in progress. The tap setting MUST NOT affect the record path: whatever the tap says, the recording is the after-VAS device-rate stream; the tap keeps its meaning for the one-pass processing only (FR-009). The record path produces no host-rate output of its own (the device mutes its amplifier while recording, S-002).
- **FR-002**: The engine MUST offer a **playback path** consisting of stage 10 (codec output band-limit) as spec 004 defines it, including its bypass, fed from a stored recording at the device rate and producing host-rate output at the engine's configured host rate, toward a selected route (FR-015). The tap setting MUST NOT affect the playback path either: stage 10 runs unless its own bypass is set.
- **FR-003**: The engine MUST be in exactly one of three states, **idle**, **recording** or **playing**, readable at any time together with the selected route, the number of the recording being recorded or played, and whether the speaker is muted: reported muted while recording and unmuted while playing (S-002 gives the amplifier's mute pin in standby during recording and active during playback); the idle state is not sourced and is reported unmuted. A label in this version, since the amplifier is not modeled. Recording and playback MUST NOT run at the same time: a start request that conflicts with the current state MUST be refused with a reason naming the activity in progress, and the activity in progress MUST be unaffected.
- **FR-004**: **Start of a recording.** Each recording MUST start from the record path's reset state (the limiter discharged at unity, the VAS unpaused and the filters and rate conversion cleared), independent of earlier recordings or playbacks (an engine decision required by FR-007 and recorded as A-037: the analog section's rail is switched, S-002 Q20; that it is off while stopped and that the hold capacitor discharges with it are inferred, low to medium confidence). Each playback MUST likewise start from the playback path's reset state.
- **FR-005**: **Stop of a recording.** When the host stops, the record path MUST first pass its own latency of silence through itself (the conversion and filters' delay as reported by FR-016) so that every input sample the host fed up to the stop is represented in the recording, then close the recording. Stop MUST return the same per-block report as a record call (FR-006) for the flushed samples, including any VAS events and a "memory full" stop if the flush reaches the limit. If no input was fed since the start, stop MUST skip the flush and discard the recording (FR-012). The recording's length MUST be reported in device samples and seconds.
- **FR-006**: **Per-block reports.** Every record call MUST report how many device samples it stored, how many VAS events it produced (with the same event records as spec 003 FR-005, positions counted in the recording's device samples from its start), whether the VAS is paused at the end of the block, and whether and why recording stopped during the block. Every playback call MUST report how many host samples it produced and whether the recording (and its drain, FR-008) has ended.
- **FR-007**: **Equivalence with the one-pass processing.** For any input, settings, seed and host rate, the playback output of a recording made from that input, **before the end-of-recording drain** (FR-008), MUST be bit-identical to the same-length prefix of the output of the one-pass processing of the same input followed by silence of the record path's latency (FR-005) plus one device sample's worth of host samples (⌈host rate ÷ 6000⌉; the one-pass processing stops when its input stops, so its output can be that much shorter than the playback of the same stored samples). The playback length before the drain MUST be exactly the stored sample count converted to the host rate and rounded up (⌈K × host rate ÷ 6000⌉ for K stored samples), at every supported rate. The stored device samples MUST depend on nothing but the input, the settings, the seed and the record host rate; cross-rate playback is checked as US1 scenario 5. This equivalence holds bit-exactly only if the one-pass processing and the record path narrow the after-VAS device-rate stream to the same 32-bit precision at the same point (FR-009); consequently the golden files of specs 001, 002 and 003, re-blessed once for that narrowing, MUST be reproduced by record-then-play (SC-002).
- **FR-008**: **End of playback.** When the stored samples are exhausted, the playback path MUST drain until it is settled and then report the end and return to idle. The drain is stage 10's settling length of device-rate silence (the first device sample after which the stage's impulse response stays below −100 dB relative to its own peak; 121 device samples at the committed coefficients, computed at plan time and pinned by a unit test; zero when stage 10 is bypassed) followed by the rate conversion's full remaining response (two conversion delays of host samples), both derived from the design, not measured at run time. The drained samples are not compared with the one-pass processing (whose record filters keep ringing into trailing silence); they MUST be deterministic (FR-018), and for a 0 dBFS 1 kHz tone recorded with the VAS bypassed and stopped on a positive peak, the last 1 ms of the drain MUST lie below −80 dBFS at every supported host rate (engineering target). The drain's length in host samples MUST be readable before playback starts. The host MAY stop playback earlier; the engine then returns to idle at once without draining.
- **FR-009**: **The one-pass processing stays, after one documented narrowing.** The existing one-pass processing calls MUST remain, with unchanged meaning, settings, taps, block reports, VAS events and latency, and MUST be refused only while a recording or playback is in progress. Before the split is built, the one-pass processing MUST narrow the device-rate stream after the VAS (the point where the recording is taken; in the device the codec's 8-bit µ-law output, A-003) to 32-bit precision, so that a 32-bit store and the one-pass processing see the same samples. This is a user-visible output change (Principle II): its measured effect (expected at most one 32-bit LSB, about −150 dB, in a minority of samples) MUST be recorded in the CHANGELOG's 0.6.0 entry, the three golden files MUST be re-blessed exactly once for it in their own commit before any split code lands, and the files MUST then stay unchanged byte for byte through the rest of the feature.

**Memory**

- **FR-010**: The engine MUST hold recordings in a **memory** whose capacity is counted in **recorded device time** and in **number of recordings**, with the device's figures as defaults: 3600 s (60 minutes; S-001) and 99 recordings (S-001, S-002), and with both figures configurable at construction, lower or higher than the device's (A-038: the manual's 60 minutes is authoritative for capacity, although 16 Mbit at 4 kbit/s would hold about 70 minutes; the difference is taken as the device's own overhead and headroom). The documentation MUST state that figures above the device's are not the device's.
- **FR-011**: The memory MUST report at any time: the number of recordings, the recorded time used and remaining (in device samples and seconds), the recording slots remaining, and for each recording its number and length. Paused time MUST cost no memory in drop mode (the device's behaviour: dropped samples are not stored); in mute mode the muted regions are stored as silence and count against the capacity, and the documentation MUST say that mute mode is not the device's behaviour.
- **FR-012**: **Limits.** A recording MUST stop by itself at the exact stored unit (a device sample in this version; a frame once the codec is modeled) at which the stored total reaches the time capacity, keeping the partial recording and reporting the reason "memory full" in that block's report; a start MUST be refused with the reason "memory full" when no time remains and with the reason "no recording slots left" when the count is at capacity (S-001: "FULL" flashes and recording will not start when either limit is reached). That a running recording stops at the limit and keeps the partial recording is assumed (A-039). A recording that stops with no stored samples MUST be discarded, reported as empty, and MUST NOT count (an engine decision, A-039).
- **FR-013**: **Numbering and erase.** Recordings MUST be numbered 1 to the count in the order they were made; erasing a recording MUST return its time to "remaining" and renumber the later recordings down by one (S-001: "any items after the erased item move down to take its place; there are no missing numbers"); the host MUST be able to erase one recording by number or all recordings (S-001). Erase MUST be refused while recording with the reason "recording in progress"; while playing, erasing the recording being played or erasing all MUST stop playback first (S-001 allows erasing the item being played), and erasing another recording MUST leave playback undisturbed. Every call that acts on a recording (play, erase, export) takes its number; the engine keeps no separate "selected" recording.
- **FR-014**: **Export and import.** The host MUST be able to obtain any recording as a sequence of opaque bytes, learning the length first so it can provide the buffer, and to add a recording to a memory from such a sequence. A recording imported from the bytes of an exported recording MUST have the same length and the same playback output at every supported host rate, in the same or another engine instance of the same version. The byte form MUST carry a form version and enough redundancy that any truncation, any single-byte alteration and any foreign sequence in the harness's set is refused with a reason (unknown form, unsupported form version, damaged), every stored sample MUST be finite and within the record path's output range or the sequence is refused as "damaged"; and an import that exceeds the remaining time or slots MUST be refused with the reason "does not fit" and leave the memory unchanged. The documentation MUST state that the stored form is the record path's device-rate output in this version and will change to encoded frames when the codec is modeled (A-004), with the version field telling them apart.
- **FR-015**: **Route.** The host MUST be able to select the output route, **speaker** or **earphone**, through a dedicated control that is not part of the settings, at any time including during playback (as plugging in an earphone on the device); the selection MUST be readable, MUST survive reset and reconfigure, and MUST take effect at the next playback block. The routes are exclusive, as on the device (S-001: "sound will not be heard from the speaker" while an earphone is connected). In this version the two routes MUST produce identical output, and the documentation MUST say that stages 11–13 (volume and power amplifier, speaker, earphone follower; A-031, A-010, A-032) are not yet modeled and that the route exists so that those features do not change the interface.

**Guarantees kept**

- **FR-016**: **Latency.** The engine MUST report the record path's latency (host input samples until the corresponding device sample is stored) and the playback path's latency (host output samples from the start of playback until the first stored sample is represented), both in host samples at the configured host rate, each rounded once from the design figures; their sum MUST match the one-pass processing's reported latency within one host sample.
- **FR-017**: **Real-time safety.** The per-block record and playback calls, the state and figure reads, stop, start, erase and route selection MUST NOT allocate memory, take locks, perform I/O or do unbounded work. The store for the full configured capacity MUST be reserved at construction or reconfiguration, so that starting a recording never allocates and never fails for lack of memory; export writes into a caller-provided buffer and import reads from one, neither allocating. Erase MAY move stored data (bounded by the store's size) and is documented as not real-time safe. The documentation MUST say which calls are real-time safe and MUST state the memory footprint of the default capacity (SC-007).
- **FR-018**: **Determinism.** Same input, settings, seed and host rate MUST give bit-identical recordings and playback output on every supported platform and for every block partition of the input and of the playback requests (FR-007, US1 scenario 6), checked by the existing determinism matrix extended with a record-then-play case.
- **FR-019**: **Reconfigure and reset.** Reconfigure MUST be allowed only when idle, MUST keep the memory's contents, and MUST be refused with the reason "stored recordings exceed the new capacity" when they would not fit. Reset MUST return the engine to idle with the path state cleared and the memory untouched.
- **FR-020**: **C API.** Every capability of FR-001 to FR-019 MUST be reachable through the C API with the same meaning, with each refusal reason as a distinct error code, with caller-provided buffers for samples, event records and exported bytes, and with no panic crossing the boundary. The one-pass C call MUST keep its signature and behaviour. The C settings structure MAY grow by the capacity fields only (the route is a separate call); its layout change MUST be recorded in the CHANGELOG, and the header MUST be regenerated and checked for drift as today.
- **FR-021**: **Traceability and documentation.** Every default and modeled figure in this feature MUST cite its S-/A- ID (S-001, S-002, A-026, A-037, A-038, A-039); the three new assumptions MUST be registered in `docs/hardware/assumptions.md` with rationale, confidence and how to verify; `docs/hardware/signal-chain.md` MUST mark stage 8 (flash) as modeled for capacity by this feature and stages 11–13 as having a reserved route; the README MUST gain a recording-and-playback section with the state model, the memory figures, the byte form's status and the real-time-safe calls; the CHANGELOG MUST record the new capability, the C structure change and the version bump.
- **FR-022**: **Harness.** The measurement harness MUST check FR-003 to FR-008 and FR-010 to FR-016 at all seven supported host rates where a requirement applies (the equivalence of FR-007 against every applicable golden entry and against the one-pass processing in the same run; the capacity scenarios with a small configured capacity; the export round trip; the latency sum), each result reporting its measured value, requirement and IDs as the existing harness does, and MUST include the record-then-play timing of FR-017 (SC-006).

### Out of Scope

The speech codec and the encoded stored form (A-004); µ-law quantization and idle noise (A-030); stages 11–13 (power amplifier clipping, speaker response, earphone corners; A-031, A-010, A-032); playback speed control (SLOW/FAST) and the 10-second review (S-001); the sensitivity setting's digital-gain question (A-033); any file format or disk I/O (the host owns persistence; the exported bytes are not a file format); live monitoring during recording; per-stage meters and the scope copy (`front-and-inside.md` E-A, E-B); the stage manifest (feature 007); battery and power behaviour (A-013); item locking against erasure (the device's F.LOCK, S-001), recording date and time stamps, and the display's formatting of the available time (S-001 shows it as a negative figure below 10 minutes), all of which a host can keep beside the engine's recording numbers.

### Key Entities

- **Engine state**: idle, recording or playing; carries the selected route, the number of the recording in progress, and whether the speaker is muted.
- **Record path**: stages 3–5 with their settings; input at the host rate, output appended to the recording in progress at the device rate; reports stored samples, VAS events, paused flag, stop reason; starts from its reset state for each recording.
- **Playback path**: stage 10 with its setting; input from a recording at the device rate, output at the host rate toward the route; reports produced samples and the end; drains its latency at the end.
- **Memory**: capacity in device time and in recordings (defaults 3600 s and 99); holds recordings in order; reports count, used and remaining time, slots remaining.
- **Recording**: a number (1 to the count, renumbered on erase; the one in progress is the count plus one), a length in device samples, and stored content at the device rate; exportable as versioned opaque bytes; importable if it fits and is intact. Calls that act on a recording take its number; there is no selection state.
- **Route**: speaker or earphone; selectable and readable; identical in sound in this version.
- **Refusal reasons**: recording in progress, playback in progress, not recording, not playing, memory full, no recording slots left, no such recording, does not fit, unknown form, unsupported form version, damaged, capacity too small, stored recordings exceed the new capacity.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: An app developer can find, using only the README and the CHANGELOG, how to record, stop, play, read the state, read the memory's figures, erase, export and import, which calls are real-time safe, and what the route does in this version, in under 5 minutes.
- **SC-002**: After the single narrowing re-bless of FR-009, 100 % of the applicable golden entries are reproduced bit-exactly by record-then-play (under FR-007's prefix and length rule) with no further golden change: the 154 entries at the default tap (all 112 of spec 001's, the 21 `default_agc` and the 21 `default_vas`) as they are, and the 42 entries at the after-VAS tap (`vas_only`, `vas_mute`) with the playback stage bypassed; the 21 `agc_only` entries at the host-rate after-AGC tap are not reproducible by record-then-play and are excluded (196 of 217), and record-then-play is bit-identical to the one-pass processing in the same run at all seven host rates for the harness's stimulus set.
- **SC-003**: In 100 % of the capacity scenarios (US2), recording stops at exactly the configured device sample, the stop reason is the stated one, paused time costs nothing, and the reported figures (used, remaining, count, slots, numbering after erase) are exact.
- **SC-004**: 100 % of export round trips (US3) reproduce length and playback output bit-exactly at all seven host rates, and 100 % of the harness's truncated, single-byte-altered, foreign and oversized sequences are refused with the stated reason and leave the memory unchanged.
- **SC-005**: 100 % of the state-transition scenarios (US1 scenario 3 and 4, the edge cases) report the stated state and refusal reason, in Rust and through the C API.
- **SC-006**: The combined per-sample cost of the record path and the playback path at 48 kHz, measured in the same run on the same machine in the release timing job, is within 25 % of the one-pass processing's cost (engineering target; the figure is reported, and 10 % is the expectation), so the split costs nothing in headroom.
- **SC-007**: An engine at the default capacity reserves at most 90 MB for the recording store (engineering budget: 21.6 million device samples at 4 bytes each is 86.4 MB), the figure is stated in the README to the nearest megabyte, matches the reservation the engine reports within ±10 %, and does not grow during recording or playback.
- **SC-008**: The reported record and playback latencies, each in host samples, sum to the one-pass latency within one host sample at all seven host rates; the reported playback latency matches the measured onset within ±1 host sample; and the reported drain length matches the produced drain exactly.

## Assumptions

- **A-037** (new): **no state carries between recordings or playbacks.** S-002 shows the analog record section on a rail switched by Q20; it does not say the rail is off while the unit is stopped (the manual lists voltages for recording and playback only), nor how the hold capacitor C6 (1 µF, 10 MΩ discharge, τ ≈ 10 s) empties when the rail drops (plausibly through Q5's junction via R8, an inference). If the rail stays up at stop, a recording started within about 10 s of a loud one would begin still limited. The engine resets the record path per recording as an **engine decision required by FR-007**; A-037 records the device claim at **low to medium confidence**. Reversal is cheap (carry the limiter state across stop; no interface change). Verify: record a +9 dBFS 1 kHz tone for 5 s, stop, wait 0.5 s, 2 s and 10 s, record a −20 dBFS tone; any upward ramp over 1 dB in the second item's first 2 s shows carried charge. Also capture the first 0.5 s of several items for a rail power-up thump (C2 22 µF via R4 39 kΩ, τ ≈ 0.9 s) or a firmware blanking delay.
- **A-038** (new): **capacity accounting.** The figures are sourced (60 minutes and 99 items, S-001; 16 Mbit flash, S-002); what is assumed is the accounting: capacity is counted in stored device time, so VAS-paused time costs nothing in drop mode (the manual says VAS "avoids blank portions in recordings" and shows the available time counting down while recording), and the two limits are independent of each other. Rationale, not a claim: 2²⁴ bit ÷ 4000 bit/s ≈ 69.9 minutes (A-004's row says 66.7 using 16 × 10⁶; the register is to be made consistent, using 2²⁴), so the 60-minute figure leaves about 14 % for frame or sync overhead (4660 bit/s would fill 60 minutes exactly), a directory or spare area, or a round-down; which one cannot be told without the DSP's documentation. On a real unit per-item overhead could make the total minutes depend on the item count; the model treats the limits as independent. Medium confidence. Verify: fill a unit with one long item and with 99 short ones, with and without VAS pauses, and compare the displayed totals.
- **A-039** (new): **what happens at the limit, and empty items.** S-001 (re-read 2026-10-11) sources the numbering, the renumbering on erase, erase one or all, erase while playing, and "FULL" refusing a start; it does not say what a running recording does when the 60 minutes run out. Assumed: it stops at the limit and the partial item is kept (medium confidence; the class behaviour of IC recorders of the period, and discarding would lose a recording the user watched being made). Engine decision, not a device claim: a recording to which no input was fed is not created (a recording of silence is not empty: the VAS starts unpaused with its hang time, A-025, and the stop flush stores zeros). Verify: record past the limit on a real unit and check whether the last item exists and its length.
- The stored form of a recording in this version is the record path's device-rate output at 32-bit precision, kept exactly (bit-exact round trip, FR-014), after the one-pass processing has been narrowed to the same precision at the same point (FR-009, hardware review B1); the byte layout is a plan decision constrained by SC-007. The device's own form at that point is 8-bit µ-law (A-003), the natural intermediate once A-030 (µ-law quantization and idle noise) is modeled; when the codec is modeled the stored form becomes encoded frames and the footprint falls to the device's order of magnitude (2 MB for 16 Mbit, S-002). Both later changes re-bless the golden files on their own terms.
- The record path's latency flush at stop (FR-005) and the playback drain to settled (FR-008) are engine conveniences so that a host gets the whole recording out, without a click, and without feeding trailing silence itself (spec 003's measurement lesson); the device has no such notion. The flush changes no stored sample compared with the one-pass processing fed the same trailing silence; the drain is the playback path's own response to silence and is specified on its own.
- The route is a label in this version; stages 11–13 are later features and will give the two routes different outputs (A-031, A-010, A-032) without an interface change.
- No release has been published; the CHANGELOG records the new capability and the C structure change as the version 0.6.0 entry (Principle II). The default one-pass output does not change.
