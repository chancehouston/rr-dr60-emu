# Feature Specification: Output Chain (volume and power amplifier, speaker, earphone; stages 11–13)

**Feature Branch**: `009-output-chain`

**Created**: 2026-10-14

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description (abridged; the full text is in the specify session's transcript): "Output chain: model signal-chain stages 11, 12 and 13 of the RR-DR60 playback path, behind the speaker/earphone route that spec 006 reserved as a label. Stage 11 is the volume control and power amplifier (50 kΩ volume control, NJM2113 bridge-tied-load amplifier, muted while recording; S-002, S-006, A-031). Stage 12 is the 2.8 cm 8 Ω speaker in its tiny enclosure, whose acoustic response is unsourced and must be a registered low-confidence assumption with its own bypass (A-010), so every published figure of this feature is quoted on the earphone route. Stage 13 is the earphone output, an emitter follower from the volume control that bypasses the power amplifier, two mild first-order high-pass corners, no clipping (A-032). A volume setting as the device's own control; each stage bypassable with a tap after each; the earlier specs' checks run with stages 11–13 bypassed; the speaker route is the device's default output and the earphone route the measurement reference. Two questions the spec must settle: the output reference level once the signal has left the codec's full scale, and whether the one-pass processing runs through the selected route."

## Overview

The emulator's playback path ends today at the codec's output filter (stage 10). What the RR-DR60 does to that signal before anyone hears it is not modeled: the volume control and the small power amplifier that drives the speaker, the speaker itself, and the separate earphone output that bypasses the amplifier. Spec 006 made the **route**, speaker or earphone, a selectable, readable control, and documented it as a label: selecting a route changes nothing in the sound. This feature gives the route its sound.

The service manual's schematic ([S-002](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)) shows the playback chain after the codec: the codec output feeds a 50 kΩ linear volume control; from the volume control one branch goes through coupling components and a series resistor into an NJM2113 bridge-tied-load amplifier ([S-006](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)) whose gain is set by two resistors, which drives a 2.8 cm 8 Ω speaker and clips at the output swing its 3 V supply allows (the datasheet's rated 55 mW into 16 Ω and the manual's 110 mW into 8 Ω both put that swing at about 1.3 V peak at 10 % distortion); the other branch goes through an emitter follower and a series resistor to the 2.5 mm earphone jack, bypassing the amplifier, with two mild high-pass corners from its coupling capacitors. The amplifier is muted while recording. The registered assumptions [A-031](../../docs/hardware/assumptions.md) (volume and amplifier), [A-032](../../docs/hardware/assumptions.md) (earphone) and [A-010](../../docs/hardware/assumptions.md) (speaker) already describe the three stages; this feature adopts them as modeled behaviour, corrects what the draft reviews found misread in them (the clip level, the gain, one corner), and registers what they still leave open.

The three stages differ in how much is known, and the feature says so. Stage 11 and stage 13 are modeled on a circuit and a datasheet: their gain, clip level and corner frequencies are derived figures with stated confidence (the schematic sheet was re-read at plan time; the earphone's two corners depend on a transistor parameter and are registered as ranges). Stage 12, the speaker, is the least sourced stage in the chain: a part number and a power rating are sourced, its acoustic response is not. It is modeled as a registered **low-confidence template** (a small enclosed driver's bass roll-off, its resonance, and the rising tilt of a small case) with its own bypass, so that the stage manifest can carry an honest "assumed, low" badge, so that a host can switch the guess off and keep the sourced stages, and so that every figure this feature publishes is quoted on the **earphone route**, the electrical output that the field-measurement note ([docs/hardware/field-measurement.md](../../docs/hardware/field-measurement.md)) identifies as the cleanest return from a real unit. The speaker-over-earphone ratio of that note is the capture that would test stages 11 and 12 against a unit.

One honesty point about the corners belongs up front: the volume control's 32 Hz coupling corner lies well **below the codec's 225 Hz record band edge** and is a DC blocker on a recording the device itself made; the amplifier's 159 Hz corner trims the band's bottom by about 1 dB at 300 Hz; the earphone's two corners (about 110 and 190 Hz, both uncertain within an octave) sit at the band's bottom edge and thin the earphone route's low end. So on device-made recordings the audible output chain is the volume, the amplifier's clipping, the speaker template, and the earphone's roll-off; the 32 Hz corner matters only on imported or codec-bypassed material. The README says so.

Two decisions the feature description asked the spec to settle, rather than leave to the plan, are settled below and recorded under Clarifications: the **output reference level** (what a full-scale output sample means once the signal has passed the amplifier, where the codec's full scale no longer applies), and that the **one-pass processing runs through the selected route** as record-then-play does, so the two stay the same chain. Nothing already checked changes: the earlier specs' checks run with stages 11–13 bypassed, as spec 008's run with the codec bypassed, so the four golden files hold; the output chain gets its own golden file. The default output does change, because the device plays through its speaker, and the CHANGELOG records that as the adoption of A-031, A-032 and A-010.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-10-14 (specify: the two questions the description asked the spec to settle)

- Q: What does a full-scale output sample mean after the amplifier and the speaker, where the codec's full scale (A-030) no longer applies? → A: **Each route's output is scaled to that route's own electrical full swing** (FR-004, A-042): on the speaker route, 1.0 is the amplifier's clip ceiling (the signal that reaches the speaker, scaled so the amplifier's output swing is full scale; the speaker template is normalized to a peak gain of 1.0 on top of it, so the speaker route never exceeds full scale); on the earphone route, 1.0 is the volume control's full swing, which is the codec's full scale (1.0 V peak, A-030) at position 1 (the earphone path's fixed series attenuation into its 16 Ω load, about 30 dB, and the earphone's own sensitivity are loudness constants outside the model). Consequence, stated in the README: at the default volume position a full-scale recording reaches full scale on the speaker route, just at the clip, and sits about 12.5 dB lower on the earphone route (20·log₁₀ of the reference position); the relative loudness of the two transducers is **not** modeled, and a host sets its own playback gain per route. The alternative (one shared scale for both routes, letting the earphone route exceed full scale by the amplifier's gain at high volume) was rejected because a host writing to integer buffers would clip the one route the device does not clip.
- Q: Does the one-pass processing run through the selected route, as record-then-play does? → A: **Yes** (FR-010). The one-pass processing is the whole chain, microphone to the selected route's output, with the route and volume controls shared with the playback path; its default output becomes the speaker route's output; the signal before the output chain (today's default output, after stage 10) stays available as a tap, as do the points after stage 11 on either route. Record-then-play and the one-pass stay bit-identical under spec 008's equivalence rule, with the precondition FR-010 states.
- Q: Is the volume a setting or a control? → A: **A control**, like the route (spec 006 FR-015): the device's volume is a knob turned at any time including during playback; it is set and read through a dedicated control, survives reset and reconfigure, takes effect at the next block, and is not a settings field. Alternative rejected: a settings field, because a reconfigure resets the paths and the knob turns mid-playback. The three bypasses are settings.
- Q: What is the volume control's default position? → A: **The reference position: the position at which a full-scale codec output just reaches the amplifier's clip ceiling** (FR-003, A-043): the clip ceiling divided by the amplifier's gain times the codec's full scale, about 0.24 with the figures of A-031 (the plan computes the exact value from the constants it registers). So the default output does not clip on full-scale material, and the amplifier's clipping is something the host turns up into. The README states that the device clips on full-scale material at any position above the reference, so typical listening volumes clip. The alternative (maximum, where a full-scale recording clips by the full gain, about 15 dB, on the speaker route) is the device at full volume and is one setting away; the clarify session may revisit the default.
- Q: Which tap values does the output chain add, and what do they mean on each route? → A: The volume control belongs to stage 11 and is common to both routes; the amplifier is the speaker route's half of stage 11. So "after stage 11" is defined on both routes (volume control and, on the speaker route, the amplifier), "after the speaker" is the speaker route's output and "after the earphone" the earphone route's; a route-independent **route output** tap, which resolves to whichever of the two the selected route ends in, is the default; the two named taps are also selectable and are refused when the other route is selected; the signal before the output chain is the existing "after the codec output filter" point (FR-011).
- Q: Does the mute while recording become an audio effect? → A: **No change.** The record path produces no host-rate output and nothing plays during recording (spec 006 FR-003 reports the mute as state); the output chain adds no behaviour there. Documented, not modeled (FR-002).

### Session 2026-10-14 (coach C1–C14 and hardware-emulation D1–D10 reviews of the draft)

The user's standing choice is the recommended option; both reviews were applied in full.

- Q (D1, C1): What is the amplifier's clip level, and what does the datasheet's "output voltage 1.18 V (1.00–1.25)" row mean? → A: That row is the output pins' **quiescent DC level** (≈ V+/2 − 0.3 V: 2.68 V at 6 V, 5.71 V at 12 V), not the swing; the draft, A-031 and the S-006 register row had misread it. The clip comes from the rated power: 55 mW into 16 Ω at 10 % distortion (S-006) and 110 mW into 8 Ω (S-002) both give 0.94 V RMS, **≈ 1.3 V peak differential at 10 % distortion**, with 1 % distortion setting in at about 1.15–1.25 V and a hard ceiling near 1.4 V from the output pins' swing limits (V+ − 1.1 V high, 0.21 V low). The spec states the clip ceiling as a constant the plan fixes between 1.3 and 1.4 V peak (A-031, medium), and **every figure of FR-003 and FR-004 is derived from that constant** (C1): the reference position, the earphone route's level at the default, the clip onset. The S-006 row and A-031 are corrected in the registers (FR-015). Open: which rail feeds the amplifier (3 V battery or the 3.3 V converter; the ceiling is ≈ 1.7 V on the latter), a plan-time re-read.
- Q (D2): Is the reference position 1 ÷ the gain, and is the earphone route 16 dB down at the default? → A: **No.** The reference position is the clip ceiling ÷ (gain × the codec's full scale of 1.0 V peak, A-030): about 1.33 ÷ 5.64 ≈ **0.24**, and the earphone route at the default reads 20·log₁₀(0.24) ≈ **−12.5 dB**, stated with ±1.5 dB for the plan's constants. Nine occurrences of "16 dB" corrected.
- Q (D3): Does the 10 kΩ series resistor into the inverting input add to the gain-setting resistor? → A: **Yes** (pin 4 is the inverting input; every series ohm from the source is part of Ri): gain = 2 × 220 ÷ (68 + 10) = 5.64, **15.0 dB** at position 1 (inferred from the component list; the placement is re-read with the schematic sheet in the plan). The volume control's wiper resistance also enters that resistor and would make the gain position-dependent; it is folded into the volume-law convention of A-044 (D4).
- Q (D4, C5): Is the volume law linear, and is it sourced? → A: The **taper** is sourced (the part is a Panasonic "B" taper: linear, high confidence); the **loaded law** is not linear: both branches hang on the wiper permanently (the routes' exclusivity is at the jack), so the control's output into its ≈ 26 kΩ load reads about 3.5 dB below the linear value at mid-travel. Decision: the engine's volume position is defined as **the fraction of the control's full-swing voltage reaching the chain**, not knob rotation, so the engine's law is exactly linear by definition and the loading is a registered simplification (**A-044**, new, medium): the mapping from knob angle to position is not modeled. The alternative, modeling the loaded law (memoryless, cheap), is recorded for a later feature if the field loop measures the knob.
- Q (D5): Is the earphone's upper corner 160 Hz? → A: **Probably not as written.** With the 10 kΩ resistor in series into the follower's base (bias 33 kΩ), the 0.1 µF capacitor sees about 40 kΩ: a **≈ 40 Hz** corner; 160 Hz needs that resistor as a shunt to ground, and the follower's emitter return is absent from the S-002 summary. Decision: A-032's corner values drop to **low** until sheet 3.1 is re-read (the resistor's role, the bias resistor's ends, the emitter resistor); the spec states the upper corner as **40–160 Hz, the plan's re-read fixing it**, and the lower corner as 33 Hz (verified: 10 µF into 470 + 16 + about 10 Ω = 32.7 Hz). FR-007's ±10 % is a tolerance on the model's own template (C12: measured as a composite fit, not two isolated −3 dB points).
- Q (D6): Where is the amplifier path's input coupling corner? → A: The two 0.1 µF capacitors in series (0.05 µF) into 78 kΩ give **≈ 41 Hz** (20 Hz if a bias return sits between them); the spec bounds it **below 100 Hz** and lets the plan fix it with its confidence (C6). Consequence recorded in the Overview: both routes' corners lie below the 225 Hz record band edge, so on device-made recordings stages 11 and 13 are essentially DC blockers.
- Q (D7, C3, C6): The speaker template: its normalization, its ranges, what it leaves out. → A: **Peak-normalized to a gain of 1.0** (so the speaker route never exceeds full scale, keeping A-042's promise to integer-buffer hosts; the 1 kHz reading is then negative and stated), replacing "unity at 1 kHz with up to +6 dB over". Registered ranges (A-010 revised; the plan picks within them and records): resonance **600–900 Hz** with **Q 1.2–2.5** (a peak of +2 … +6 dB over the passband), second order below the resonance; **a first-order rising shelf of +3 … +6 dB from about 700 Hz toward 3 kHz** (the on-axis tilt of a 56 mm-wide case, whose baffle step sits near 2 kHz; this is much of the "thin" character); **flat above 2.5 kHz** (a 28 mm driver radiates well beyond the band, and the clipper now creates harmonics above the codec's band, so the choice is explicit); no driver nonlinearity, enclosure modes or room.
- Q (D8): Are the distortion criteria measurable at every host rate, and does the bracket discriminate? → A: **Not at 6, 8 and 16 kHz**: a 1 kHz tone's fifth and seventh harmonics alias onto the fundamental and the third and ninth onto the band edge. Distortion and peak figures are measured **at 44.1 kHz and above**; at the three low rates only the peak ceiling is asserted. The bracket is replaced: a hard clipper already gives 9 % at +2 dB over and 23 % at +6 dB, so "≥ 10 % at +6 dB" passed any shape; the new criteria are **10 % distortion reached between +1 and +3 dB over the ceiling, 15–30 % at +6 dB over, ≤ 1 % at 6 dB under** (the datasheet's 0.5 % at 20 mW into 8 Ω is 7.4 dB under the ceiling). The shape is **symmetric** (the high-side pin reaches its limit on both half-cycles of a bridge output) with a knee of about 1 dB of rounding; the plan picks a shape that uses no platform math function (constitution Principle IV). The README says that at 6–16 kHz host rates the clipped speaker route is band-limited by the host rate and is not the device's.
- Q (D9, C7, C8): Latency, group delay and the equivalence through filters with memory. → A: No reported latency, agreed, with the plan **constrained to minimum-phase recursive filters** (a linear-phase design would break FR-008). The chain's **group delay at 1 kHz is bounded at 0.5 ms per route and reported** (the resonance adds about 0.2–0.4 ms, 10–20 samples at 48 kHz, to the new default output); spec 004 FR-004's latency-equals-group-delay check is met with the chain bypassed; the README tells hosts aligning the default output to add the reported figure. The one-pass equivalence (FR-010) gains its precondition: the decoder's leading-delay output is exact digital zero, so the chain's state at the skip point is the rest state; the plan verifies this at all seven rates.
- Q (C2): Do the draft's tolerances survive its own corners? → A: **No**: a 160 Hz first-order corner reads −0.11 dB at 1 kHz and −0.97 dB at twice its frequency. The earphone level at 1 kHz is now **−0.25 … 0 dB** of full scale at maximum volume; the amplifier passband is flat within 0.2 dB **above five times** its corner; the two-corner earphone response is checked as a **composite** template within ±0.5 dB from 20 Hz to 2.5 kHz with the fitted corners within ±10 %.
- Q (C4): What does bypassing stage 11 mean? → A: The whole stage: volume control, coupling corner, gain and clipper off together, unity gain at the codec's scale (A-030); the volume position is retained and has no effect while bypassed. Alternative (amplifier only) rejected: a volume control without the stage it belongs to is not a device configuration either.
- Q (C9): "Position 0 gives digital silence" and "no sample exceeds the larger gain" through filters that ring. → A: Exact zeros are asserted at the **after-stage-11 tap** from the next block; at the route output the signal **decays below −120 dBFS within 50 ms and the state flushes to exactly zero**; the no-overshoot rule is scoped to the volume gain itself (the clipper and the templates downstream are not part of it).
- Q (C10): Is the default tap route-following? → A: Yes: a route-independent **route output** tap is the default and resolves to stage 12 or 13; the two named taps are also selectable and refused off-route.
- Q (C11): The clip's peak wording. → A: The speaker route's stage 11 output **never exceeds 1.0**; a sine 6 dB over the ceiling peaks at **≥ −0.1 dB**; the knee compresses a sine exactly at the ceiling by **at most 1 dB** (a bound on the shape the plan picks); FR-004's default-position reading follows from it.
- Applied without a question: US1 says "modeled as thin and resonant (A-010, low)" (C13); the S-006 register row's misreading, A-031's clip sentence and gain, A-032's confidence and the signal-chain rows' figures are listed for correction in FR-015 (D10); the checklist re-validated (C14). The hardware reviewer's "verify first on a real unit" list (earphone level against knob angle; a 20 Hz – 3 kHz sweep for the two low corners, where 40 against 160 Hz is a clean fourfold discriminator even through a phone input; the speaker terminals' peak where distortion passes 10 %) goes into the field-measurement note's table and the new assumptions' verification columns.

### Session 2026-10-14 (plan: the sheet 3.1 re-read and four plan-time amendments)

- Q (research R-02): What does S-002's analog sheet actually show for the playback path? → A: Read at plan time from the Internet Archive item's full-resolution scans (5100 × 5634 px). **The gain is 22.7 dB**, not 15 or 16: R18 68 kΩ is the feedback resistor from the output pin to the inverting input and R16 10 kΩ the series input resistor (2 × 68 ÷ 10 = 13.6); R19 220 kΩ with C16 10 pF is the microcontroller's beep injection into the same input, not the gain network. **The couplings**: C14 0.1 µF sits before the 50 kΩ volume control (a 32 Hz corner common to both routes); C8 0.1 µF into R16 at the inverting input gives the amplifier path a **159 Hz** corner. **The earphone branch is taken from the amplifier's input node** through C60 0.1 µF and R65 10 kΩ in series into the emitter follower's base (R64 33 kΩ to the 3.2 V analog rail), so it carries signal only while the amplifier is shut down (the microcontroller shuts it down through pin 1; a jack contact grounds R66's return and the microcontroller reads the plug); the emitter drives the tip through C61 10 µF; **R66 470 Ω is the emitter's DC return, not a series resistor**, so the draft's "30 dB series attenuation" and "33 Hz corner" were wrong and so were both readings of a "160 Hz or 40 Hz" corner. The earphone's two corners are the follower's **input coupling (C8 + C60 in series into 20 kΩ plus the follower's loaded input impedance: 110 Hz nominal, 70 – 145 Hz)** and its **output coupling (C61 into the 16 Ω load plus the follower's output resistance: 190 Hz nominal, 110 – 300 Hz)**; both depend on the transistor's current gain, so A-032's corner values stay **low** confidence as ranges with nominal values the hardware review of the plan may move. **The amplifier runs from the battery rail** (VDD read 2.8 V; the rail question is closed), so the clip ceiling is S-006's 3 V figure, ≈ 1.33 V peak, perhaps a little lower. Consequences: the reference position is about **0.098** and the earphone route at the default reads about **−20 dB** (FR-003, FR-004, US1 scenario 6, SC-004); FR-006 reads 22.7 dB with the 32 Hz and 159 Hz corners; FR-007 reads the two earphone corners with their ranges; the "DC blockers on device-made recordings" note is softened: the 159 Hz corner trims the band's bottom by about 1 dB at 300 Hz, and the earphone's corners sit at the band's bottom edge, so stage 13 shapes device-made recordings too (Overview, Edge Cases, FR-015's coverage notes). The field-measurement note gains the point that a high-impedance recording input bypasses the 16 Ω-load corner, so the electrical return reads the input corner alone. The S-002 register row, A-031 and A-032 are corrected on the branch.
- Q (research R-11, coach C9): Can the route output reach −120 dBFS within 50 ms after the volume drops to 0? → A: **No**: the 32 Hz corner's time constant is 5 ms, so −120 dB takes about 69 ms. The bound is **100 ms** (FR-009, SC-004, Edge Cases). Exact zeros from the next block at the after-stage-11 tap hold on the **earphone route** (stage 11 there is the coupling corner and the volume multiply: the corner's input is zero and its state rings; so the exact-zero statement is for the volume multiply's output, which the earphone branch's filters then ring down) — restated: at position 0 the chain's input to every filter is exactly zero from the next block, and every route output decays below −120 dBFS within 100 ms and flushes to exactly zero.
- Q (research R-08): Does the playback drain change? → A: **Yes**: the drain grows by the chain's settle length (83 ms: the 32 Hz corner to below 2⁻²⁴ of full scale) whenever any output stage runs, so spec 006 FR-008's "to settled silence" keeps its meaning; the earlier specs' length checks run with the chain bypassed (FR-016).
- Q (research R-06): Does any existing tap change meaning? → A: **No**: `AfterPlayback` stays "after stage 10, before the output chain" (version 0.8's default output) and meets FR-011's "named tap" clause; a route-independent **route output** tap is the new default and resolves to the selected route's last stage; `AfterVolumeAmp`, `AfterSpeaker` and `AfterEarphone` are appended.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Hear the speaker and the earphone differ (Priority: P1)

An app developer plays a recording back through the speaker route and then through the earphone route, and hears two different outputs: the speaker route modeled as thin and resonant (A-010, low confidence), with the amplifier's clipping when the volume is high; the earphone route the codec's sound with a roll-off below about 200 Hz and no clipping. The developer turns the volume up and down and hears the level follow it on either route. A vintage-gear enthusiast does the same to hear how much of the device's playback character is the speaker and how much is everything before it, switching the speaker stage off to keep only the sourced stages.

**Why this priority**: this is the mission's "sound leaving the speaker": the last stage of the device's chain and the most audible missing character for both audiences. It is also the point at which the route selected since spec 006 first changes the sound.

**Independent Test**: can be fully tested with a fresh engine in the **output-chain-only configuration** (every earlier stage bypassed, FR-011) at each supported host rate, feeding sweeps, tones and level steps to the playback path or the one-pass processing: measure each route's response against FR-005 to FR-007, the clip curve against the volume position (FR-003, FR-006), the earphone's corners, the volume law, and the bypasses. The memory, export and the C API are not needed. P1 alone is a usable engine with the device's two outputs.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the output-chain-only configuration at 48 kHz and the earphone route, **When** a slow sweep from 20 Hz to 3 kHz at −20 dBFS is played, **Then** the response relative to 1 kHz matches the earphone template of FR-007 within ±0.5 dB from 20 Hz to 2.5 kHz, and the same input on the speaker route with the speaker stage bypassed shows the amplifier path's response of FR-006 (the 32 Hz and 159 Hz corners and a flat passband).
2. **Given** the speaker route with the speaker stage bypassed, at the default volume position and 48 kHz, **When** a 1 kHz sine at full scale is played, **Then** the output peak reads full scale within the bound of FR-004 and the distortion is at most 1 %; **When** the volume is raised by 6 dB, **Then** the output peak still never exceeds full scale, reads at least −0.1 dB, and the distortion is between 15 % and 30 % (FR-006).
3. **Given** the speaker route with the speaker stage on, **When** the sweep is played, **Then** the response matches the registered template of FR-005 within ±0.5 dB from 100 Hz to 2.5 kHz (the bass roll-off, the resonance, the rising shelf), never exceeds a gain of 1.0, and bypassing the speaker stage removes it.
4. **Given** either route, **When** the volume position is set to 0.5 then 0.25, **Then** the output level follows the linear law of FR-003 within 0.1 dB at each position; **When** it is set to 0, **Then** the chain's filter input is exact zero from the next block and the route output decays below −120 dBFS within 100 ms and flushes to exactly zero.
5. **Given** the earphone route at 48 kHz, **When** a 1 kHz sine at full scale is played at the maximum volume position, **Then** the output peak reads −0.25 … 0 dB of full scale, no sample exceeds full scale and the distortion is below 0.1 % (FR-007: the earphone path has no clipping).
6. **Given** the earphone route and the default volume, **When** a full-scale 1 kHz sine is played, **Then** the output reads about 20 dB below full scale (20·log₁₀ of the reference position, within ±1.5 dB for the plan's constants), and the README states this consequence and its reason.

### User Story 2 - The whole chain, the route, the bypasses and the earlier checks (Priority: P2)

The developer who already uses the engine finds that the one-pass processing and record-then-play both end at the selected route, that switching the route or turning the volume during playback takes effect at the next block, that the pre-chain signal is still available as a tap, and that switching the three new stages off returns the engine of version 0.8 bit for bit. Everything earlier measured still holds.

**Why this priority**: the output chain must be a continuation of the chain the previous eight features built, not a side path; the equivalence rule and the golden files are the proof.

**Independent Test**: with stages 11–13 bypassed, every existing harness check and the four golden files pass unchanged; with them on, the one-pass processing and record-then-play of the same stimulus are compared under spec 008's equivalence rule at the seven rates; route and volume changes mid-playback are checked for their block timing; the output chain gets its own golden file.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the default configuration (every stage on, speaker route, default volume), **When** a stimulus is recorded, stopped and played, and the same stimulus is run through the one-pass processing with the same route and volume, **Then** the playback is bit-identical to the one-pass output under spec 008 FR-009's rule at all seven host rates, the decoder's leading-delay output having been verified as exact zero (FR-010).
2. **Given** stages 11–13 bypassed, **When** the four golden files and every pre-009 harness check run, **Then** they pass unchanged and the four SHA guards are unmoved.
3. **Given** a playback in progress on the speaker route, **When** the host selects the earphone route, **Then** the output from the next block on is the earphone route's and the earphone stage starts from its rest state (FR-009); **When** the host sets the volume, **Then** the next block carries the new gain.
4. **Given** the default configuration, **When** the host selects the tap after the codec output filter, **Then** the one-pass output equals version 0.8's default output bit for bit (the chain before stage 11 is unchanged).

### User Story 4 - The same from C (Priority: P4)

A Swift or C developer selects the route, sets and reads the volume, switches the three stages, selects the new taps and plays back through the C API with the same meaning and the same figures; nothing panics across the boundary.

**Why this priority**: the C API is the integration surface for the iOS app, which exposes a volume control and the route in its front view (product notes: front-and-inside.md).

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the C API, **When** a C caller sets the volume to a valid position and reads it back, **Then** the value round-trips; **When** it sets a position outside 0–1 or a non-finite value, **Then** the call is refused with a distinct status and the position is unchanged.
2. **Given** the C settings structure, **When** a 0.8 caller passes the old structure size, **Then** it is refused as spec 006 and 008 refuse old sizes; the new fields sit at the recorded offsets.
3. **Given** the C smoke loop, **When** it plays the exactly representable stimulus through the speaker route and the earphone route, **Then** both outputs match the Rust path's pinned hashes.

### Edge Cases

- **Volume position 0.** The volume multiply's output is exact zero from the next block (the control at its end), so every filter after it sees zeros and rings down: the route output falls below −120 dBFS within 100 ms and the state flushes to exactly zero; the taps before stage 11 are unaffected.
- **Volume position out of range or not finite.** Refused with a reason; the previous position stays.
- **Volume changed mid-block.** The new position applies from the next block; the engine MAY ramp within that block but the volume gain itself MUST NOT overshoot either position's value (the clipper and the templates downstream are outside this rule).
- **Route changed mid-playback.** Takes effect at the next block (spec 006 FR-015); the newly selected route's stages start from their rest state; the stages of the route just left are reset so a later return also starts from rest.
- **Output above full scale.** On the speaker route the clipper bounds stage 11's output at full scale and the speaker template's peak gain is 1.0, so the speaker route's output never exceeds full scale; on the earphone route the output can exceed full scale only if the input to the chain does (the playback filter's reconstruction overshoot, spec 005) scaled by the volume position, which never exceeds unity. Documented, not clamped.
- **DC and very low frequencies.** The playback filter passes DC (spec 004); the amplifier path's input coupling and the earphone path's corners block it, so neither route's output carries DC with the stages on. With stage 11 bypassed on the speaker route the speaker template still blocks it; with everything bypassed the DC passes as in 0.8.
- **Audibility on device-made recordings.** The 32 Hz volume-control corner lies well below the 225 Hz record band edge and is a DC blocker on a recording the device made; the amplifier's 159 Hz corner trims the band's bottom by about 1 dB at 300 Hz; the earphone's corners near 110 and 190 Hz thin the earphone route's low end. Stated in the README and the manifest coverage notes.
- **Low host rates and clipping.** At 6, 8 and 16 kHz a clipped 1 kHz tone's harmonics alias within the host band, so distortion is measured at 44.1 kHz and above only; the clipped speaker route at the three low rates is band-limited by the host rate and is not the device's (README).
- **The after-stage-11 tap on the earphone route.** Defined: the volume control's output (the amplifier is not on that route).
- **Named route taps with the other route selected.** Refused at the processing call with a distinct reason ("tap is not on the selected route"); the route output tap always means the selected route's output.
- **Stage 11 bypassed.** The volume control, the coupling corner, the gain and the clipper are off together: unity gain at the codec's scale; the volume position is retained and ignored. A bypassed output chain is not a device configuration (documentation).
- **Recording.** Nothing plays; the mute is reported state (spec 006); the output chain holds no state across a recording and a later playback starts from rest.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**Stages 11, 12 and 13**

- **FR-001**: The engine MUST model signal-chain stage 11 as the device's **volume control and power amplifier**, stage 12 as its **speaker**, and stage 13 as its **earphone output**, at the end of the playback path after the codec output filter (stage 10) and in the one-pass processing after the same point, arranged as the service manual's schematic shows (S-002, A-031, A-032): the volume control common to both routes; from it, on the speaker route, the amplifier's input coupling, the amplifier with its gain and its clipping, then the speaker; on the earphone route, the earphone path's two corners with no clipping (the branch is fed from the amplifier's input node while the amplifier is shut down, S-002). Which branch runs is the selected route (spec 006 FR-015); the routes are exclusive.
- **FR-002**: The three stages MUST be **on by default** (the device plays through its speaker) and **independently bypassable** by three settings; bypassing stage 11 removes the volume control, the coupling corner, the gain and the clipper together (unity gain at the codec's scale, A-030; the volume position retained and without effect). The mute while recording stays reported state (spec 006 FR-003) and adds no audio behaviour, because nothing plays during recording. Turning the chain on by default is a user-visible change (constitution Principle II) recorded in the CHANGELOG as the adoption of A-031 and A-032 and the revision of A-010. The documentation MUST say that a bypassed output chain is a combination the device cannot produce.

**The volume control and the output scale**

- **FR-003**: **Volume.** The host MUST be able to set and read the **volume position**, a number from 0 (silence) to 1 (maximum), through a dedicated control that is not part of the settings, at any time including during playback, taking effect at the next block, surviving reset and reconfigure; positions outside 0–1 or not finite MUST be refused with a reason. The position is defined as **the fraction of the volume control's full-swing voltage reaching the chain** (A-044), so the engine's law is exactly **linear in voltage**: the gain at position *p* is *p* times the gain at 1, within 0.1 dB at every position above 0.01 (the control's taper is linear, S-002; the loading of the control by the two branches, which bends the knob-angle law by a few dB, is a registered simplification, A-044). The **default position** MUST be the **reference position** (A-043): the clip ceiling divided by the amplifier's gain and the codec's full scale of 1.0 V peak (A-030), about **0.10** with A-031's figures (1.33 V ÷ 13.6), the exact value computed by the plan from the constants it registers, so the default output does not clip on full-scale material. The README MUST state that positions above the reference clip on full-scale material.
- **FR-004**: **Output scale** (A-042). Each route's output MUST be scaled to **that route's electrical full swing**: on the speaker route, a sample of 1.0 is the amplifier's **clip ceiling** (the constant of FR-006; the speaker template is then applied with a peak gain of 1.0, so the route never exceeds full scale); on the earphone route, a sample of 1.0 is the volume control's full swing, which is the codec's full scale at position 1 (the earphone path's fixed series attenuation into its 16 Ω load, about 30 dB, and the earphone's sensitivity are loudness constants outside the model, stated in A-042). Consequences to be asserted with every earlier stage bypassed at 48 kHz: a full-scale 1 kHz sine at the default volume reads a peak within **−1.0 … 0 dB** of full scale on the speaker route with the speaker stage bypassed (just at the ceiling; the knee's compression of FR-006 bounds the reading), and **20·log₁₀ of the reference position, about −20 dB, within ±1.5 dB** relative to full scale on the earphone route; at the maximum volume the earphone route reads **−0.25 … 0 dB** (the corners' residual at 1 kHz). The relative loudness of the two transducers is not modeled, and the README MUST say that a host sets its own playback gain per route.

**Measurable properties**

- **FR-005**: **Speaker template** (stage 12, A-010 revised). The speaker MUST be modeled as a registered **response template** of a small enclosed driver in a small case, whose parts and ranges A-010 registers and the plan narrows to chosen figures: a second-order bass roll-off with a resonance centred between **600 and 900 Hz** of **Q 1.2–2.5** (a peak of +2 … +6 dB over the passband below the shelf), a first-order rising shelf of **+3 … +6 dB** from about 700 Hz toward 3 kHz (the on-axis tilt of the 56 mm-wide case), **flat above 2.5 kHz** (stated explicitly, because the clipper creates harmonics above the codec's band), the whole normalized to a **peak gain of 1.0** (the 1 kHz reading is negative and stated); **no nonlinearity** of the driver (the audible distortion at high volume is attributed to the amplifier's clipping, FR-006), no enclosure modes and no room. The template's measured response MUST match its registered figures within **±0.5 dB** from 100 Hz to 2.5 kHz at every supported host rate, and its gain MUST never exceed 1.0. The documentation MUST describe these as the model's template, never as a measured driver.
- **FR-006**: **Amplifier path** (stage 11 on the speaker route, A-031 adopted and corrected). The amplifier path MUST have a voltage gain of **22.7 dB ± 0.5 dB** at 1 kHz at position 1 (2 × 68 ÷ 10 = 13.6: the 68 kΩ feedback resistor over the 10 kΩ series input resistor, read from the schematic sheet, and the datasheet's gain formula; S-002, S-006), applied after the volume control; two **input coupling corners**, one at **32 Hz** at the volume control's input (0.1 µF into the 50 kΩ control; common to both routes and so part of stage 11 on either) and one at **159 Hz** at the amplifier's input (0.1 µF into the 10 kΩ input resistor at the inverting input; speaker route only), each within ±10 % as fitted from the composite response (A-031; the volume control's wiper resistance, which lowers the gain and the 159 Hz corner at mid positions, is the registered simplification A-044), with the passband above five times the upper corner flat within 0.2 dB up to 2.5 kHz; and a **clipper** at the amplifier's **clip ceiling**, a constant the plan fixes between **1.3 and 1.4 V peak** differential (the output swing at the 3 V supply: the rated 55 mW into 16 Ω and 110 mW into 8 Ω both give 0.94 V RMS, 1.33 V peak, at 10 % distortion; the output pins' swing limits give the hard ceiling; A-031, medium), with a **symmetric** shape with about 1 dB of rounding at the knee, using no platform math function. Measured at 44.1 kHz and above with a 1 kHz sine: the output **never exceeds 1.0**; a sine 6 dB over the ceiling peaks at **≥ −0.1 dB** with distortion between **15 % and 30 %**; **10 % distortion is reached between +1 and +3 dB over** the ceiling; a sine 6 dB under the ceiling has at most **1 %** distortion (the datasheet's 0.5 % at 20 mW into 8 Ω sits 7.4 dB under); a sine exactly at the ceiling is compressed by **at most 1 dB**. At 6, 8 and 16 kHz only the peak ceiling is asserted. The gain, the corner, the ceiling and the knee shape are design constants cited to A-031.
- **FR-007**: **Earphone path** (stage 13, A-032 adopted and corrected). The earphone path MUST apply two first-order high-pass corners, modeled on the emitter follower the schematic sheet shows: its **input coupling** (the amplifier's input capacitor and the branch's own in series into the two 10 kΩ resistors plus the follower's loaded input impedance), **110 Hz nominal, registered as 70 – 145 Hz**, and its **output coupling** (10 µF into the 16 Ω load plus the follower's output resistance), **190 Hz nominal, registered as 110 – 300 Hz** (A-032: both depend on the transistor's current gain, confidence low; the plan picks the nominal figures inside the ranges and the hardware review of the plan may move them), each within ±10 % of its registered figure as fitted from the composite response, with unity gain in the passband (the follower's drive into 16 Ω, 10 – 19 dB below its input, is a loudness constant of FR-004), the passband above 1 kHz flat within **0.2 dB** up to 2.5 kHz, and **no clipping**: the output is the input scaled by the volume position and filtered, so a full-scale input at maximum volume passes with a peak of **−0.25 … 0 dB** of full scale, never exceeding it, and distortion below **0.1 %** at 44.1 kHz and above. The response (the 32 Hz volume-control corner and the two earphone corners) MUST match the template as a **composite** within **±0.5 dB from 20 Hz to 2.5 kHz**, with the corners fitted from it within ±10 % of the registered figures.
- **FR-008**: **Latency and group delay.** The output chain MUST add **no reported latency** on either route: its stages are memoryless or **minimum-phase recursive filters** (a linear-phase design is excluded), and their group delay is not counted, as spec 004 does not count the band-limit stages' edge group delay; the reported one-pass, record and playback latencies MUST be unchanged by the chain. The chain's **group delay at 1 kHz** per route MUST be at most **0.5 ms** and MUST be reported by the harness; spec 004 FR-004's check that the reported latency equals the measured 1 kHz group delay is met with the chain bypassed, and the README tells hosts aligning the default output to add the reported figure. The chain MUST run at the host rate after the resampler (like the limiter runs at the host rate before it), and each template MUST meet its stated response at every supported host rate.

**The chain, the route, the taps and the earlier checks**

At position 0 the volume multiply's output MUST be exact zero from the next block (the input to every filter after it), and every route output MUST decay below −120 dBFS within 100 ms with its state flushing to exactly zero (the 32 Hz corner's time constant is 5 ms). ## Assumptions

- **A-031** (adopted, corrected and extended by this feature): the volume control's taper is linear (a Panasonic "B"-taper part, S-002; high); the amplifier's gain is **22.7 dB** (the 68 kΩ feedback resistor over the 10 kΩ series input resistor, read from the schematic sheet at plan time; high for the topology, medium for the loaded value); the **clip ceiling is 1.3–1.4 V peak differential** from the rated output power at 10 % distortion and the output pins' swing limits at the battery rail (medium; the draft's "1.0–1.25 V" misread the datasheet's quiescent-level row; the rail was read as the battery line, VDD 2.8 V); the knee shape is a design choice bracketed by the datasheet's distortion figures (low); the input coupling corners are 32 Hz at the volume control and 159 Hz at the amplifier (high for the ideal values; the wiper resistance lowers both the gain and the 159 Hz corner at mid positions, A-044); the 220 kΩ / 10 pF path into the same input is the microcontroller's beep injection, out of scope. Verify: a capture at the speaker terminals with a 1 kHz tone and rising volume (the peak where distortion passes 10 % gives the ceiling), and the field-measurement note's speaker-over-earphone ratio.
- **A-032** (adopted and corrected): the earphone branch is fed from the amplifier's input node through a 0.1 µF capacitor and a 10 kΩ resistor in series into an emitter follower biased from the 3.2 V rail, and carries signal while the amplifier is shut down; the emitter drives the 16 Ω jack through 10 µF, with the 470 Ω resistor as the emitter's DC return to the jack's plug-detect contact (not a series resistor); its two corners are the input coupling (110 Hz nominal, 70–145 Hz) and the output coupling into 16 Ω (190 Hz nominal, 110–300 Hz), both depending on the transistor's current gain (low); no clipping at the codec's levels (medium: the follower's headroom is not analyzed). Verify: the earphone return of the field-measurement loop with a 20 Hz – 3 kHz sweep, into a 16 Ω load for both corners and into a high-impedance input for the input corner alone.
- **A-010** (revised): the speaker's acoustic response is a **template**, not a measurement: a small enclosed driver's second-order bass roll-off with a resonance at 600–900 Hz of Q 1.2–2.5, a first-order rising shelf of +3 … +6 dB toward 3 kHz from the small case's baffle step, flat above 2.5 kHz, normalized to a peak gain of 1.0; figures chosen by the plan within these ranges and recorded; low confidence. Verify: an acoustic capture of a real unit, or the field loop's speaker-over-earphone ratio with its room residual.
- **A-042** (new): the **output scale per route**: the speaker route's full scale is the amplifier's clip ceiling, the earphone route's is the volume control's full swing (the codec's full scale at position 1); the earphone path's series attenuation into 16 Ω (about 30 dB) and both transducers' sensitivities are loudness constants outside the model. Not a hardware fact but an engine convention, like A-030's mapping of 0 dBFS to +3 dBm0; registered so hosts and later features share it.
- **A-043** (new): the **volume control's default position** is the reference position at which a full-scale codec output just reaches the clip ceiling (the ceiling ÷ (gain × 1.0 V), about 0.24). The device has no default: its knob sits wherever the user left it; the choice keeps the default output unclipped on full-scale material, and positions above it clip. Low confidence as a "typical" setting; the clarify session may revisit.
- **A-044** (new): the **volume position is the fraction of the control's full-swing voltage reaching the chain**, so the engine's law is exactly linear; the loading of the control by the two branches, which makes the knob-angle law read a few dB below linear at mid-travel, is not modeled (medium). Verify: the earphone level against knob angle on a real unit.
- The chain runs at the host rate after the resampler and adds no reported latency; its group delay at 1 kHz is bounded and reported; its filters are minimum-phase and recursive.
- The 32 Hz volume-control corner lies below the codec's 225 Hz record band edge and is a DC blocker on device-made recordings; the 159 Hz amplifier corner and the earphone's corners near 110 and 190 Hz shape the band's bottom and are audible on them.
- The amplifier is active only on the speaker route and the earphone branch only on the earphone route (the routes are exclusive, S-001); the mute while recording is state, not audio.
- Distortion figures are measured at 44.1 kHz and above; at the three low host rates a clipped tone's harmonics alias within the host band, and the clipped speaker route there is band-limited by the host rate.
- The earlier specs' checks bypass stages 11–13; the output chain gets its own golden file; the CHANGELOG records version 0.9.0 and the default-output change.
- The tolerances of FR-005 – FR-007 are engineering figures stated against the model's own templates and the datasheet's figures; they bound the model, not the device.
- No release has been published; the frozen-decoder rule of spec 008 is untouched because the stored form does not change.
