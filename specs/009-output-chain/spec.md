# Feature Specification: Output Chain (volume and power amplifier, speaker, earphone; stages 11–13)

**Feature Branch**: `009-output-chain`

**Created**: 2026-10-14

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description (abridged; the full text is in the specify session's transcript): "Output chain: model signal-chain stages 11, 12 and 13 of the RR-DR60 playback path, behind the speaker/earphone route that spec 006 reserved as a label. Stage 11 is the volume control and power amplifier (50 kΩ volume control, NJM2113 bridge-tied-load amplifier, about 16 dB of gain, clips near 1.0–1.25 V at the 3 V supply, muted while recording; S-002, S-006, A-031). Stage 12 is the 2.8 cm 8 Ω speaker in its tiny enclosure, whose acoustic response is unsourced and must be a registered low-confidence assumption with its own bypass (A-010), so every published figure of this feature is quoted on the earphone route. Stage 13 is the earphone output, an emitter follower from the volume control that bypasses the power amplifier, two mild first-order high-pass corners near 160 Hz and 33 Hz, no clipping (A-032). A volume setting as the device's own control; each stage bypassable with a tap after each; the earlier specs' checks run with stages 11–13 bypassed; the speaker route is the device's default output and the earphone route the measurement reference. Two questions the spec must settle: the output reference level once the signal has left the codec's full scale, and whether the one-pass processing runs through the selected route."

## Overview

The emulator's playback path ends today at the codec's output filter (stage 10). What the RR-DR60 does to that signal before anyone hears it is not modeled: the volume control and the small power amplifier that drives the speaker, the speaker itself, and the separate earphone output that bypasses the amplifier. Spec 006 made the **route**, speaker or earphone, a selectable, readable control, and documented it as a label: selecting a route changes nothing in the sound. This feature gives the route its sound.

The service manual's schematic ([S-002](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)) shows the playback chain after the codec: the codec output feeds a 50 kΩ volume control; from the volume control one branch goes through coupling components into an NJM2113 bridge-tied-load amplifier ([S-006](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)) with a gain of about 16 dB set by two resistors, which drives a 2.8 cm 8 Ω speaker and clips near its documented output swing at the 3 V supply; the other branch goes through an emitter follower and a series resistor to the 2.5 mm earphone jack, bypassing the amplifier, with two mild high-pass corners from its coupling capacitors. The amplifier is muted while recording. The registered assumptions [A-031](../../docs/hardware/assumptions.md) (volume and amplifier: topology and gain high, clip level medium), [A-032](../../docs/hardware/assumptions.md) (earphone: topology high, corner values medium) and [A-010](../../docs/hardware/assumptions.md) (speaker: part high, acoustic response low) already describe the three stages; this feature adopts them as modeled behaviour and registers what they still leave open.

The three stages differ in how much is known, and the feature says so. Stage 11 and stage 13 are modeled on a circuit and a datasheet: their gain, clip level and corner frequencies are derived figures with stated confidence. Stage 12, the speaker, is the least sourced stage in the chain: a part number and a power rating are sourced, its acoustic response is not. It is modeled as a registered **low-confidence template** (a small driver's bass roll-off and a resonance in the high hundreds of hertz) with its own bypass, so that the stage manifest can carry an honest "assumed, low" badge, so that a host can switch the guess off and keep the sourced stages, and so that every figure this feature publishes is quoted on the **earphone route**, the electrical output that the field-measurement note ([docs/hardware/field-measurement.md](../../docs/hardware/field-measurement.md)) identifies as the cleanest return from a real unit. The speaker-over-earphone ratio of that note is the capture that would test stages 11 and 12 against a unit.

Two decisions the feature description asked the spec to settle, rather than leave to the plan, are settled below and recorded under Clarifications: the **output reference level** (what a full-scale output sample means once the signal has passed the amplifier, where the codec's full scale no longer applies), and that the **one-pass processing runs through the selected route** as record-then-play does, so the two stay the same chain. Nothing already checked changes: the earlier specs' checks run with stages 11–13 bypassed, as spec 008's run with the codec bypassed, so the four golden files hold; the output chain gets its own golden file. The default output does change, because the device plays through its speaker, and the CHANGELOG records that as the adoption of A-031, A-032 and A-010.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-10-14 (specify: the two questions the description asked the spec to settle)

- Q: What does a full-scale output sample mean after the amplifier and the speaker, where the codec's full scale (A-030) no longer applies? → A: **Each route's output is scaled to that route's own electrical full swing** (FR-004, A-042): on the speaker route, 1.0 is the amplifier's clip level (the signal that would reach the speaker, scaled so the amplifier's output swing is full scale; the speaker stage is normalized to unity gain at 1 kHz on top of it, so its resonance can exceed full scale by its stated peak gain); on the earphone route, 1.0 is the volume control's output at full scale (the signal that reaches the earphone, with the earphone path's fixed series attenuation and the earphone's own sensitivity treated as loudness constants outside the model). Consequence, stated in the README: at the default volume position a full-scale recording reaches full scale on the speaker route, just at the clip, and sits 16 dB lower on the earphone route (the amplifier's gain, which the earphone path does not have); the relative loudness of the two transducers is **not** modeled, and a host sets its own playback gain per route. The alternative (one shared scale for both routes, letting the earphone route exceed full scale by the amplifier's gain at high volume) was rejected because a host writing to integer buffers would clip the one route the device does not clip.
- Q: Does the one-pass processing run through the selected route, as record-then-play does? → A: **Yes** (FR-010). The one-pass processing is the whole chain, microphone to the selected route's output, with the route and volume controls shared with the playback path; its default output becomes the speaker route's output; the signal before the output chain (today's default output, after stage 10) stays available as a tap, as do the points after stage 11 on either route. Record-then-play and the one-pass stay bit-identical under spec 008's equivalence rule.
- Q: Is the volume a setting or a control? → A: **A control**, like the route (spec 006 FR-015): the device's volume is a knob turned at any time including during playback; it is set and read through a dedicated control, survives reset and reconfigure, takes effect at the next block, and is not a settings field. The three bypasses are settings.
- Q: What is the volume control's default position? → A: **The reference position: the position at which a full-scale codec output just reaches the amplifier's clip level** (FR-003, A-043), so the default output does not clip on full-scale material and the amplifier's clipping is something the host turns up into, as on the device. The alternative (maximum, where a full-scale recording clips by about 16 dB on the speaker route) is the device at full volume and is one setting away; the clarify session may revisit the default.
- Q: Which tap values does the output chain add, and what do they mean on each route? → A: The volume control belongs to stage 11 and is common to both routes; the amplifier is the speaker route's half of stage 11. So "after stage 11" is defined on both routes (volume control and, on the speaker route, the amplifier), "after stage 12" is the speaker route's output and "after stage 13" the earphone route's; the route's output is the default tap, and the signal before the output chain is the existing "after the codec output filter" point (FR-011).
- Q: Does the mute while recording become an audio effect? → A: **No change.** The record path produces no host-rate output and nothing plays during recording (spec 006 FR-003 reports the mute as state); the output chain adds no behaviour there. Documented, not modeled (FR-002).

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Hear the speaker and the earphone differ (Priority: P1)

An app developer plays a recording back through the speaker route and then through the earphone route, and hears two different outputs: the speaker route thin and resonant, with the amplifier's clipping when the volume is high; the earphone route the codec's sound with a gentle roll-off below about 160 Hz and no clipping. The developer turns the volume up and down and hears the level follow it on either route. A vintage-gear enthusiast does the same to hear how much of the device's playback character is the speaker and how much is everything before it, switching the speaker stage off to keep only the sourced stages.

**Why this priority**: this is the mission's "sound leaving the speaker": the last stage of the device's chain and the most audible missing character for both audiences. It is also the point at which the route selected since spec 006 first changes the sound.

**Independent Test**: can be fully tested with a fresh engine in the **output-chain-only configuration** (every earlier stage bypassed, FR-011) at each supported host rate, feeding sweeps, tones and level steps to the playback path or the one-pass processing: measure each route's response against FR-005 to FR-007, the clip curve against the volume position (FR-003, FR-006), the earphone's corners, the volume law, and the bypasses. The memory, export and the C API are not needed. P1 alone is a usable engine with the device's two outputs.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the output-chain-only configuration at 48 kHz and the earphone route, **When** a slow sweep from 20 Hz to 3 kHz at −20 dBFS is played, **Then** the response relative to 1 kHz is within the tolerances of FR-007 (about −3 dB at the 160 Hz corner, falling toward the 33 Hz corner, flat within 0.2 dB above 1 kHz), and the same input on the speaker route with the speaker stage bypassed shows the amplifier path's response of FR-006.
2. **Given** the speaker route at the default volume position, **When** a 1 kHz sine at full scale is played, **Then** the output peak reads full scale within the tolerance of FR-004 and the output is a sine within the distortion bound of FR-006 below the clip; **When** the volume is raised by 6 dB, **Then** the output peak still reads full scale within tolerance and the distortion rises above the clipping threshold of FR-006.
3. **Given** the speaker route with the speaker stage on, **When** the sweep is played, **Then** the response relative to 1 kHz matches the registered template of FR-005 within its tolerance (the bass roll-off and the resonance), and bypassing the speaker stage removes it.
4. **Given** either route, **When** the volume position is set to 0.5 then 0.25, **Then** the output level follows the volume law of FR-003 within 0.1 dB at each position, and at position 0 the output is digital silence from stage 11 on.
5. **Given** the earphone route, **When** a 1 kHz sine at full scale is played at the maximum volume position, **Then** the output peak reads the earphone route's full scale within tolerance and no sample is clipped (FR-007: the earphone path has no clipping).
6. **Given** the earphone route and the default volume, **When** a full-scale 1 kHz sine is played, **Then** the output reads 16 dB below full scale within the tolerance of FR-004 (the amplifier's gain the earphone path does not have), and the README states this.

### User Story 2 - The whole chain, the route, the bypasses and the earlier checks (Priority: P2)

The developer who already uses the engine finds that the one-pass processing and record-then-play both end at the selected route, that switching the route or turning the volume during playback takes effect at the next block, that the pre-chain signal is still available as a tap, and that switching the three new stages off returns the engine of version 0.8 bit for bit. Everything earlier measured still holds.

**Why this priority**: the output chain must be a continuation of the chain the previous eight features built, not a side path; the equivalence rule and the golden files are the proof.

**Independent Test**: with stages 11–13 bypassed, every existing harness check and the four golden files pass unchanged; with them on, the one-pass processing and record-then-play of the same stimulus are compared under spec 008's equivalence rule at the seven rates; route and volume changes mid-playback are checked for their block timing; the output chain gets its own golden file.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the default configuration (every stage on, speaker route, default volume), **When** a stimulus is recorded, stopped and played, and the same stimulus is run through the one-pass processing with the same route and volume, **Then** the playback is bit-identical to the one-pass output under spec 008 FR-009's rule at all seven host rates.
2. **Given** stages 11–13 bypassed, **When** the four golden files and every pre-009 harness check run, **Then** they pass unchanged and the four SHA guards are unmoved.
3. **Given** a playback in progress on the speaker route, **When** the host selects the earphone route, **Then** the output from the next block on is the earphone route's and the earphone stage starts from its rest state (FR-009); **When** the host sets the volume, **Then** the next block carries the new gain.
4. **Given** the default configuration, **When** the host selects the tap before the output chain, **Then** the one-pass output equals version 0.8's default output bit for bit (the chain before stage 11 is unchanged).
5. **Given** any configuration, **When** the latency and the per-block work are read, **Then** the reported latencies are unchanged by the output chain (FR-012) and the cost is within the budget of SC-007.

### User Story 4 - The same from C (Priority: P4)

A Swift or C developer selects the route, sets and reads the volume, switches the three stages, selects the new taps and plays back through the C API with the same meaning and the same figures; nothing panics across the boundary.

**Why this priority**: the C API is the integration surface for the iOS app, which exposes a volume control and the route in its front view (product notes: front-and-inside.md).

**Independent Test**: the C smoke loop plays a representable stimulus through each route and compares against the Rust path's output through a pinned hash; the settings layout change is checked; the new control functions are exercised with valid and invalid positions.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the C API, **When** a C caller sets the volume to a valid position and reads it back, **Then** the value round-trips; **When** it sets a position outside 0–1 or a non-finite value, **Then** the call is refused with a distinct status and the position is unchanged.
2. **Given** the C settings structure, **When** a 0.8 caller passes the old structure size, **Then** it is refused as spec 006 and 008 refuse old sizes; the new fields sit at the recorded offsets.
3. **Given** the C smoke loop, **When** it plays the exactly representable stimulus through the speaker route and the earphone route, **Then** both outputs match the Rust path's pinned hashes.

### Edge Cases

- **Volume position 0.** The output of stage 11 and everything after it is digital silence on both routes (the pot at its end), and the taps before stage 11 are unaffected.
- **Volume position out of range or not finite.** Refused with a reason; the previous position stays.
- **Volume changed mid-block.** The new position applies from the next block; the engine MAY ramp within that block but no output sample may exceed what the larger of the two gains would give (no overshoot from the change itself).
- **Route changed mid-playback.** Takes effect at the next block (spec 006 FR-015); the newly selected route's stages start from their rest state; the stages of the route just left are reset so a later return also starts from rest.
- **Output above full scale.** On the speaker route the clipper bounds the amplifier's output at full scale and the speaker template's resonance may then exceed it by at most the template's stated peak gain; on the earphone route the output can exceed full scale only if the input to the chain does (the playback filter's reconstruction overshoot, spec 005) scaled by the volume position, which never exceeds unity. Documented, not clamped.
- **DC and very low frequencies.** The playback filter passes DC (spec 004); the amplifier path's input coupling and the earphone path's corners block it, so neither route's output carries DC with the stages on. With stage 11 bypassed on the speaker route the speaker template still blocks it; with everything bypassed the DC passes as in 0.8.
- **The 6 kHz host rate.** The corners near 33 Hz and 160 Hz and the speaker template are well inside the band at every supported rate; the chain runs at the host rate after the resampler, so the templates are realized at each rate and must match their stated response at each rate within the same tolerance.
- **The after-stage-11 tap on the earphone route.** Defined: the volume control's output (the amplifier is not on that route).
- **Taps after stage 12 or 13 with the other route selected.** Refused at the processing call with a distinct reason ("tap is not on the selected route"); the default tap always means the selected route's output.
- **Bypassed chain.** With stages 11–13 bypassed the route is again a label, as in 0.8; the documentation says a bypassed output chain is not a device configuration.
- **Recording.** Nothing plays; the mute is reported state (spec 006); the output chain holds no state across a recording and a later playback starts from rest.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**Stages 11, 12 and 13**

- **FR-001**: The engine MUST model signal-chain stage 11 as the device's **volume control and power amplifier**, stage 12 as its **speaker**, and stage 13 as its **earphone output**, at the end of the playback path after the codec output filter (stage 10) and in the one-pass processing after the same point, arranged as the service manual's schematic shows (S-002, A-031, A-032): the volume control common to both routes; from it, on the speaker route, the amplifier's input coupling, the amplifier with its gain and its clipping, then the speaker; on the earphone route, the earphone path's two corners with no clipping. Which branch runs is the selected route (spec 006 FR-015); the routes are exclusive.
- **FR-002**: The three stages MUST be **on by default** (the device plays through its speaker) and **independently bypassable** by three settings; the mute while recording stays reported state (spec 006 FR-003) and adds no audio behaviour, because nothing plays during recording. Turning the chain on by default is a user-visible change (constitution Principle II) recorded in the CHANGELOG as the adoption of A-031 and A-032 and the revision of A-010. The documentation MUST say that a bypassed output chain is a combination the device cannot produce.

**The volume control and the output scale**

- **FR-003**: **Volume.** The host MUST be able to set and read the **volume position**, a number from 0 (silence) to 1 (maximum), through a dedicated control that is not part of the settings, at any time including during playback, taking effect at the next block, surviving reset and reconfigure; positions outside 0–1 or not finite MUST be refused with a reason. The control's law MUST be **linear in voltage** (the schematic names a 50 kΩ "B"-taper control, S-002; A-031 extended), so the gain at position *p* is *p* times the gain at 1, within 0.1 dB at every position above 0.01. The **default position** MUST be the **reference position**: the position at which a full-scale codec output just reaches the amplifier's clip level on the speaker route (about 1 ÷ the amplifier's voltage gain, 0.15 for 16 dB; A-043, the exact figure the plan's), so the default output does not clip on full-scale material.
- **FR-004**: **Output scale** (A-042). Each route's output MUST be scaled to **that route's electrical full swing**: on the speaker route, a sample of 1.0 is the amplifier's clip level (the signal that reaches the speaker, with the amplifier's output swing as full scale; the speaker stage then applies its template normalized to unity gain at 1 kHz); on the earphone route, a sample of 1.0 is the volume control's output at full scale (the signal that reaches the earphone; the earphone path's fixed series attenuation into its 16 Ω load and the earphone's sensitivity are loudness constants outside the model, stated in A-042). Consequence to be asserted: with every earlier stage bypassed, a full-scale 1 kHz sine at the default volume reads a peak of 1.0 within −1.0 … 0 dB on the speaker route with the speaker stage bypassed (just at the clip) and −16 dB ± 0.5 dB relative to full scale on the earphone route (the amplifier's gain the earphone path lacks); at the maximum volume the earphone route reads full scale within ±0.1 dB. The relative loudness of the two transducers is not modeled, and the README MUST say that a host sets its own playback gain per route.

**Measurable properties**

- **FR-005**: **Speaker template** (stage 12, A-010 revised). The speaker MUST be modeled as a registered **response template** of a small enclosed driver: a second-order bass roll-off with a resonance whose centre lies in the **high hundreds of hertz** (the plan picks the centre, the resonance's height and the slope within the ranges A-010 registers; the register records the chosen figures and their low confidence) normalized to unity gain at 1 kHz, with no response modeled above the codec's band (the playback filter has already removed it), **no nonlinearity** of the driver (the audible distortion at high volume is attributed to the amplifier's clipping, FR-006, not to the speaker), and no enclosure or room acoustics. The template's measured response MUST match its registered figures within **±0.5 dB** from 100 Hz to 2.5 kHz at every supported host rate, and the template's peak gain above unity MUST be stated and at most **+6 dB** (engineering bound on how far the speaker route's output may exceed full scale). The documentation MUST describe these as the model's template, never as a measured driver.
- **FR-006**: **Amplifier path** (stage 11 on the speaker route, A-031 adopted and extended). The amplifier path MUST have a voltage gain of **16 dB ± 0.5 dB** at 1 kHz (2 × 220 ÷ 68 from the schematic's resistors and the datasheet's gain formula, S-002, S-006), applied after the volume control; an **input coupling corner** derived by the plan from the schematic's coupling components (A-031 extended; stated with its confidence), with the passband above twice the corner flat within 0.2 dB up to 2.5 kHz; and a **clipper** at the amplifier's full swing (the output scale's 1.0, FR-004) with a shape the plan picks and the register records (the datasheet gives 0.5 % distortion typical below clipping and 10 % at the rated output, S-006): a sine whose level would reach 6 dB above the clip MUST produce an output whose peak is 1.0 within **±0.1 dB** and whose total harmonic distortion is at least **10 %**, and a sine at 6 dB below the clip MUST produce distortion at most **1 %** (engineering figures bracketing the datasheet's). The gain, the corner and the clip shape are design constants cited to A-031.
- **FR-007**: **Earphone path** (stage 13, A-032 adopted). The earphone path MUST apply two first-order high-pass corners at **160 Hz** and **33 Hz** (the schematic's coupling components into the follower and into the 16 Ω load; A-032: corner values medium), each within **±10 %** of its nominal frequency as measured by the −3 dB point of that corner alone, unity gain in the passband (the follower's gain and the series attenuation are the loudness constants of FR-004), the passband above 1 kHz flat within **0.2 dB** up to 2.5 kHz, and **no clipping**: the output is the input scaled by the volume position and filtered, so a full-scale input at maximum volume passes with a peak of 1.0 within ±0.1 dB and distortion below **0.1 %**.
- **FR-008**: **Latency and phase.** The output chain MUST add **no reported latency** on either route: its stages are minimum-phase and memoryless or low-order, and their group delay is not counted, as spec 004 does not count the band-limit stages' edge group delay; the reported one-pass, record and playback latencies MUST be unchanged by the chain. The chain MUST run at the host rate after the resampler (like the limiter runs at the host rate before it), and each template MUST meet its stated response at every supported host rate.

**The chain, the route, the taps and the earlier checks**

- **FR-009**: **Route and volume changes.** A route change MUST take effect at the next block (spec 006 FR-015) with the newly selected route's stages starting from their rest state and the stages of the route left reset; a volume change MUST take effect at the next block, and no output sample during the change MAY exceed the value the larger of the two gains would give. Both controls are shared between the playback path and the one-pass processing, which read them at each block.
- **FR-010**: **The one-pass processing runs through the route.** The one-pass processing MUST end at the selected route's output with the same volume, so that spec 008 FR-009's equivalence between record-then-play and the one-pass holds bit for bit with the output chain on at all seven host rates, and so that a host hears the same chain whichever way it feeds the engine. The one-pass processing's **default output becomes the speaker route's output**; its 0.8 default output (after stage 10) stays available as a tap (FR-011).
- **FR-011**: **Taps and configurations.** The tap "after the codec output filter" (stage 10, before the output chain, version 0.8's default output) MUST be a named tap; "after stage 11" MUST be a tap defined on both routes (the volume control's output, with the amplifier's on the speaker route); the selected route's output MUST be the default tap, named for the stage it is ("after the speaker" or "after the earphone"); selecting a route-specific tap while the other route is selected MUST be refused with a distinct reason. The **output-chain-only** configuration (every earlier stage bypassed, each route) MUST be a supported harness case so that each stage's figures are attributed to it alone (constitution Principle VII).
- **FR-012**: **Determinism and real-time safety.** The three stages MUST produce bit-identical output on every supported platform and for every block partition (constitution Principle IV; the golden matrix and the determinism tests extend to the chain); the per-block playback and one-pass calls and the volume and route controls MUST NOT allocate, lock, perform I/O or do unbounded work; the chain's cost MUST stay within SC-007's budget.
- **FR-013**: **Memory and stored form unchanged.** The output chain sits after the decoder: the memory's accounting, the stored form (version 2) and export and import MUST be unchanged, and a recording made before this feature MUST play back through the chain unchanged in its stored content.

**Interfaces, registers and documentation**

- **FR-014**: **C API.** Every capability of FR-001 – FR-013 MUST be reachable through the C API with the same meaning: the three bypass settings in the settings structure (the only layout change, recorded in the CHANGELOG with the new size and offsets; an old size refused as before), the volume control as set-and-read functions with a distinct status for a refused position, the new tap values, the route-tap refusal as a distinct status; no Rust-only types, no panic across the boundary; the header regenerated and drift-checked; the C smoke loop extended to both routes with pinned hashes of an exactly representable stimulus.
- **FR-015**: **Manifest and registers.** The manifest's stage 11, 12 and 13 entries MUST read status "assumed" with confidence **medium**, **low** and **medium** (bounded by A-031's clip level, A-010's response and A-032's corner values), model version equal to this feature's library version, coverage notes naming what each stage leaves out (stage 11: the clip shape's transistor-level detail and the supply-voltage dependence of the clip level; stage 12: the driver's nonlinearity, the enclosure and any room; stage 13: the load dependence of the corner into other earphones) and controls notes naming the route, the volume control, the bypass settings and the taps; the header counts MUST read 5 not modeled / 10 assumed; the signal-chain rows 11–13 MUST carry the "Modeled by" marker. The registers MUST carry A-031 and A-032 as **adopted** (A-031 extended with the coupling corner, the linear taper and the clip shape), A-010 **revised** to the template's chosen figures with low confidence, and two new assumptions, **A-042** (the output scale per route and the loudness constants outside it) and **A-043** (the volume control's default position), each with rationale, confidence and the capture that would test it (the field-measurement note's speaker-over-earphone ratio for stages 11–12; the earphone return's low corners for stage 13).
- **FR-016**: **Golden files.** The four existing golden files MUST stay byte-identical and their SHA guards unmoved; the earlier specs' harness checks run with stages 11–13 bypassed. The output chain MUST get its own golden file (stimuli × routes × volume positions × the seven host rates, including the output-chain-only and default configurations), blessed once at the end of implementation.
- **FR-017**: **Documentation.** The README MUST gain an output-chain section (what each stage does and on what basis, the three confidence levels, the figures of FR-004 – FR-007 as reported with the earphone route's and the speaker route's figures separately labelled, the output scale rule and the 16 dB consequence, the volume default and why, the bypasses and their "not the device" caveat, the field-measurement capture that would test the stages); the CHANGELOG MUST record the new stages, the default-output change, the C layout change, the register changes and the version bump to **0.9.0**; the field-measurement note's table row for the output chain MUST cite the new assumptions.
- **FR-018**: **Wording.** Every string and document this feature adds MUST follow the claims policy and spec 007's denylist: "modeled on the circuit and the datasheet", "a template of a small speaker", never "measured" of the device, "the DR60's speaker", "accurate" or "verified"; the speaker's figures are the model's template; no statement about what recordings contain.

### Out of Scope

The speaker's own nonlinearity, power compression and enclosure resonances beyond the one registered template (A-010); the absolute loudness or sound-pressure level of either output; the earphone path's behaviour into loads other than 16 Ω (S-002); the supply-voltage dependence of the amplifier's clip level (3 V nominal fixed; the plan may register the range); the clip level's temperature or battery-age drift; live monitoring during recording (product decision 2026-10-11); the playback speed control and the 10-second review (S-001); per-stage meters (E-A); any app UI; measuring a real unit (the field-measurement note says how).

### Key Entities

- **Route** (revised from spec 006): speaker or earphone, exclusive, a set-and-read control; from this feature on it selects which branch of the output chain runs and which output scale applies.
- **Volume position**: a number 0–1, a set-and-read control, linear in voltage, default at the reference position (A-043); common to both routes.
- **Output scale**: per route, the meaning of a full-scale sample (A-042): the amplifier's clip level on the speaker route; the volume control's full swing on the earphone route.
- **Stage 11**: the volume control (both routes) and the amplifier path (speaker route: input coupling corner, 16 dB gain, clipper at full scale); bypassable; design constants cited to A-031.
- **Stage 12**: the speaker template (bass roll-off with a resonance, unity at 1 kHz, stated peak gain); bypassable; cited to A-010 (revised, low).
- **Stage 13**: the earphone path (two first-order corners at 160 Hz and 33 Hz, unity passband, no clipping); bypassable; cited to A-032.
- **Taps** (revised): after the codec output filter (before the output chain; 0.8's default output), after stage 11 (both routes), after the speaker or after the earphone (the selected route's output; the default).
- **Configurations** (added): output-chain-only per route; the default configuration gains the chain.
- **Refusal reasons** (added): volume position out of range or not finite; tap not on the selected route.

## Assumptions

- **A-031** (adopted and extended by this feature): the volume control is linear in voltage (the schematic's "B" taper, S-002; medium confidence in the taper reading), the amplifier's gain is 16 dB from the schematic's resistors and the datasheet's formula (high), the clip level is the datasheet's output swing at 3 V (medium), the clip's shape is a design choice bracketed by the datasheet's distortion figures (low), and the amplifier path's input coupling corner is derived from the schematic's components (medium). Verify: a capture at the speaker terminals against the volume position (the field-measurement note's speaker-over-earphone ratio).
- **A-032** (adopted): the earphone path's two corners at about 160 Hz and 33 Hz from the schematic's components into a 16 Ω load (medium) with no clipping (high). Verify: the earphone return of the field-measurement loop below 500 Hz.
- **A-010** (revised): the speaker's acoustic response is a **template**, not a measurement: a small enclosed driver's second-order bass roll-off with a resonance in the high hundreds of hertz, figures chosen by the plan within the ranges the register states and recorded there; low confidence. Verify: an acoustic capture of a real unit, or the field loop's speaker-over-earphone ratio with its room residual.
- **A-042** (new): the **output scale per route**: the speaker route's full scale is the amplifier's clip level, the earphone route's is the volume control's full swing; the earphone path's series attenuation into 16 Ω (about 30 dB) and both transducers' sensitivities are loudness constants outside the model. Not a hardware fact but an engine convention, like A-030's mapping of 0 dBFS to +3 dBm0; registered so hosts and later features share it.
- **A-043** (new): the **volume control's default position** is the reference position at which a full-scale codec output just reaches the clip (about 0.15 for 16 dB). The device has no default: its knob sits wherever the user left it; the choice keeps the default output unclipped on full-scale material. Low confidence as a "typical" setting; the clarify session may revisit.
