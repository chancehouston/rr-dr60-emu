# Feature Specification: Output Chain (volume and power amplifier, speaker, earphone; stages 11–13)

**Feature Branch**: `009-output-chain`

**Created**: 2026-10-14

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description (abridged; the full text is in the specify session's transcript): "Output chain: model signal-chain stages 11, 12 and 13 of the RR-DR60 playback path, behind the speaker/earphone route that spec 006 reserved as a label. Stage 11 is the volume control and power amplifier (50 kΩ volume control, NJM2113 bridge-tied-load amplifier, muted while recording; S-002, S-006, A-031). Stage 12 is the 2.8 cm 8 Ω speaker in its tiny enclosure, whose acoustic response is unsourced and must be a registered low-confidence assumption with its own bypass (A-010), so every published figure of this feature is quoted on the earphone route. Stage 13 is the earphone output, an emitter follower that bypasses the power amplifier, two first-order high-pass corners (A-032). A volume setting as the device's own control; each stage bypassable with a tap after each; the earlier specs' checks run with stages 11–13 bypassed; the speaker route is the device's default output and the earphone route the measurement reference. Two questions the spec must settle: the output reference level once the signal has left the codec's full scale, and whether the one-pass processing runs through the selected route."

## Overview

The emulator's playback path ends today at the codec's output filter (stage 10). What the RR-DR60 does to that signal before anyone hears it is not modeled: the volume control and the small power amplifier that drives the speaker, the speaker itself, and the separate earphone output that bypasses the amplifier. Spec 006 made the **route**, speaker or earphone, a selectable, readable control, and documented it as a label: selecting a route changes nothing in the sound. This feature gives the route its sound.

The service manual's schematic ([S-002](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)) shows the playback chain after the codec: the codec output feeds a 50 kΩ linear volume control; from the volume control one branch goes through coupling components and a series resistor into an NJM2113 bridge-tied-load amplifier ([S-006](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)) whose gain is set by two resistors, which drives a 2.8 cm 8 Ω speaker and clips at the output swing its 3 V supply allows (the datasheet's rated 55 mW into 16 Ω and the manual's 110 mW into 8 Ω both put that swing at about 1.3 V peak at 10 % distortion); the other branch goes through an emitter follower and a series resistor to the 2.5 mm earphone jack, bypassing the amplifier, with two mild high-pass corners from its coupling capacitors. The amplifier is muted while recording. The registered assumptions [A-031](../../docs/hardware/assumptions.md) (volume and amplifier), [A-032](../../docs/hardware/assumptions.md) (earphone) and [A-010](../../docs/hardware/assumptions.md) (speaker) already describe the three stages; this feature adopts them as modeled behaviour, corrects what the draft reviews found misread in them (the clip level, the gain, one corner), and registers what they still leave open.

The three stages differ in how much is known, and the feature says so. Stage 11 and stage 13 are modeled on a circuit and a datasheet: their gain, clip level and corner frequencies are derived figures with stated confidence (the schematic sheet was re-read at plan time; the earphone's two corners depend on a transistor parameter and are registered as ranges). Stage 12, the speaker, is the least sourced stage in the chain: a part number and a power rating are sourced, its acoustic response is not. It is modeled as a registered **low-confidence template** (a small enclosed driver's bass roll-off, its resonance, and the rising tilt of a small case) with its own bypass, so that the stage manifest can carry an honest "assumed, low" badge, so that a host can switch the guess off and keep the sourced stages, and so that every figure this feature publishes is quoted on the **earphone route**, the electrical output that the field-measurement note ([docs/hardware/field-measurement.md](../../docs/hardware/field-measurement.md)) identifies as the cleanest return from a real unit. The speaker-over-earphone ratio of that note is the capture that would test stages 11 and 12 against a unit.

One honesty point about the corners belongs up front: the volume control's 32 Hz coupling corner lies well **below the codec's 225 Hz record band edge** and is a DC blocker on a recording the device itself made; the amplifier's 159 Hz corner trims the band's bottom by about 1 dB at 300 Hz; the earphone's two corners (about 120 and 280 Hz, both uncertain with the follower transistor's current gain) sit at the band's bottom edge and thin the earphone route's low end. So on device-made recordings the audible output chain is the volume, the amplifier's clipping, the speaker template, and the earphone's roll-off; the 32 Hz corner matters only on imported or codec-bypassed material. The README says so.

Two decisions the feature description asked the spec to settle, rather than leave to the plan, are settled below and recorded under Clarifications: the **output reference level** (what a full-scale output sample means once the signal has passed the amplifier, where the codec's full scale no longer applies), and that the **one-pass processing runs through the selected route** as record-then-play does, so the two stay the same chain. Nothing already checked changes: the earlier specs' checks run with stages 11–13 bypassed, as spec 008's run with the codec bypassed, so the four golden files hold; the output chain gets its own golden file. The default output does change, because the device plays through its speaker, and the CHANGELOG records that as the adoption of A-031, A-032 and A-010.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-10-14 (specify: the two questions the description asked the spec to settle)

- Q: What does a full-scale output sample mean after the amplifier and the speaker, where the codec's full scale (A-030) no longer applies? → A: **Each route's output is scaled to that route's own electrical full swing** (FR-004, A-042): on the speaker route, 1.0 is the amplifier's clip ceiling (the signal that reaches the speaker, scaled so the amplifier's output swing is full scale; the speaker template is normalized to a peak gain of 1.0 on top of it, so the speaker route never exceeds full scale (superseded at implementation: steady tones below the knee only; see the 2026-10-15 session)); on the earphone route, 1.0 is the volume control's full swing, which is the codec's full scale (1.0 V peak, A-030) at position 1 (the follower's drive into its 16 Ω load and the earphone's own sensitivity are loudness constants outside the model). Consequence, stated in the README: at the default volume position a full-scale recording reaches full scale on the speaker route, just at the clip, and sits lower on the earphone route by the loaded law's figure (about 18.5 dB after the plan session; this entry's earlier figures, 12.5 dB and a 30 dB series attenuation, are superseded there); the relative loudness of the two transducers is **not** modeled, and a host sets its own playback gain per route. The alternative (one shared scale for both routes, letting the earphone route exceed full scale by the amplifier's gain at high volume) was rejected because a host writing to integer buffers would clip the one route the device does not clip.
- Q: Does the one-pass processing run through the selected route, as record-then-play does? → A: **Yes** (FR-010). The one-pass processing is the whole chain, microphone to the selected route's output, with the route and volume controls shared with the playback path; its default output becomes the speaker route's output; the signal before the output chain (today's default output, after stage 10) stays available as a tap, as do the points after stage 11 on either route. Record-then-play and the one-pass stay bit-identical under spec 008's equivalence rule, with the precondition FR-010 states.
- Q: Is the volume a setting or a control? → A: **A control**, like the route (spec 006 FR-015): the device's volume is a knob turned at any time including during playback; it is set and read through a dedicated control, survives reset and reconfigure, takes effect at the next block, and is not a settings field. Alternative rejected: a settings field, because a reconfigure resets the paths and the knob turns mid-playback. The three bypasses are settings.
- Q: What is the volume control's default position? → A: **The reference position: the position at which a full-scale codec output just reaches the amplifier's clip ceiling** (FR-003, A-043): the clip ceiling divided by the amplifier's gain times the codec's full scale, about 0.24 with the figures of A-031 (the plan computes the exact value from the constants it registers). So the default output does not clip on full-scale material, and the amplifier's clipping is something the host turns up into. The README states that the device clips on full-scale material at any position above the reference, so typical listening volumes clip. The alternative (maximum, where a full-scale recording clips by the full gain, about 15 dB, on the speaker route) is the device at full volume and is one setting away; the clarify session may revisit the default.
- Q: Which tap values does the output chain add, and what do they mean on each route? → A: The volume control belongs to stage 11 and is common to both routes; the amplifier is the speaker route's half of stage 11. So "after stage 11" is defined on both routes (volume control and, on the speaker route, the amplifier), "after the speaker" is the speaker route's output and "after the earphone" the earphone route's; a route-independent **route output** tap, which resolves to whichever of the two the selected route ends in, is the default; the two named taps are also selectable and are refused when the other route is selected; the signal before the output chain is the existing "after the codec output filter" point (FR-011).
- Q: Does the mute while recording become an audio effect? → A: **No change.** The record path produces no host-rate output and nothing plays during recording (spec 006 FR-003 reports the mute as state); the output chain adds no behaviour there. Documented, not modeled (FR-002).

### Session 2026-10-14 (coach C1–C14 and hardware-emulation D1–D10 reviews of the draft)

The user's standing choice is the recommended option; both reviews were applied in full.

- Q (D1, C1): What is the amplifier's clip level, and what does the datasheet's "output voltage 1.18 V (1.00–1.25)" row mean? → A: That row is the output pins' **quiescent DC level** (≈ V+/2 − 0.3 V: 2.68 V at 6 V, 5.71 V at 12 V), not the swing; the draft, A-031 and the S-006 register row had misread it. The clip comes from the rated power: 55 mW into 16 Ω at 10 % distortion (S-006) and 110 mW into 8 Ω (S-002) both give 0.94 V RMS, **≈ 1.3 V peak differential at 10 % distortion**, with 1 % distortion setting in at about 1.15–1.25 V and a hard ceiling near 1.4 V from the output pins' swing limits (V+ − 1.1 V high, 0.21 V low). The spec states the clip ceiling as a constant the plan fixes (A-031, medium; 1.3 – 1.4 V at this session, 1.15 V at the 2.8 V rail the schematic marks (S-002) after the plan reviews), and **every figure of FR-003 and FR-004 is derived from that constant** (C1): the reference position, the earphone route's level at the default, the clip onset. The S-006 row and A-031 are corrected in the registers (FR-015). Open: which rail feeds the amplifier (3 V battery or the 3.3 V converter; the ceiling is ≈ 1.7 V on the latter), a plan-time re-read.
- Q (D2): Is the reference position 1 ÷ the gain, and is the earphone route 16 dB down at the default? → A: **No.** The reference position is the clip ceiling ÷ (gain × the codec's full scale of 1.0 V peak, A-030): about 1.33 ÷ 5.64 ≈ **0.24**, and the earphone route at the default reads 20·log₁₀(0.24) ≈ **−12.5 dB**, stated with ±1.5 dB for the plan's constants (both figures superseded by the plan session's re-read and loaded law: 0.13 and about −18.5 dB). Nine occurrences of "16 dB" corrected.
- Q (D3): Does the 10 kΩ series resistor into the inverting input add to the gain-setting resistor? → A: **Yes** (pin 4 is the inverting input; every series ohm from the source is part of Ri): gain = 2 × 220 ÷ (68 + 10) = 5.64, **15.0 dB** at position 1 (inferred from the component list; the placement is re-read with the schematic sheet in the plan). The volume control's wiper resistance also enters that resistor and would make the gain position-dependent; it is folded into the volume-law convention of A-044 (D4).
- Q (D4, C5): Is the volume law linear, and is it sourced? → A: The **taper** is sourced (the part is a Panasonic "B" taper: linear, high confidence); the **loaded law** is not linear: both branches hang on the wiper permanently (the routes' exclusivity is at the jack), so the control's output into its ≈ 26 kΩ load reads about 3.5 dB below the linear value at mid-travel. Decision: the engine's volume position is defined as **the fraction of the control's full-swing voltage reaching the chain**, not knob rotation, so the engine's law is exactly linear by definition and the loading is a registered simplification (**A-044**, new, medium): the mapping from knob angle to position is not modeled. The alternative, modeling the loaded law (memoryless, cheap), is recorded for a later feature if the field loop measures the knob. **Reversed at the plan review**: the loaded law is modeled (see the plan session).
- Q (D5): Is the earphone's upper corner 160 Hz? → A: **Probably not as written.** With the 10 kΩ resistor in series into the follower's base (bias 33 kΩ), the 0.1 µF capacitor sees about 40 kΩ: a **≈ 40 Hz** corner; 160 Hz needs that resistor as a shunt to ground, and the follower's emitter return is absent from the S-002 summary. Decision: A-032's corner values drop to **low** until sheet 3.1 is re-read (the resistor's role, the bias resistor's ends, the emitter resistor); the spec states the upper corner as **40–160 Hz, the plan's re-read fixing it**, and the lower corner as 33 Hz (verified: 10 µF into 470 + 16 + about 10 Ω = 32.7 Hz). FR-007's ±10 % is a tolerance on the model's own template (C12: measured as a composite fit, not two isolated −3 dB points).
- Q (D6): Where is the amplifier path's input coupling corner? → A: The two 0.1 µF capacitors in series (0.05 µF) into 78 kΩ give **≈ 41 Hz** (20 Hz if a bias return sits between them); the spec bounds it **below 100 Hz** and lets the plan fix it with its confidence (C6). Consequence recorded in the Overview: both routes' corners lie below the 225 Hz record band edge, so on device-made recordings stages 11 and 13 are essentially DC blockers.
- Q (D7, C3, C6): The speaker template: its normalization, its ranges, what it leaves out. → A: **Peak-normalized to a gain of 1.0** (so the speaker route never exceeds full scale (superseded at implementation: steady tones below the knee only; see the 2026-10-15 session), keeping A-042's promise to integer-buffer hosts (superseded at implementation: the speaker route can ring up to about 1.9 on clipped or transient material; see the 2026-10-15 session); the 1 kHz reading is then negative and stated), replacing "unity at 1 kHz with up to +6 dB over". Registered ranges (A-010 revised; the plan picks within them and records): resonance **600–900 Hz** with **Q 1.2–2.5** (a peak of +2 … +6 dB over the passband), second order below the resonance; **a first-order rising shelf of +3 … +6 dB from about 700 Hz toward 3 kHz** (the on-axis tilt of a 56 mm-wide case, whose baffle step sits near 2 kHz; this is much of the "thin" character); **flat above 2.5 kHz** (a 28 mm driver radiates well beyond the band, and the clipper now creates harmonics above the codec's band, so the choice is explicit); no driver nonlinearity, enclosure modes or room.
- Q (D8): Are the distortion criteria measurable at every host rate, and does the bracket discriminate? → A: **Not at 6, 8 and 16 kHz**: a 1 kHz tone's fifth and seventh harmonics alias onto the fundamental and the third and ninth onto the band edge. Distortion and peak figures are measured **at 44.1 kHz and above**; at the three low rates only the peak ceiling is asserted. The bracket is replaced: a hard clipper already gives 9 % at +2 dB over and 23 % at +6 dB, so "≥ 10 % at +6 dB" passed any shape; the new criteria are **10 % distortion reached between +1 and +3 dB over the ceiling, 15–30 % at +6 dB over, ≤ 1 % at 6 dB under** (the datasheet's 0.5 % at 20 mW into 8 Ω is 7.4 dB under the ceiling). The shape is **symmetric** (the high-side pin reaches its limit on both half-cycles of a bridge output) with a knee of about 1 dB of rounding; the plan picks a shape that uses no platform math function (constitution Principle IV). The README says that at 6–16 kHz host rates the clipped speaker route is band-limited by the host rate and is not the device's.
- Q (D9, C7, C8): Latency, group delay and the equivalence through filters with memory. → A: No reported latency, agreed, with the plan **constrained to minimum-phase recursive filters** (a linear-phase design would break FR-008). The chain's **group delay at 1 kHz is bounded at 0.5 ms per route and reported** (the resonance adds about 0.2–0.4 ms, 10–20 samples at 48 kHz, to the new default output); spec 004 FR-004's latency-equals-group-delay check is met with the chain bypassed; the README tells hosts aligning the default output to add the reported figure. The one-pass equivalence (FR-010) gains its precondition: the decoder's leading-delay output is exact digital zero, so the chain's state at the skip point is the rest state; the plan verifies this at all seven rates.
- Q (C2): Do the draft's tolerances survive its own corners? → A: **No**: a 160 Hz first-order corner reads −0.11 dB at 1 kHz and −0.97 dB at twice its frequency. The earphone level at 1 kHz is now **−0.25 … 0 dB** of full scale at maximum volume; the amplifier passband is flat within 0.2 dB **above five times** its corner; the two-corner earphone response is checked as a **composite** template within ±0.5 dB from 20 Hz to 2.5 kHz with the fitted corners within ±10 %.
- Q (C4): What does bypassing stage 11 mean? → A: The whole stage: volume control, coupling corner, gain and clipper off together, unity gain at the codec's scale (A-030); the volume position is retained and has no effect while bypassed. Alternative (amplifier only) rejected: a volume control without the stage it belongs to is not a device configuration either.
- Q (C9): "Position 0 gives digital silence" and "no sample exceeds the larger gain" through filters that ring. → A: Exact zeros are asserted at the **after-stage-11 tap** from the next block; at the route output the signal **decays below −120 dBFS within 50 ms and the state flushes to exactly zero**; the no-overshoot rule is scoped to the volume gain itself (the clipper and the templates downstream are not part of it).
- Q (C10): Is the default tap route-following? → A: Yes: a route-independent **route output** tap is the default and resolves to stage 12 or 13; the two named taps are also selectable and refused off-route.
- Q (C11): The clip's peak wording. → A: The speaker route's stage 11 output **never exceeds 1.0**; a sine 6 dB over the ceiling peaks at **≥ −0.1 dB**; the knee compresses a sine exactly at the ceiling by **at most 1 dB** (a bound on the shape the plan picks); FR-004's default-position reading follows from it.
- Applied without a question: US1 says "modeled as thin and resonant (A-010, low)" (C13); the S-006 register row's misreading, A-031's clip sentence and gain, A-032's confidence and the signal-chain rows' figures are listed for correction in FR-015 (D10); the checklist re-validated (C14). The hardware reviewer's "verify first on a real unit" list (earphone level against knob angle; a 20 Hz – 3 kHz sweep for the two low corners, where 40 against 160 Hz is a clean fourfold discriminator even through a phone input; the speaker terminals' peak where distortion passes 10 %) goes into the field-measurement note's table and the new assumptions' verification columns.

### Session 2026-10-14 (plan: the sheet 3.1 re-read and four plan-time amendments)

- Q (research R-02): What does S-002's analog sheet actually show for the playback path? → A: Read at plan time from the Internet Archive item's full-resolution scans (5100 × 5634 px). **The gain is 22.7 dB**, not 15 or 16: R18 68 kΩ is the feedback resistor from the output pin to the inverting input and R16 10 kΩ the series input resistor (2 × 68 ÷ 10 = 13.6); R19 220 kΩ with C16 10 pF is the microcontroller's beep injection into the same input, not the gain network. **The couplings**: C14 0.1 µF sits before the 50 kΩ volume control (a 32 Hz corner common to both routes); C8 0.1 µF into R16 at the inverting input gives the amplifier path a **159 Hz** corner. **The earphone branch is taken from the amplifier's input node** through C60 0.1 µF and R65 10 kΩ in series into the emitter follower's base (R64 33 kΩ to the 3.2 V analog rail), so it carries signal only while the amplifier is shut down (the microcontroller shuts it down through pin 1; a jack contact grounds R66's return and the microcontroller reads the plug); the emitter drives the tip through C61 10 µF; **R66 470 Ω is the emitter's DC return, not a series resistor**, so the draft's "30 dB series attenuation" and "33 Hz corner" were wrong and so were both readings of a "160 Hz or 40 Hz" corner. The earphone's two corners are the follower's **input coupling (C8 + C60 in series into 20 kΩ plus the follower's loaded input impedance: 120 Hz nominal, 65 – 130 Hz)** and its **output coupling (C61 into the 16 Ω load plus the follower's output resistance: 280 Hz nominal, 240 – 320 Hz)** at the transistor's marked rank (2SD1819A S, hFE 290 – 460; the hardware review of the plan moved the draft's 110 and 190 Hz), so A-032's corner values stay **low** confidence as ranges with nominal values. **The amplifier runs from the battery rail** (VDD read 2.8 V; the rail question is closed): at 2.8 V the pins' swing limits put the clip ceiling near **1.15 V peak** (1.33 V is the datasheet's 3.0 V figure; 1.0 – 1.4 V over the cells' state is registered). **The volume law is the circuit's loaded law** (the hardware review of the plan reversed the ideal-law convention: the control's wiper resistance adds to the amplifier's input resistor, and the ideal law would have put full-scale material 3 dB under the clip at the "reference" position; the position is the wiper fraction, the gains follow `p · 2 Rf / (Ri + 50 kΩ · p (1 − p))` on the speaker route and the branch's own divider on the earphone route, one divide per route per block; A-044 restated). Consequences: the reference position is about **0.13** and the earphone route at the default reads about **−18.5 dB** (FR-003, FR-004, US1 scenario 6, SC-004); FR-006 reads 22.7 dB at full travel with the 32 Hz and 159 Hz corners and the ceiling at 1.15 V; FR-007 reads the two earphone corners with their ranges and its level and flatness figures widened for the 280 Hz corner (−0.5 … 0 dB at 1 kHz, 0.3 dB flat above it); **the follower's class-A cut-off** (about 4 mA of quiescent current can sink only 50 – 75 mV of negative swing into 16 Ω, so a limiter-held recording is half-wave clipped above roughly 0.4 – 0.8 of the travel) is **not modeled** by this feature, registered in A-032 at low confidence and named in stage 13's "not modeled here" note; FR-005's tolerance is per rate (the Rust against the design's discrete realization within ±0.5 dB everywhere, the realization against the analog template within ±1.2 dB at 6 and 8 kHz and ±0.5 dB above, the bilinear warp near the band's top); the corners' −3 dB points are checked from the coefficients in core tests, not fitted from a measured response (three corners within two octaves are not separable); S-003 pages 4 – 17 (the codec's analog output level) are read before the golden is blessed, because 22.7 dB from a 1.0 V peak output puts full scale at the ceiling at 13 % of the travel, at the plausible edge; the "DC blockers on device-made recordings" note is softened: the 159 Hz corner trims the band's bottom by about 1 dB at 300 Hz, and the earphone's corners sit at the band's bottom edge, so stage 13 shapes device-made recordings too (Overview, Edge Cases, FR-015's coverage notes). The field-measurement note gains the point that a high-impedance recording input bypasses the 16 Ω-load corner, so the electrical return reads the input corner alone. The S-002 register row, A-031 and A-032 are corrected on the branch.
- Q (research R-11, coach C9): Can the route output reach −120 dBFS within 50 ms after the volume drops to 0? → A: The volume scalars are exactly zero from the next block; the sections after them (the speaker template on the speaker route, where the 159 Hz amplifier corner sits before the multiply; the earphone's 120 and 280 Hz corners on the earphone route) ring down below −120 dB in about 20 – 30 ms, while the 32 Hz corner before the multiply rings on but never reaches the output. The bound is restated as **100 ms** (a loose bound; FR-009, SC-004, Edge Cases) and the "flushes to exactly zero" wording goes: floating-point state flushes to exactly 0.0 only about 350 ms after the input stops (the sections' 1e-30 rule), which a core unit test proves on 400 ms of zeros; the drain and the harness never claim exact zeros at the output.
- Q (research R-08): Does the playback drain change? → A: **Yes**: the drain grows by the chain's settle length (about 58 ms: the 32 Hz corner's response below −100 dB of its peak, the rule spec 006 FR-008 uses for stage 10) whenever any output stage runs, so spec 006 FR-008's "to settled silence" and its last-millisecond check keep their meaning; the earlier specs' length checks run with the chain bypassed (FR-016).
- Q (research R-06): Does any existing tap change meaning? → A: **No**: `AfterPlayback` stays "after stage 10, before the output chain" (version 0.8's default output) and meets FR-011's "named tap" clause; a route-independent **route output** tap is the new default and resolves to the selected route's last stage; `AfterVolumeAmp`, `AfterSpeaker` and `AfterEarphone` are appended.

### Session 2026-10-15 (implementation: the design script's figures)

- Q: FR-007 and SC-001 bound the earphone passband above 1 kHz as flat within 0.3 dB up to 2.5 kHz; the design script's analytic composite of the three registered corners (32, 120 and 280 Hz nominal) is 0.33 dB (−0.39 dB at 1 kHz, −0.06 dB at 2.5 kHz). → A: The bound is amended to **0.4 dB**. The plan's 0.3 dB (research R-14, coach Q9) counted the 280 Hz corner alone (0.28 dB); the registered nominals are not moved to meet a tolerance that was set by that arithmetic (an exception to T014's rule that the fix is never in the bound, taken because the bound, not the realization, was in error). The 1 kHz level (−0.5 … 0 dB) and every other FR-005 – FR-008 bound hold at the seven rates (102 analytic checks). The script's figures replace the plan's provisional ones: `VOLUME_REFERENCE` 0.13345174 (−17.49 dB of travel; K_SPK unity within 3.6e-8 after the `f32` rounding), K_SPK(1) 11.83 (21.46 dB in the route's scale), the earphone level at the reference position −18.57 dB, the 6 dB-over position 0.36513, the knee's 10 % crossing +2.27 dB (1.48 % at the ceiling, 22.95 % at +6 dB), the template's peak 828 – 838 Hz with the 1 kHz reading −0.90 … −0.66 dB by rate and the discrete-vs-analog deviation 1.04 / 0.57 / 0.14 dB at 6 / 8 / 16 kHz, the 1 kHz group delays 0.31 – 0.33 ms (speaker) and 0.07 ms (earphone), `N_OUT` 346 / 461 / 922 / 2539 / 2764 / 5078 / 5527 (the 32 Hz section's state decaying by 100 dB: spec 006's −100 dB criterion applied to the pole's envelope, because after a sustained signal a one-pole high-pass's state is of the signal's order, not of its impulse response's later samples), the volume-0 decay 13.8 ms (speaker) and 18.4 ms (earphone).
- Q (US1 coach review T015, S1): FR-007, AS5 and SC-001 say the earphone route's full-scale sine at maximum volume "peaks" within −0.5 … 0 dB, never exceeding full scale; the harness reads the **steady-state AES17 amplitude after 0.2 s** and the steady-state sample ceiling. → A: Amended to say so. At 6 and 8 kHz a 1 kHz tone has 6 or 8 samples per cycle, so after the corners' 24° of phase no sample sits on the peak and a sample reading measures phase, not level (−1.0 dB at 6 kHz against an amplitude of −0.33 dB; spec 004's lesson); and a tone that starts abruptly rings the resampler pair by about 3 % at 16 kHz and above, in the chain's input, which the "output above full scale" edge case already covers. ## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Hear the speaker and the earphone differ (Priority: P1)

An app developer plays a recording back through the speaker route and then through the earphone route, and hears two different outputs: the speaker route modeled as thin and resonant (A-010, low confidence), with the amplifier's clipping when the volume is high; the earphone route the codec's sound with a roll-off below about 300 Hz (the follower's cut-off on loud material is not modeled by this feature). The developer turns the volume up and down and hears the level follow it on either route. A vintage-gear enthusiast does the same to hear how much of the device's playback character is the speaker and how much is everything before it, switching the speaker stage off to keep only the sourced stages.

**Why this priority**: this is the mission's "sound leaving the speaker": the last stage of the device's chain and the most audible missing character for both audiences. It is also the point at which the route selected since spec 006 first changes the sound.

**Independent Test**: can be fully tested with a fresh engine in the **output-chain-only configuration** (every earlier stage bypassed, FR-011) at each supported host rate, feeding sweeps, tones and level steps to the playback path or the one-pass processing: measure each route's response against FR-005 to FR-007, the clip curve against the volume position (FR-003, FR-006), the earphone's corners, the volume law, and the bypasses. The memory, export and the C API are not needed. P1 alone is a usable engine with the device's two outputs.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the output-chain-only configuration at 48 kHz and the earphone route, **When** the chain's response is measured (an impulse at −40 dBFS read by the DTFT at exact frequencies against the same path with the chain bypassed, research R-10), **Then** the response matches the earphone template of FR-007 within ±0.5 dB from 20 Hz to 2.5 kHz, and the same input on the speaker route with the speaker stage bypassed shows the amplifier path's response of FR-006 (the 32 Hz and 159 Hz corners and a flat passband).
2. **Given** the speaker route with the speaker stage bypassed, at the default volume position and 48 kHz, **When** a 1 kHz sine at full scale is played, **Then** the output peak reads full scale within the bound of FR-004 and the distortion is at most 1.5 % at 44.1 kHz and above (the knee's figure for a sine at the ceiling, FR-006; the design contract's table); **When** the volume is raised to the position at which the loaded law puts full-scale material 6 dB over the ceiling, **Then** the output peak still never exceeds full scale, reads at least −0.1 dB, and the distortion is between 15 % and 30 % (FR-006).
3. **Given** the speaker route with the speaker stage on, **When** the chain's response is measured the same way, **Then** the response matches the design's discrete realization of FR-005's template within ±0.5 dB from 100 Hz to 2.5 kHz (the bass roll-off, the resonance, the rising shelf), never exceeds a gain of 1.0, and bypassing the speaker stage removes it.

### User Story 4 - The same from C (Priority: P4)

A Swift or C developer selects the route, sets and reads the volume, switches the three stages, selects the new taps and plays back through the C API with the same meaning and the same figures; nothing panics across the boundary.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

2. **Given** the C settings structure, **When** a 0.8 caller passes the old structure size, **Then** it is refused as spec 006 and 008 refuse old sizes; the new fields sit at the recorded offsets.

### Edge Cases

- **Volume position 0.** The volume scalars are exactly zero from the next block (the control at its end), so every filter after them sees zeros and rings down: the route output falls below −120 dBFS within 100 ms (the floating-point state flushes to exactly zero much later, about 350 ms, a property a core test proves); the taps before stage 11 are unaffected.
- **Volume position out of range or not finite.** Refused with a reason; the previous position stays.
- **Volume changed mid-block.** The new position applies from the next block; the engine MAY ramp within that block but the volume gain itself MUST NOT overshoot either position's value (the clipper and the templates downstream are outside this rule).
- **Route changed mid-playback.** Takes effect at the next block (spec 006 FR-015); the newly selected route's stages start from their rest state; the stages of the route just left are reset so a later return also starts from rest.
- **Output above full scale.** On the speaker route the clipper bounds stage 11's output at full scale and the speaker template's gain is at most 1.0, so a **steady tone below the knee** never exceeds full scale after the template; a transient or a clipped signal can, because the template's resonance rings (its impulse response's L1 norm is above 1: a full-scale square wave, or a full-scale tone at position 1, reads about 1.9 after the template), as a real driver's cone overshoots; on the earphone route the output can exceed full scale only if the input to the chain does (the playback filter's reconstruction overshoot, spec 005) scaled by the volume position, which never exceeds unity. Documented, not clamped (the amendment at implementation: the draft claimed the speaker route never exceeds full scale, which holds for sinusoids only).
- **DC and very low frequencies.** The playback filter passes DC (spec 004); the amplifier path's input coupling and the earphone path's corners block it, so neither route's output carries DC with the stages on. With stage 11 bypassed on the speaker route the speaker template still blocks it; with everything bypassed the DC passes as in 0.8.
- **Audibility on device-made recordings.** The 32 Hz volume-control corner lies well below the 225 Hz record band edge and is a DC blocker on a recording the device made; the amplifier's 159 Hz corner trims the band's bottom by about 1 dB at 300 Hz; the earphone's corners near 120 and 280 Hz thin the earphone route's low end. Stated in the README and the manifest coverage notes.
- **Low host rates and clipping.** At 6, 8 and 16 kHz a clipped 1 kHz tone's harmonics alias within the host band, so distortion is measured at 44.1 kHz and above only; the clipped speaker route at the three low rates is band-limited by the host rate and is not the device's (README).
- **The after-stage-11 tap on the earphone route.** Defined: the volume control's output (the amplifier is not on that route).
- **Named route taps with the other route selected.** Refused at the processing call with a distinct reason ("tap is not on the selected route"); the route output tap always means the selected route's output.
- **Stage 11 bypassed.** The volume control, the coupling corner, the gain and the clipper are off together: unity gain at the codec's scale; the volume position is retained and ignored. A bypassed output chain is not a device configuration (documentation).
- **Recording.** Nothing plays; the mute is reported state (spec 006); the output chain holds no state across a recording and a later playback starts from rest.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**Stages 11, 12 and 13**

- **FR-001**: The engine MUST model signal-chain stage 11 as the device's **volume control and power amplifier**, stage 12 as its **speaker**, and stage 13 as its **earphone output**, at the end of the playback path after the codec output filter (stage 10) and in the one-pass processing after the same point, arranged as the service manual's schematic shows (S-002, A-031, A-032): the volume control common to both routes; from it, on the speaker route, the amplifier's input coupling, the amplifier with its gain and its clipping, then the speaker; on the earphone route, the earphone path's two corners with no clipper modeled (the branch is fed from the amplifier's input node while the amplifier is shut down, S-002; the follower's cut-off on loud material is registered, not modeled, A-032). Which branch runs is the selected route (spec 006 FR-015); the routes are exclusive.
- **FR-002**: The three stages MUST be **on by default** (the device plays through its speaker) and **independently bypassable** by three settings; bypassing stage 11 removes the volume control, the coupling corner, the gain and the clipper together (unity gain at the codec's scale, A-030; the volume position retained and without effect). The mute while recording stays reported state (spec 006 FR-003) and adds no audio behaviour, because nothing plays during recording. Turning the chain on by default is a user-visible change (constitution Principle II) recorded in the CHANGELOG as the adoption of A-031 and A-032 and the revision of A-010. The documentation MUST say that a bypassed output chain is a combination the device cannot produce.

**The volume control and the output scale**

- **FR-003**: **Volume.** The host MUST be able to set and read the **volume position**, a number from 0 (silence) to 1 (maximum), through a dedicated control that is not part of the settings, at any time including during playback, taking effect at the next block, surviving reset and reconfigure; positions outside 0–1 or not finite MUST be refused with a reason. The position is the control's **wiper fraction** (knob travel; the taper is linear, S-002), and the engine's gain on each route MUST follow the circuit's **loaded law** (A-044): on the speaker route *p* × 2 Rf ÷ (Ri + 50 kΩ · *p* (1 − *p*)) in volts per volt of codec output (the wiper's resistance adds to the amplifier's input resistor: 22.7 dB at position 1, 9.6 dB at 0.5), on the earphone route *p* × (20 kΩ + Z) ÷ (20 kΩ + Z + 50 kΩ · *p* (1 − *p*)) with Z the branch's registered input impedance (unity at position 1), each within 0.1 dB of the formula at every position above 0.01; the amplifier corner's shift with the wiper resistance is not modeled (under 1 dB at 300 Hz, A-044). The **default position** MUST be the **reference position** (A-043): the position at which the loaded law puts a full-scale codec output (1.0 V peak, A-030) at the clip ceiling, about **0.13** with A-031's figures (1.15 V), the exact value solved by the plan from the constants it registers, so the default output does not clip on full-scale material. The README MUST state that positions above the reference clip on full-scale material.
- **FR-004**: **Output scale** (A-042). Each route's output MUST be scaled to **that route's electrical full swing**: on the speaker route, a sample of 1.0 is the amplifier's **clip ceiling** (the constant of FR-006; the speaker template is then applied with a peak gain of 1.0, so a steady tone below the knee never exceeds full scale after it; a transient or a clipped signal can ring above it through the template's resonance, see Edge Cases); on the earphone route, a sample of 1.0 is the volume control's full swing, which is the codec's full scale at position 1 (the follower's drive into its 16 Ω load, about 15 – 24 dB below its input, and the earphone's sensitivity are loudness constants outside the model, stated in A-042). Consequences to be asserted with every earlier stage bypassed at 48 kHz: a full-scale 1 kHz sine at the default volume reads a peak within **−1.0 … 0 dB** of full scale on the speaker route with the speaker stage bypassed (just at the ceiling; the knee's compression of FR-006 bounds the reading), and **the loaded law's earphone figure at the reference position, about −18.5 dB, within ±1.5 dB** relative to full scale on the earphone route; at the maximum volume the earphone route reads **−0.5 … 0 dB** (the corners' residual at 1 kHz). The relative loudness of the two transducers is not modeled, and the README MUST say that a host sets its own playback gain per route.

**Measurable properties**

- **FR-005**: **Speaker template** (stage 12, A-010 revised). The speaker MUST be modeled as a registered **response template** of a small enclosed driver in a small case, whose parts and ranges A-010 registers and the plan narrows to chosen figures: a second-order bass roll-off with a resonance centred between **600 and 900 Hz** of **Q 1.2–2.5** (a peak of +2 … +6 dB over the passband below the shelf), a first-order rising shelf of **+3 … +6 dB** from about 700 Hz toward 2.5 kHz (the on-axis tilt of the 56 mm-wide case), **no further feature above 2.5 kHz** and the shelf's remaining rise there at most 0.7 dB (stated explicitly, because the clipper creates harmonics above the codec's band), the whole normalized to a **peak gain of 1.0 at each host rate** (the 1 kHz reading is negative, within 1 dB of the peak, and stated); **no nonlinearity** of the driver (the audible distortion at high volume is attributed to the amplifier's clipping, FR-006), no enclosure modes and no room. The template's measured response MUST match **the design's discrete realization** of the registered figures within **±0.5 dB** from 100 Hz to 2.5 kHz at every supported host rate, and the discrete realization MUST match the analog template within **±1.2 dB at 6 and 8 kHz and ±0.5 dB at 16 kHz and above** (the frequency warp of a discrete filter near the top of the band at the low rates; checked by the design tool and reported); its gain MUST never exceed 1.0. The documentation MUST describe these as the model's template, never as a measured driver.
- **FR-006**: **Amplifier path** (stage 11 on the speaker route, A-031 adopted and corrected). The amplifier path MUST have a voltage gain of **22.7 dB ± 0.5 dB** at 1 kHz at position 1 (2 × 68 ÷ 10 = 13.6: the 68 kΩ feedback resistor over the 10 kΩ series input resistor, read from the schematic sheet, and the datasheet's gain formula; S-002, S-006), applied after the volume control; two **input coupling corners**, one at **32 Hz** at the volume control's input (0.1 µF into the 50 kΩ control; common to both routes and so part of stage 11 on either) and one at **159 Hz** at the amplifier's input (0.1 µF into the 10 kΩ input resistor at the inverting input; speaker route only), each within ±10 % of its registered figure as computed from the realized section's coefficients (a core test; the corners are too close to fit from a measured response) and the composite response within ±0.5 dB (A-031; the gain's dependence on the wiper position is the loaded law of FR-003, the 159 Hz corner's shift with it is not modeled, A-044), with the passband above five times the upper corner flat within 0.2 dB up to 2.5 kHz; and a **clipper** at the amplifier's **clip ceiling**, a constant the plan fixes at about **1.15 V peak** differential at the 2.8 V battery rail the schematic marks (S-002) (the output pins' swing limits about their quiescent level; the datasheet's rated 55 mW into 16 Ω at 3.0 V is 1.33 V peak; **1.0 – 1.4 V** over the cells' state is registered; A-031, medium), with a **symmetric** shape with about 1 dB of rounding at the knee, using no platform math function. Measured at 44.1 kHz and above with a 1 kHz sine: the output **never exceeds 1.0**; a sine 6 dB over the ceiling peaks at **≥ −0.1 dB** with distortion between **15 % and 30 %**; **10 % distortion is reached between +1 and +3 dB over** the ceiling; a sine 6 dB under the ceiling has at most **1 %** distortion (the datasheet's 0.5 % at 20 mW into 8 Ω sits 7.4 dB under); a sine exactly at the ceiling is compressed by **at most 1 dB**. At 6, 8 and 16 kHz only the peak ceiling is asserted. The gain, the corner, the ceiling and the knee shape are design constants cited to A-031.
- **FR-007**: **Earphone path** (stage 13, A-032 adopted and corrected). The earphone path MUST apply two first-order high-pass corners, modeled on the emitter follower the schematic sheet shows: its **input coupling** (the amplifier's input capacitor and the branch's own in series into the two 10 kΩ resistors plus the follower's loaded input impedance), **120 Hz nominal, registered as 65 – 130 Hz**, and its **output coupling** (10 µF into the 16 Ω load plus the follower's output resistance), **280 Hz nominal, registered as 240 – 320 Hz** (A-032: both follow the transistor's marked current-gain rank, confidence low), each within ±10 % of its registered figure as computed from the realized section's coefficients (a core test), with unity gain in the passband (the follower's drive into 16 Ω, 15 – 24 dB below its input, is a loudness constant of FR-004), the passband above 1 kHz flat within **0.4 dB** up to 2.5 kHz, and **no clipper in the model**: the output is the input scaled by the loaded law and filtered, so a full-scale input at maximum volume passes with a **steady-state amplitude** (AES17, measured after the resampler's onset transient) of **−0.5 … 0 dB** of full scale, no steady-state sample exceeding it (the onset's overshoot is the chain's input exceeding full scale, Edge Cases; and at 6 and 8 kHz a 1 kHz tone has too few samples per cycle for a sample reading to measure the level), and distortion below **0.1 %** at 44.1 kHz and above. The follower's class-A cut-off on loud material (A-032) is registered and named in the manifest, not modeled. The response (the 32 Hz volume-control corner and the two earphone corners) MUST match the design's discrete realization of the template as a **composite** within **±0.5 dB from 20 Hz to 2.5 kHz**.
- **FR-008**: **Latency and group delay.** The output chain MUST add **no reported latency** on either route: its stages are memoryless or **minimum-phase recursive filters** (a linear-phase design is excluded), and their group delay is not counted, as spec 004 does not count the band-limit stages' edge group delay; the reported one-pass, record and playback latencies MUST be unchanged by the chain. The chain's **group delay at 1 kHz** per route MUST be at most **0.5 ms** and MUST be reported by the harness; spec 004 FR-004's check that the reported latency equals the measured 1 kHz group delay is met with the chain bypassed, and the README tells hosts aligning the default output to add the reported figure. The chain MUST run at the host rate after the resampler (like the limiter runs at the host rate before it), and each template MUST meet its stated response at every supported host rate.

**The chain, the route, the taps and the earlier checks**

- **FR-009**: **Route and volume changes.** A route change MUST take effect at the next block (spec 006 FR-015) with the newly selected route's stages starting from their rest state and the stages of the route left reset; a volume change MUST take effect at the next block, and the volume gain itself MUST NOT overshoot either position's value during the change. At position 0 the volume scalars MUST be exactly zero from the next block (the input to every filter after them), and every route output MUST decay below −120 dBFS within 100 ms (a loose bound: the sections after the multiply settle in 20 – 30 ms). Both controls are shared between the playback path and the one-pass processing, which read them at each block.
- **FR-010**: **The one-pass processing runs through the route.** The one-pass processing MUST end at the selected route's output with the same volume, so that spec 008 FR-009's equivalence between record-then-play and the one-pass holds bit for bit with the output chain on at all seven host rates, and so that a host hears the same chain whichever way it feeds the engine. Precondition, verified by the plan at the seven rates: the decoder's leading-delay output is exact digital zero, so the chain's filter state at the skip point equals the rest state the playback starts from. ### Key Entities

- **Route** (revised from spec 006): speaker or earphone, exclusive, a set-and-read control; from this feature on it selects which branch of the output chain runs and which output scale applies.
- **Volume position**: a number 0–1, a set-and-read control, the control's wiper fraction (knob travel for the linear taper); the two routes' gains follow the circuit's loaded law (A-044); default at the reference position (A-043); common to both routes.
- **Output scale**: per route, the meaning of a full-scale sample (A-042): the amplifier's clip ceiling on the speaker route; the volume control's full swing (the codec's full scale at position 1) on the earphone route.
- **Clip ceiling**: the amplifier's output swing at the battery supply (2.8 V read), a constant of about 1.15 V peak differential fixed by the plan (1.0 – 1.4 V registered; A-031); the reference position and the earphone's default-position level derive from it.
- **Stage 11**: the volume control with its 32 Hz input coupling (both routes) and the amplifier path (speaker route: the 159 Hz input corner, 22.7 dB gain, symmetric clipper at the ceiling); bypassable as a whole; design constants cited to A-031.

- **Taps** (revised): after the codec output filter (before the output chain; 0.8's default output), after stage 11 (both routes), route output (the default; resolves to the selected route's last stage), after the speaker and after the earphone (named; refused off-route).

## Assumptions

- **A-031** (adopted, corrected and extended by this feature): the volume control's taper is linear (a Panasonic "B"-taper part, S-002; high); the amplifier's gain is **22.7 dB** (the 68 kΩ feedback resistor over the 10 kΩ series input resistor, read from the schematic sheet at plan time; high for the topology, medium for the loaded value); the **clip ceiling is about 1.15 V peak differential at the 2.8 V rail the schematic marks (S-002), 1.0–1.4 V over the cells' state** (medium; the draft's "1.0–1.25 V" misread the datasheet's quiescent-level row; the datasheet's 1.33 V is the 3.0 V figure); the knee shape is a design choice bracketed by the datasheet's distortion figures (low); the input coupling corners are 32 Hz at the volume control and 159 Hz at the amplifier (high for the ideal values; the wiper resistance lowers both the gain and the 159 Hz corner at mid positions, A-044); the 220 kΩ / 10 pF path into the same input is the microcontroller's beep injection, out of scope. Verify: a capture at the speaker terminals with a 1 kHz tone and rising volume (the peak where distortion passes 10 % gives the ceiling), and the field-measurement note's speaker-over-earphone ratio.
- **A-032** (adopted and corrected): the earphone branch is fed from the amplifier's input node through a 0.1 µF capacitor and a 10 kΩ resistor in series into an emitter follower biased from the 3.2 V rail, and carries signal while the amplifier is shut down; the emitter drives the 16 Ω jack through 10 µF, with the 470 Ω resistor as the emitter's DC return to the jack's plug-detect contact (not a series resistor); its two corners are the input coupling (120 Hz nominal, 65–130 Hz) and the output coupling into 16 Ω (280 Hz nominal, 240–320 Hz), both following the transistor's marked current-gain rank (2SD1819A S, hFE 290–460; low); the follower is class A with about 4 mA of quiescent current and can sink only about 50–75 mV of negative swing into 16 Ω, so loud material is expected to be half-wave clipped above roughly 0.4–0.8 of the travel: registered, **not modeled by this feature** (low). Verify: the earphone return of the field-measurement loop with a 20 Hz – 3 kHz sweep, into a 16 Ω load for both corners and into a high-impedance input for the input corner alone.
- **A-010** (revised): the speaker's acoustic response is a **template**, not a measurement: a small enclosed driver's second-order bass roll-off with a resonance at 600–900 Hz of Q 1.2–2.5, a first-order rising shelf of +3 … +6 dB toward 3 kHz from the small case's baffle step, flat above 2.5 kHz, normalized to a peak gain of 1.0; figures chosen by the plan within these ranges and recorded; low confidence. Verify: an acoustic capture of a real unit, or the field loop's speaker-over-earphone ratio with its room residual.
- **A-042** (new): the **output scale per route**: the speaker route's full scale is the amplifier's clip ceiling, the earphone route's is the volume control's full swing (the codec's full scale at position 1); the follower's drive into 16 Ω (about 15–24 dB below its input) and both transducers' sensitivities are loudness constants outside the model. - **A-043** (new): the **volume control's default position** is the reference position at which a full-scale codec output just reaches the clip ceiling under the loaded law (about 0.13 with the 1.15 V ceiling). Medium confidence as an engine convention, low as a "typical" setting (the register cell reads in that order so the manifest's row 11 keeps its medium headline).
- **A-044** (new): the **volume position is the control's wiper fraction** (knob travel for the linear taper), and the engine's gains follow the **circuit's loaded law**: the wiper resistance 50 kΩ·p(1−p) adds to the amplifier's 10 kΩ input resistor and divides against the earphone branch's input impedance (about 24 kΩ, low confidence), so the speaker route's gain is p × 136 kΩ ÷ (10 kΩ + 50 kΩ·p(1−p)) in volts per volt and the earphone route's p × (20 kΩ + Z) ÷ (20 kΩ + Z + 50 kΩ·p(1−p)); the 159 Hz amplifier corner's shift with the wiper (to about 70 Hz at mid travel) is not modeled (under 1 dB at 300 Hz).
