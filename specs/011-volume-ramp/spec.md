# Feature Specification: Volume Ramp (a smooth transition for volume position changes, stage 11)

**Feature Branch**: `011-volume-ramp`

**Created**: 2026-10-16

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description (abridged; the full text is in the specify session's transcript): "Volume ramp: a fixed-length ramp for volume position changes on the output chain (signal-chain stage 11), the follow-up queued by spec 009's post-implementation hardware review (T043 N2). Today a position change is an instantaneous gain step at the next block boundary; a large step (for example +20 dB during playback) produces an audible click, and the size of the discontinuity depends on the host's block size. The device's own volume control is a 50 kΩ analog potentiometer turned by a thumbwheel, which cannot jump between positions, so the emulated knob should move smoothly. A short, fixed-length, smooth transition from the old gain to the new one, starting at the next block, on both routes, in the one-pass processing and in the playback path. To settle: the length (fixed in time) and shape; no overshoot of either position's value; a change arriving mid-ramp starts from the current gain; position 0 still reaches exact zeros once the ramp ends; with no volume change the output bit-identical to version 0.10 at every tap (the six golden files unmoved); deterministic and real-time safe with a cost budget stated as a ratio; what reset, reconfigure, a route change, the stage-11 bypass and the start of a playback do to a ramp; whether the length is a setting; how the length traces to a source or an assumption, or is an emulator convenience. Tests compare against the two steady gains (no overshoot, monotonic, length within a tolerance, the click's high-frequency energy reduced against today's step) at the seven host rates. Out of scope: route-change clicks (spec 009 N3), the earphone follower's cut-off, a clip indicator."

## Overview

Since [spec 009](../009-output-chain/spec.md) the volume position is a live control: the host sets it at any time, including during playback, and it takes effect at the next block (009 FR-003, FR-009). The engine turns the position into each route's gain by the circuit's loaded law ([A-044](../../docs/hardware/assumptions.md)) once per block, so a change is an **instantaneous gain step at a block boundary**. On a playing signal a large step, for example from a quiet position to the top of the travel (about +20 dB), puts a discontinuity into the waveform that is heard as a click, and where the step lands depends on the host's block size. Spec 009's post-implementation hardware review recorded this (T043 N2) and queued a fixed-length ramp.

The device has no such step. Its volume control is a 50 kΩ linear potentiometer turned by a thumbwheel ([S-002](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)); the wiper moves continuously, so the gain passes through every intermediate position on its way. This feature makes the emulated knob do the same: a position change becomes a **20 ms transition**, starting at the next block, during which the knob's position moves monotonically, starting and ending at rest, from where it was to where the host set it, and the gain at every sample is the loaded law's gain at that intermediate position. A full-travel change and a small nudge take the same time; the transition is the same length at every host rate and does not depend on the block size.

The feature changes nothing when the volume does not change. The first block after construction, reset or reconfigure, and the first block of each playback, take the position at once (a playback starts from rest, [A-037](../../docs/hardware/assumptions.md)), so every configuration that sets its volume before it processes is bit-identical to version 0.10 at every tap, and the six golden files stay unmoved. What changes is the output during the transition after a mid-stream change, which spec 009's checks measured as a step at the next block.

The transition is not a limiter, a fade on route changes or a model of how fast a person turns the thumbwheel: the host decides when the position changes, and the engine moves the knob there in a fixed time. The potentiometer's own artifacts (the wiper's contact noise on a worn track, momentary lift-off) are not modeled. Route-change clicks (spec 009 N3) stay out of scope.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-10-16 (specify: the three choices the description left open, answered by the user)

- Q: How long is the transition? → A: **20 ms**, fixed in time at every host rate (FR-002). It is slightly longer than a 60 Hz UI frame (16.7 ms), so a dragged thumbwheel sending one position per frame chains into one continuous motion, and the knob trails a drag by at most 20 ms. The specify step's numerical check (a ramp **linear in gain**, 0.1 → 1 on a 1 kHz tone at 48 kHz, a 1 s Hann window, the energy more than 250 Hz from the tone) read about 32 dB below the instantaneous step; that is not the model FR-001 specifies (corrected by the spec review below). Rejected: 10 ms (a smooth shape reaches only about 22 dB on the speaker law) and 50 ms (it lags a UI visibly; a change to 0 waits 50 ms for the exact zeros).
- Q: Is the transition a modeled behavior or an emulator convenience? → A: **A modeled behavior of stage 11, with a new assumption A-045** (FR-010): the change sits inside a device stage and changes its tap, the device's wiper moves continuously (S-002), and the length is a default parameter value, which constitution II requires to trace to a registered assumption. Stage 11's model version moves to 0.11.0 and the canonical text and the README table are re-blessed once. Rejected: an emulator convenience like spec 010's output clamp, whose bound sits after the transducer templates and corresponds to nothing in the device; this transition does.
- Q: Is the transition fixed, or a host setting? → A: **Fixed and always on** (FR-011): no new setting and no C structure change; the instantaneous step stays reachable through the test hooks only, for the route-output comparison (US1 AS7). The wider controls discussion the user queued may revisit it. Rejected: an on/off setting and a length setting with 0 meaning the step (each a C structure change with refusals and a test across values, without an app request).

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Click-free volume changes during playback (Priority: P1)

An app developer binds the recorder's thumbwheel in the app to the engine's volume position. While a recording plays, the user flicks the wheel from near the bottom to the top. With version 0.10 the playback clicks at the change; with this feature the level rises smoothly over 20 ms and there is no click, whatever block size the app's audio callback uses. When the user drags the wheel, the app sends a new position every screen frame and the level follows the drag continuously. An audiophile turning the volume during a listening comparison hears the level change, not an artifact of the engine.

**Why this priority**: this is the whole feature: the one audible defect spec 009 left in the live control. It is a usable increment on its own.

**Independent Test**: can be fully tested with a fresh engine at each supported host rate in a **stage-11-only configuration** (every earlier stage bypassed, spec 009's output-chain-only configuration with the speaker template and the earphone output bypassed, read at the tap after stage 11), so the output is the volume gain applied to the input after the volume control's coupling corners (and, on the speaker route, before the knee), with the resampler pair the only other processing, common to every run and cancelling in each comparison (constitution VII). The stimuli are synthetic steady tones; the oracles are runs of the same engine held at a constant position on the same input. The memory, the export and the C API are not needed.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the stage-11-only configuration on the earphone route at each of the seven host rates, a steady full-scale tone and a position of 0.1, **When** the position is set to 1 between two blocks, **Then** (a) the output up to the change is bit-identical to a run held at 0.1, and from transition sample N on bit-identical to a run held at 1, the first sample differing from the old-position run being transition sample 1 and the last sample differing from the new-position run being sample N − 1; (b) the per-sample gain sequence, read through the test hooks, is monotonic, starts above the old gain at sample 1, equals the loaded law at the shape's position at every sample and the 0.10 gain at the target exactly at sample N, and never leaves the range of the two gains; (c) the ratio of the output to a reference run at position 1, over the samples where the reference is at least 0.25 of the tone's peak, is monotonic within 4 f32 ULP. The same holds for the change from 1 to 0.1.
2. **Given** the same configuration, **When** the same change is made with block sizes of 1, 64 and 4096 host samples and an irregular partition, the change requested at a sample that is a boundary of every partition (a multiple of 4096), **Then** the outputs are bit-identical (the transition does not depend on the partition).
3. **Given** the same configuration, **When** the position changes from 0.1 to 1 (and, separately, from 1 to 0.1) on the earphone route with a full-scale 1 kHz tone and on the speaker route with a −30 dBFS 1 kHz tone, **Then** the click energy of FR-004 is at least 30 dB below that of the same change made as an instantaneous step (the splice of the two constant-position runs at the change), at every host rate, and within ±1 dB of the plan's computed figure for that rate and route.
4. **Given** the speaker route in the stage-11-only configuration (the amplifier's knee on), **When** the same changes are made, **Then** a −30 dBFS tone (below the knee at both positions) shows scenario 1's trajectory, and a full-scale tone never exceeds the ceiling.
5. **Given** a transition in progress, **When** the host sets a new position, **Then** a new full-length transition starts at the next block from the knob's position at the last sample before that block, with no jump in the gain, and moves to the new position; **When** the host sets the position the knob is already moving to, **Then** the transition in progress continues unchanged.
6. **Given** a transition to position 0, **When** it ends, **Then** the volume gain is exactly zero, the tap after stage 11 reads exact zeros from transition sample N, and every route output decays below −120 dBFS within 100 ms of sample N (spec 009 FR-009's bound, measured from the transition's end).
7. **Given** spec 009's output-chain-only configuration (the speaker template or the earphone output in place) and the stimuli of scenario 3, **When** the same changes are made, **Then** the click energy at the route output against the test hooks' instantaneous step is **reported** per rate and route (not asserted).
8. **Given** the stage-11-only configuration and a drag, ten position updates 16.7 ms apart from 0.1 to 1, **When** it is played, **Then** the gain sequence is monotonic and the click energy of FR-004 over the drag is at least 30 dB below the same drag made as instantaneous steps (version 0.10's behavior), on both routes at the seven rates, the figure reported.

### Edge Cases

- **A change before the first block.** Construction, reset, reconfigure and the start of a playback take the position at once (no transition); a host that sets its position and then processes gets exactly 0.10's output. A zero-length processing call does not consume "the first block".
- **A change to the same position.** Not a change: no transition starts, and a transition already moving to that position continues.
- **Several positions set in one gap.** Only the last counts, compared with the knob's current target.
- **A change mid-transition.** A new full-length transition starts at the next block from the knob's position at the last sample before it, starting from rest; the gain never jumps and never leaves the range between that position's gain and the new target's.
- **Rapid updates (a dragging UI).** With updates arriving more often than every 20 ms, each update restarts a transition from the current position; the knob trails the latest setting by at most 20 ms, and the gain stays continuous and monotonic for a monotonic drag (US1 AS8).
- **A refused position.** Unchanged position, no transition.
- **Position 0.** The gain is exactly zero from transition sample N; spec 009's exact zeros at the tap after stage 11 hold from then on.
- **The read-back position.** Reading the volume returns the host's last setting, not the intermediate knob position, so 009's read-after-write behavior is unchanged.
- **A route change during a transition, or with a position change in the same gap.** The transition completes at once; the new route starts from rest at the target position. The route change's own click is spec 009 N3's and out of scope.
- **Stage 11 bypassed.** The position and any transition have no effect (unity gain at the codec's scale, 009 C4); a later reconfigure that re-enables stage 11 takes the position at once.
- **A transition at the end of a playback.** The drain (`N_OUT`, more than 2.87 transition lengths at every rate) completes it; its length is unchanged. The next playback starts at the set position.
- **A change during the drain.** It starts a transition as usual; if the playback finishes first the transition is cut, and the next playback starts at once at the set position. Spec 009 FR-008's last-millisecond bound applies to a volume unchanged during the drain (a change there lifts the tail in 0.10 as well).
- **The one-pass after a playback in which the volume changed.** The one-pass processing and the player each keep their own knob; the one-pass is not reset by a playback, so its next block ramps from its own last position to the current setting (the README says so).
- **Recording.** The record path has no volume; the speaker is muted while recording (009); nothing here applies.
- **Low host rates.** At 6 kHz the transition is 120 host samples, at 96 kHz 1920; the duration is exactly 20 ms at every supported rate.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: **The transition.** A change of the volume position MUST take effect as a **transition** over transition samples k = 1 … N (FR-002), sample 1 being the first sample of the next block that processes at least one sample. At sample k the volume gain on the selected route MUST be the loaded law's gain (A-044) at the knob position p_s + (p_t − p_s) · s(k/N), where p_s is the knob position at the last sample before that block, p_t the newly set position and s a non-decreasing shape with s(0) = 0 and s(1) = 1 whose slope is zero at both ends (the knob starts and ends at rest); sample N MUST carry **exactly** version 0.10's gain at p_t, and every later sample the same until the next change. (The other route's gain is not observable during a transition: a route change completes it at once, FR-005.)
- **FR-002**: **Fixed length.** N MUST be 0.02 × host rate exactly (**20 ms**, A-045): 120 at 6 kHz, 160 at 8 kHz, 320 at 16 kHz, 882 at 44.1 kHz, 960 at 48 kHz, 1764 at 88.2 kHz and 1920 at 96 kHz, for any size of change; the transition MUST NOT depend on the block size or partition.
- **FR-003**: **No overshoot, no jump.** At no sample during a transition MAY the volume gain lie outside the range between the gain at p_s and the gain at p_t (spec 009's edge case "volume changed mid-block"), in floating point as well as in the formula, and the gain MUST be non-decreasing (upward) or non-increasing (downward) from sample to sample by construction, including on a retarget to a position within rounding of the knob's; a change arriving during a transition MUST start a new full-length transition from p_s as FR-001 defines it, with no discontinuity in the gain.
- **FR-004**: **Click reduction.** In the stage-11-only configuration at the tap after stage 11, for a change between positions 0.1 and 1 in either direction, on the earphone route with a full-scale 1 kHz cosine and on the speaker route with a −30 dBFS one (below the knee at both positions), the **click energy** MUST be at least **30 dB** below that of the same change made as an instantaneous step at the same block boundary, at every host rate, and within ±1 dB of the plan's computed figure per rate and route. The click energy is the energy, in a Hann window of round(0.5 × host rate) samples centred on the transition's midpoint (on the step for the reference), at frequencies between 20 Hz and 2750 Hz more than 250 Hz from 1 kHz; the tone is settled at least 0.5 s before the change and the change's first sample falls on a positive peak of the host grid. A movement linear in position does not meet this floor on the speaker law; the plan's shape MUST.
- **FR-005**: **Start at once, end at once.** The first block that processes at least one sample after construction, reset or reconfigure, and the first such block of each playback, MUST run at the set position from its first sample with no transition; a reset, a reconfigure, the end of a playback and a route change MUST end any transition in progress (the route change at the new position). The drain length MUST be unchanged; a transition in progress when the drain starts MUST continue into it and complete inside it: the output chain's share of the drain (`N_OUT`) alone exceeds the transition length at every rate (2.879 – 2.883 times, reported; 100 · N_OUT > 287 · N asserted), and spec 009 FR-008's last-millisecond bound MUST hold for a volume unchanged during the drain.
- **FR-006**: **Unchanged without a change.** With a volume that does not change after the first block, every tap's output MUST be bit-identical to version 0.10's at every route, position and host rate; the six existing golden files MUST be byte-identical and their SHA guards unchanged. The plan MUST list every existing check that changes the volume mid-stream (spec 009's volume-change checks, the allocation check, spec 010's change-timing identity, the golden generators, the C smoke loop) with each one's restated or unchanged expectation, and MUST verify that no golden entry changes the volume after its first block.
- **FR-007**: **Both paths, both routes.** The transition MUST apply on the speaker and the earphone route, in the playback path and in the one-pass processing, which share the volume control as in spec 009; the same change requested at the same output sample position in record-then-play and in the one-pass (counted after spec 008's skipped prefix) MUST give bit-identical output from that sample on, and record-then-play MUST equal the one-pass under spec 008's rule with a constant volume.
- **FR-008**: **Exact zeros.** A transition to position 0 MUST end with the volume gain exactly zero at sample N; the tap after stage 11 MUST read exact zeros from sample N, and every route output MUST decay below −120 dBFS within 100 ms of sample N (spec 009 FR-009, restated from the transition's end).
- **FR-009**: **Determinism, real-time safety and cost.** The transition MUST be deterministic (bit-identical on every supported platform and partition; no platform math function) and allocation-free; its per-sample work MUST be bounded. With no transition in progress the codec-bypassed one-pass cost per host sample at 48 kHz on the speaker route MUST rise by at most **3 %** against version 0.10, measured as an A/B of the 0.10 and 0.11 builds (nine alternating runs each) on the development machine and once on the CI runner before the merge; in the worst case of US2 AS7 (every sample inside a transition) by at most **15 %**, measured in one build against the same partition with the transition replaced by the instantaneous step (a test hook), on the development machine and the CI runner, on material whose cost does not depend on the position; the figure against 0.10 is the product of the two ratios; the nanoseconds and the duty reported beside each ratio (spec 010's lesson: budgets are ratios).
- **FR-010**: **Traceability.** The transition MUST be a **modeled behavior of stage 11**: the device's wiper moves continuously ([S-002](../../docs/hardware/sources.md)), and the transition's length and shape are an engine convention registered as a new assumption **A-045** with its rationale, a split confidence (high for the continuous wiper, medium for the 20 ms and the shape as an engine convention) and a verification method; the code and the records MUST cite it. Stage 11's manifest entry MUST cite A-045, name the wiper's contact noise and the knob's speed in its "not modeled here" note, and move its model version to 0.11.0; the canonical text and the README's manifest table MUST be re-blessed once for that change (and the header's library version) in the feature's first phase, after the wording review of the changed strings; a second re-bless is allowed only if a later review changes a string, each recorded in the CHANGELOG (spec 007 FR-015). The manifest's controls note does not list the published constant of FR-011 (a constant, not a control). A-044's row MUST name the transition; the signal-chain table's row 11 "Modeled by" marker MUST name it.
- **FR-011**: **Control surface.** The transition MUST be **fixed and always on**: no new setting, no change to the Rust settings or the C settings structure (it stays 112 bytes) and no new C function; version 0.10's instantaneous step is reachable only through the test hooks, for US1 AS7's comparison, and the per-sample gain sequence is readable through them for US1 AS1 (b). The volume control's own surface (setting, reading and refusing a position) MUST be unchanged; reading the volume MUST return the host's last setting. The length MAY be published as a read-only constant (Rust and C) and a Rust helper giving N per rate; a constant is not a setting.
- **FR-012**: **Its own golden file.** The transition MUST have its own golden file at the seven host rates: the stage-11-only configuration with a steady tone and the default engine with the voiced stimulus, both routes, with at least an upward change, a downward change, a change to 0, a change arriving mid-transition and a drag, blessed once and reproducing on every golden-matrix platform.
- **FR-013**: **Records.** The README's volume paragraph, the API documentation of the volume control (Rust and C), the CHANGELOG, spec 009's FR-009 note and its T043 N2 entry (recorded closed by this feature) MUST state the transition, its length, its start at the next block, the start-at-once rule, the one-pass's ramping of a mid-stream change and the read-back rule; the "Hardware evidence" issue template MUST gain the knob-flick capture of A-045; spec 009's checks that assert a step at the next block MUST be restated against the transition (the old position up to the next block, the new position's gain exactly from sample N). The version MUST be 0.11.0.

### Out of Scope

Route-change clicks and a fade on route changes (spec 009 N3); a model of how fast a person turns the thumbwheel (the host decides when the position changes); the potentiometer's own artifacts (the wiper's contact noise or crackle, lift-off, detents) and its end resistance (a lead for A-044); the knob-angle-to-position mapping (A-044 leaves it unmodeled); a volume law change; the earphone follower's class-A cut-off (A-032); a clip indicator; making other settings live controls (the controls discussion the user queued).

### Key Entities

- **Volume position**: the host's setting, 0 to 1 (spec 009 FR-003); what reading the volume returns.
- **Knob position**: the position the gain is computed from at each sample; equal to the volume position except during a transition.
- **Transition**: the fixed-length movement of the knob position from p_s to p_t over samples 1 … N, starting at the next block, starting and ending at rest.
- **Transition length**: N = 0.02 × host rate host samples (FR-002).
- **Stage-11-only configuration**: every earlier stage bypassed, stage 11 on, the speaker template and the earphone output bypassed, read at the tap after stage 11.
- **Click energy**: FR-004's windowed out-of-band energy around a change; the reduction is the step's click energy over the transition's, in dB.
- **Instantaneous step**: version 0.10's behavior; at the tap after stage 11 exactly the splice of two constant-position runs at the change, elsewhere the test hooks' step.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: At the seven host rates on both routes (the speaker route at −30 dBFS), a change from 0.1 to 1 and from 1 to 0.1 is bit-identical to the old position's run before the change and to the new position's run from sample N on; the gain sequence is exactly monotonic and within the two positions' gains; the ratio reader is monotonic within 4 f32 ULP; and the output is bit-identical across block partitions of 1, 64 and 4096 samples and an irregular one.
- **SC-002**: The click reduction (FR-004) is at least 30 dB at every host rate on both routes and within ±1 dB of the plan's computed figure, reported per rate and route; the route-output figure (US1 AS7) is reported.
- **SC-003**: At a change mid-transition no per-sample gain increment exceeds the larger of the two transitions' largest increments, at every host rate; a ten-update drag is monotonic and at least 30 dB below the stepped drag; a transition to 0 ends at exactly zero gain at sample N and the route output decays below −120 dBFS within 100 ms of it.
- **SC-004**: The six existing golden files are byte-identical with unchanged SHA guards; every pre-existing check that does not change the volume mid-stream passes unchanged, and every one that does is restated as the plan lists; the feature's golden file is blessed once and reproduces on every golden-matrix platform.
- **SC-005**: Record-then-play equals the one-pass bit for bit with a constant volume and from the change on with a change at the same output sample, on both routes, at the seven host rates; 100 % of the determinism and allocation-free cases pass with transitions running; the drain length is unchanged and a transition in the last block completes inside it.
- **SC-006**: The cost rises by at most 3 % with no transition in progress (the build A/B) and at most 15 % with every sample inside a transition (against the step, US2 AS7), nanoseconds and duty reported beside each ratio, on the development machine and the CI runner (the idle A/B once there before the merge).
- **SC-007**: The manifest cross-check, the traceability audit and the wording checks pass; the README, the API documentation, the CHANGELOG, the register, the signal-chain table and the issue template carry the stated text.
- **SC-008**: A developer can find, from the README alone, how long a volume change takes, when it starts, and what the start of a stream does, in under 5 minutes.

## Assumptions

- **A-045** (new): a volume position change moves the device's wiper continuously, which the engine models as a fixed 20 ms movement of the knob position, starting and ending at rest, from the old position to the new, the gain following the loaded law (A-044) at every sample. Confidence, in A-043's style: **high** that the wiper moves continuously with no step (and with no DC thump, since C14 before the control and C8 after the wiper keep DC off the track, A-031; an inference from the register's topology); **medium (engine convention)** for the 20 ms and the shape: the device's own transition time is the user's thumb, which the engine does not model, and 20 ms is chosen for a block-based host (one 60 Hz UI frame plus margin). Verification: record a 1 kHz tone at −10 dBFS on a real unit, play it back on the earphone route into a high-impedance input, and flick the thumbwheel end to end ten times fast and ten times slow; measure the envelope's 10–90 % time, and the band energy of FR-004's window and band during the motion relative to the tone's energy in a steady segment of the same capture, against the engine's figure for a transition of the measured 10–90 % time; report both end levels, which anchor the positions through A-044 (a real unit has no instantaneous-step reference). This shows the continuity, any contact crackle and the fastest end-to-end flick (expected 50 ms or more, speculative); it cannot pin 20 ms. Not modeled: the wiper's contact noise on a worn or dirty track, momentary lift-off (dropouts), detents, the knob's speed and inertia, the 159 Hz corner's movement with the wiper (already in A-044), the track's end resistance (a lead for A-044: a real unit may not be exactly silent at 0).
- The knob position is interpolated, not the gain (and not the gain in dB, which is undefined at 0): the gain during a transition is the loaded law's gain at an intermediate position (FR-001). The loaded law is monotonic in the position on both routes: d/dp [p · A ÷ (R_i + R · p (1 − p))] = A (R_i + R p²) ÷ D², positive for any positive resistances (the earphone route with R_i + R65 + Z = 26.4 kΩ in place of R_i), so a monotonic knob movement gives a monotonic gain and the no-overshoot rule (FR-003) follows in the formula; the plan states how it holds in floating point.
- **Expected shape: the smoothstep** s(u) = 3u² − 2u³ (plain multiplies, no platform math function, zero slope at both ends), 33.9 – 34.5 dB on the speaker law and 40.6 – 41.2 dB on the earphone law against the instantaneous step over the seven rates (the plan's reference script; the Rust chain reproduces them within 0.03 dB, the plan review). A movement linear in position does not meet FR-004 on the speaker law (18.7 – 19.8 dB); a raised cosine needs a cosine; the smootherstep (6u⁵ − 15u⁴ + 10u³) is better on a flick (about 45 dB) but makes a 60 Hz drag worse (+13 dB in the 40–250 Hz band) for no audible gain. The plan confirms the choice.
- On a retarget the knob restarts from rest; for a 60 Hz drag the smoothstep's 0.10 dB peak-to-peak gain ripple at 60 Hz is far below amplitude-modulation thresholds (the hardware reviewer's inference).
- The transition starts at a block boundary, as every control change does since spec 006 (the controls are read once per block); a host that wants a change at a given sample splits its block there.
- A playback always starts from rest (A-037), so the first block of each playback takes the set position at once; the one-pass processing does the same after construction, reset and reconfigure, and ramps a change made mid-stream.
- A route change already resets the route's sections (spec 009 FR-009); completing the transition at once there adds no new discontinuity of its own.
- No golden entry changes the volume after its first block (to be verified by the plan, FR-006); the existing checks that change the volume mid-stream are listed by the plan with their expectations.
- The cost budget is stated as ratios against version 0.10's codec-bypassed speaker-route one-pass (about 57 – 68 ns per host sample on the development machine) at the same partition, because a fixed figure did not transfer to the x86-64 CI runner (spec 010); the hardware reviewer's estimate is +2 – 5 ns (3 – 8 %) with every sample in a transition, computing only the active route's gain per sample.
- The version is 0.11.0; no release has been published.
