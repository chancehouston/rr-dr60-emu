# Changelog

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

Changes to assumed hardware values ([assumption register](docs/hardware/assumptions.md)) alter emulator output and are always listed here.

## [Unreleased]

### Documentation

- Hardware evidence pass (2026-10-10): the RR-DR60 service manual (S-002) and the OKI codec family datasheet (S-005) were read in full. The register now records the device's **6 kHz sampling rate**, **4 kbit/s CELP** codec and 16 Mbit flash, the µ-law coding law (A-003 verified), the discrete mic amp and the **limiter-type AGC** with a ~10 s release (A-028), the codec's PLL-clocked filters and levels (A-027, A-030), the earphone path that bypasses the power amp (A-032), and that the sensitivity setting and the VAS are DSP firmware functions after the AGC (A-033). A-001 is superseded by A-026; A-014, A-017 and A-018 are marked for revision. Emulator output was unchanged by this pass; the 6 kHz rate and the filter templates were adopted by spec 004 (see **Changed** below, 2026-10-11), which supersedes A-014. The AGC revision (A-017, A-018, A-028) was adopted by spec 005 (see **Changed**, 2026-10-11). README gains a per-stage fidelity table.

### Changed

- **The device-rate stream is narrowed to 32-bit precision after the VAS; the three golden files re-blessed once** ([spec 006](specs/006-record-playback-split/spec.md) FR-009, 2026-10-11; version **0.6.0**). **Output change at every host rate, inaudible, recorded because output changes.** The one-pass processing now rounds every kept post-VAS device-rate sample to `f32` before stage 10, the point where this feature's record path takes a recording (in the device an 8-bit µ-law code sits there, A-003, 16 bits coarser than this rounding; a defence of inaudibility, not a fidelity claim), so that record-then-play through a 32-bit store is bit-identical to the one-pass processing and the golden files stay the oracle. The pre-0.6 un-narrowed engine is kept as a test-only hook so the figure stays measurable. Measured by `cargo test -p rr_dr60_harness --test narrowing_delta -- --nocapture` over every golden entry at the seven host rates, in full-scale LSBs (2⁻²³ × max(1, peak of the un-narrowed output); bound 4, derived from the L1 norms ‖h₁₀‖₁ = 2.65 of stage 10 and 2.33 of the largest interpolator branch, both pinned by unit tests):
  - Golden entries: 112 / 42 / 63, re-blessed once for the `f32` narrowing. `golden-v1.json`: 100 of 112 entries changed, 30.1 % of samples, max |Δ| 1.00 full-scale LSB. `golden-agc-v1.json`: 21 of 42, 15.8 %, max |Δ| 1.87 (`agc_staircase` / `default_agc` at 88.2 kHz, the clamped-onset case). `golden-vas-v1.json`: 57 of 63, 12.7 %, max |Δ| 0.06. The largest change is about 1.87 × 2⁻²³ of full scale, about −133 dBFS. VAS events, output lengths and the paused state are unchanged in every entry.
  - The 39 entries that are byte-identical: the 21 `agc_only` entries (the host-rate "after AGC" tap never enters the device chain) and, at the 6 kHz identity rate, the 14 with no arithmetic before the narrowing (`bypass_all`, `playback_only`, `vas_only`, `vas_mute`: the limiter and stage 4 bypassed, and the clamp and the VAS keep an `f32`-exact value exact) and the 4 with none after it (`record_only`: no stage 10 and no interpolator, so the output's own rounding is the same operation). `narrowing_delta` asserts exactly that set, so a narrowing at the wrong point would be caught.
  - `golden-vas-v1.json` gains a SHA-256 guard like the other two files. The golden window is closed: no later change in this feature may move any of the three hashes.

- **The record-path AGC is now the schematic's limiter; the ADC clamp; the VAS threshold re-anchored** ([spec 005](specs/005-limiter-agc/spec.md), 2026-10-11; version **0.5.0**). **Assumption change: the default output changes at every host rate.** Spec 002 modeled an assumed wide-range compressor before the service manual's schematic (S-002) had been read. The schematic shows a **peak-detecting limiter** at the codec input, so **A-028 is adopted** (reinterpreted after a circuit analysis) and **A-017 and A-018 are superseded**; A-019 (the detector) and A-022 (the VAS threshold) are revised, A-007 is verified in circuit, A-029 is refined, and **A-034** (attack dead time), **A-035** (release) and **A-036** (sensed polarity) are new. Every figure below is **modeled on the circuit with estimated transistor parameters, not measured against a unit**: the dead time is uncertain by about 3× either way, the release by about 40 %, the knee by ±2 dB. After the post-implementation hardware-emulation review (2026-10-11) the default knee moved from +1 to **−1 dBFS**: the model's hard 0.6 V detector threshold is the transistor's base–emitter voltage at the attack's milliampere charge peaks, and at the detector's steady-state current the same transistor model puts the sustained ceiling about 2 dB lower, just **below** full scale (−0.8 dBFS for the model's own saturation current); the dead time and the clipped onset are unaffected. The review also found that the register's SPL anchor did not close with its own figures (A-029 restated as 80–100 dB SPL for full scale) and that the fast attack after a loud sound lasts a few seconds, not ten (the 10 s is the arming decay's time constant).
  - **What a listener hears.** Below the knee nothing happens: quiet input passes bit for bit, nothing is raised, and background noise no longer rises in pauses. A loud sound (about full scale and above) passes at full level for a few milliseconds of clipped onset, then is held at a ceiling about 1 dB below full scale, a clean sine, for as long as it lasts; the gain recovers over about 1.4 s afterwards, and for a few seconds a new loud onset is limited within about half a millisecond (the dead time is back to two thirds of its full value after 10 s). Pauses are decided against −40 dBFS instead of −18 dBFS.
  - **Level anchor.** 0 dBFS (a full-scale sine) is the codec's full scale, about 0.196 V peak at the mic-amp output (A-030, A-029). Where speech sits against it depends on the mic chain's gain, the least certain number in the register: roughly 10–35 dB below full scale at a conversational distance (full scale at 80–100 dB SPL, A-029, low confidence), so with the default knee the limiter acts on loud sounds and, at the high end of the estimate, on the peaks of ordinary speech.
  - **Honesty caveat.** The knee is modeled 1 dB below full scale, so sustained loud input comes out as a clean sine held just below full scale and only the onset clips; the hard-threshold value (+1 dBFS, flat-topped by the codec for about 30 % of each cycle) is the upper end of A-028's ±2 dB and a real unit may show that instead. This is the most audible open question; the hardware-evidence issue template lists the deciding captures (the recorded waveform of a tone held at a fixed loud level). The detector's hard threshold is a known simplification: an exponential detector would give a soft knee, deferred until a capture shows one.
  - **Not modeled** (research R-14): the detector's 13 kHz roll-off and 7 Hz DC block (a host input's DC offset is seen by the detector); the detector's exponential (soft-knee) behavior; the codec input amp's rail clip in the signal path (only its effect on the detector, where it sets the maximum overdrive); the knee's temperature drift (about −0.3 dB per 10 °C); the mic amp's own clipping 7–16 dB above the knee (stage 2, out of scope). The detector's bandwidth is the host rate's, so it differs between host rates: a model property, not a determinism failure.

- **Device rate 8 → 6 kHz and the codec filter templates** ([spec 004](specs/004-device-rate-6khz/spec.md)). **Assumption change: the output changes at every host rate.** The service manual's specification table (S-002) states a 6 kHz sampling rate, so **A-026 is adopted and A-001 superseded**: the band-limit stages, the AGC and the VAS all run at 6 kHz (Nyquist 3 kHz). The two band-limit stages are now modeled on the codec family datasheet's templates (S-005) scaled by 0.75 to that clock (**A-027 adopted; A-002 and A-014 superseded**), with unity gain at 765 Hz (A-015):
  - the record stage (signal-chain stage 4) is the transmit band-pass: loss within −0.15 … +0.2 dB from 225 to 2250 Hz, 0.31 dB at 2550 Hz (datasheet typical 0.4), at least 20 dB attenuation at 45 Hz and zeros at DC;
  - the playback stage (stage 10) is the receive low-pass only, which **passes DC** (the 0.3 stage rejected it): loss within −0.15 … +0.25 dB from DC to 2250 Hz, 0.48 dB at 2550 Hz (typical 0.56).
  Both are minimum-phase (A-016, now partially verified against S-005's group-delay rows; group delay at 1 kHz 0.09 and 0.05 ms, at 2550 Hz 1.43 and 1.58 ms), implemented as a 5th-order Chebyshev-II low-pass with a 32 dB stopband, plus a 3rd-order Butterworth high-pass at 112.5 Hz in the record stage. The roll-off between 2550 Hz and 3 kHz is a design choice not constrained by any source (reported at 2700 and 2900 Hz: 16 / 33 dB record, 19 / 32 dB playback). Medium confidence: modeled on the datasheet scaled to the measured clock, **not measured against a real unit**. Known deviations, not modeled: the device's partial aliasing of 3000–3450 Hz input into the top of its band, and its DAC imaging residue above 3 kHz; the emulator's rate conversion boundary rejects both by at least 60 dB.
- **Host rates.** 6 kHz is a supported host rate and the identity: with every stage bypassed the output equals the input sample for sample and the latency is 0. 8 and 16 kHz are no longer the identity but 3:4 and 3:8 conversions, so with every stage bypassed their output passes through the rate conversion boundary (flat within ±0.1 dB to 2550 Hz, at least 60 dB rejection from 3000 Hz) instead of being the input itself. **Breaking (0.3 → 0.4):** `SUPPORTED_HOST_RATES` is `[u32; 7]`, `[6000, 8000, 16000, 44100, 48000, 88200, 96000]`; the C API accepts `host_rate_hz = 6000`; any other rate is still rejected. No setting was added or removed.

- Version 0.4.0 (crates and `RR_DR60_VERSION_*`).
- **Default output now drops pauses** (S-001, A-008, A-022 – A-025), so a block can produce fewer output samples than it consumed, or none: read `BlockInfo::produced` (C: `RrDr60BlockInfo.produced`). Through the AGC's static curve (A-017) the default threshold is about −58 dBFS peak input-referred at sensitivity level 3, so the default pauses only on near-silence; an input floor whose peaks exceed about −58 dBFS keeps recording at levels 2–5. To get the 0.2 sound (AGC and band-limiting, fixed length), set `settings.vas.enabled = false` (C: `vas_enabled = false`).
