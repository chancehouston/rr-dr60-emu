DIRECTION 01

Vintage Instrument

A warm, tactile late-1990s recorder with a phosphor LCD, molded keys, engraved labels and a restrained olive circuit board when flipped. It makes the recorder feel immediately familiar and gives the inside a clear physical metaphor.

Strength: memorable hardware character and an inviting, simple front. Trade-off: material realism can crowd small screens and may suggest a literal replica where the product is an emulator.

DIRECTION 02

Modern Industrial

A precise contemporary instrument: graphite surfaces, a compact live display, crisp typography and teal signal accents. Device hardware remains the visual anchor, while familiar iPhone navigation and restrained cards keep the experience legible and approachable.

Strength: strong scanability, credible measurements and room to grow. Trade-off: can read like a technical utility if the tactile recorder cues become too subtle.

DIRECTION 03

Experimental

A retrofuturist field instrument with an observatory palette, amber readouts, deep indigo glass and modular component bays. The atmosphere borrows from scientific equipment and speculative fiction, while every plotted reading stays literal, labelled and visually separate from decoration.

Strength: distinctive emotional presence for field sessions and a striking flip reveal. Trade-off: theatrical styling needs careful restraint so sensor readings never feel like evidence or imply a conclusion.

What the concepts keep consistent

  • The front stays a recorder: timer, level meter, large Record and Stop, playback, numbered memory, storage gauge, and the optional flip.
  • The inside shows the active path and engine-returned values. Tapping a stage opens its description, read-out and evidence status; the text list remains available.
  • Investigation separates the audio and sensor tracks, labels units and status, shows user-authored marks by kind, and uses one cursor for review.
  • Language stays neutral: no paranormal conclusions, no implied detection, and no claim that stages have been checked against real hardware.

Concept decisions, not requirements

Screen composition, color, materials, names and navigation placement are exploratory. The brief leaves exact navigation, default mode, storage and deletion affordances for kept microphone audio, and several Investigation choices open. These mockups use Recorder as the default, show a review or live session as a representative screen, and leave those decisions visible for discussion.
