# The 1.0 feature set: what the product is and what the engine can do

A briefing for anyone designing the app's screens without the project's history, written 2026-10-31 against engine **0.22.0** (branch `022-abi-pass`). It gathers the agreed product direction from the notes in this folder and the engine's actual capabilities from its guide and its two self-describing manifests. It is a description, not a screen specification and not a commitment: the **Open decisions** section lists what is undecided, and a design should present options there rather than assume. It follows the claims policy in section 8.

## 1. The product and the road to 1.0

One instrument with two ways to explore it: a free phone app, iPhone first and Android planned, that reproduces the **signal chain of the Panasonic RR-DR60**, a 1998 IC voice recorder, stage by stage from its service manual and codec datasheets, on an open-source engine (`rr_dr60`, MIT). The **front** is a simple recorder; the **inside**, reached by flipping the device over, is the signal chain working, each stage labelled with what it is modeled on and whether it has been checked against a real unit (today: none). **Recorder mode** is the plain device experience for every user; **Investigation mode** is a field instrument that records the same audio together with time-aligned tracks from the phone's own sensors and reviews them on one timeline. The road to 1.0 (owner's decision, 2026-10-31): the engine takes one more feature after the current ABI pass, pitch-kept slow and fast playback as a selectable option, and is then declared **feature complete at 0.90.0**; the app is built on that engine, with the open product questions reopened first; **engine and app go 1.0 together**. No revenue is expected in year one; the year-one goal is credibility.

## 2. How to read this document

Every engine fact below carries one of three labels where it matters to the design:

| Label | Meaning for the UI |
|---|---|
| **device** | Documented about the real recorder (owner's manual S-001, service manual S-002). Present it as the device's own behaviour. |
| **modeled** | A registered assumption (A-###) about the device, built from documents, **not measured** against a real unit. Present it as "modeled on …". |
| **emulator** | Something the device cannot do; a convenience of the software. Present it as such, outside the device drawing or labelled "(emulator)". |

The engine's status today: 15 signal-chain stages, **13 modeled (assumed), 2 not modeled (noise reduction, clock), 0 sourced, 0 checked against a real unit**. The real-unit check is out of scope until the project owns a unit it can open (decision 2026-10-18); the header on the inside says so plainly.

## 3. The two modes

Both modes share the engine, the recording library and the flip to the inside. The front-and-exposed-back concept is the visual baseline; Investigation mode has its own theatrical direction (brass, glowing tubes) with measurements clearly separated from atmosphere. Skins are a later appearance layer that changes materials, colours and meter styling, never function.

### 3.1 Recorder mode: the front

The simple view: "it should feel like using a piece of audio hardware, not operating an engineering tool." Elements agreed so far (the dossier's concept 01, a placeholder rendering):

- an LCD-style **timer** and **level meter**;
- a large **record / stop** button; Stop is persistent and large everywhere, including on the back;
- **play**, with the device's own transport: the recording plays back at once after Stop with the pauses already removed (the device's signature moment);
- the **recordings** list (the memory: up to 99 items, 60 minutes in total, **device**);
- the device controls (section 4.2): volume, speaker or earphone, playback speed, microphone sensitivity;
- the storage gauge (minutes of 60, recordings of 99);
- an **"Inside"** flip button (also a drag gesture; never automatic, never required; completes well under a second);
- a proposed front indicator that a custom (non-default) chain is active.

The front must stand alone: group-hunt and one-time users flip once and go back. The app opens on the front (the default mode is open, O-5).

### 3.2 Recorder mode: the inside

What you see when you flip the recorder over and take the back off: the signal moving through the components. Not a settings page, not a diagnostics screen. Rules that stand:

- **Honesty of motion.** A component animates only from a number the engine returned for the current block (the meters, section 4.7). Nothing moves for effect. Stages not modeled are **labelled empty sockets** ("not yet modeled") with their source and assumption IDs; the sockets are the visible work list.
- **Two paths.** While recording, the record path lights left to right into the flash and the speaker is visibly greyed, "muted while recording" (**device**, S-002). During playback the flash, decoder, speed stage, playback filter, volume and the selected output light; the record path is dark. Recording state and Stop stay reachable on the back; the flip never interrupts a recording.
- **Status header**, from the engine's stage manifest: "Modeled on the service manual and codec datasheets. 0 of 15 stages checked against a real unit." plus links to the evidence form and the registers. It counts stages, not features.
- **Status badges** per stage, distinguished by shape and label, not colour alone: *Sourced*, *Assumed* (with confidence low / medium / high), *Not modeled*, *Checked against a real unit* (applies to no stage today). Never "verified".
- **Tap to inspect.** Tapping a component raises a card: (1) one neutral sentence on what the stage does to sound (from the manifest), (2) the live read-out, large, (3) source and assumption IDs, confidence, and the model version that last changed the stage, (4) the stage's controls, where the tier allows (section 5), each card with one "device default" button; a dot on a component marks it off the device default. The manifest's "not modeled here" note must be shown beside the description, labelled as the gap.
- **No global controls** on the board; every control lives in its component's card.
- **The scope** (waveform) is the riskiest element in this category, because a waveform is read as evidence: keep it calm, labelled with level and time, and show no marks other than the engine's own splice and ceiling events.
- After a period without touch the inside drops to a low frame rate (sessions run an hour or more on battery).
- **Accessibility:** Reduce Motion replaces the flip with a crossfade; nothing flashes faster than about three times per second; meters move with ballistics and indicators latch rather than blink; each component is one accessible element with its name, badge and a throttled value; a **flat text list** mirrors the board (it is also the store-review fallback).
- The inside is the second App Store screenshot, with the status header visible.
- Rendering is procedural from one style sheet (no commissioned illustration before the first reviews).

Workbench additions to the inside (section 5): "Listen here" at a stage, bypass switches and model-internal knobs in the cards.

### 3.3 Investigation mode

A field instrument: audio through the same chain, plus synchronized phone-sensor tracks, on one elapsed timeline. Phone sensors only (magnetometer, motion, barometer, optional location and heading); no Bluetooth meters, no multi-phone sessions.

**The 1.0 scope** (decision 2026-10-29) is the smaller one: plain sensor tracks (the vibration track included), manual marks (MARK and "Question asked here"), one timeline and a versioned case file. Automatic marks, vibration-track marks and the "Inside this moment" cards move to 1.1.

The session flow and its screens:

| Phase | Screen elements |
|---|---|
| Before | Name the case; a sensor check (magnetometer "settling → ready"); location opt-in per session; a one-time neutral note on recording other people. |
| During (live) | Large **REC** and **MARK** buttons; a **"Question asked here"** button (one tap, its own shape); the audio scope; three labelled readouts (magnetic field in µT, acceleration in g, pressure in hPa, each with its sensor's accuracy or status); the flip to the inside. The app stays silent: no haptics, no speaker output, so it does not record itself. |
| After | "Close session": a summary card (elapsed, recorded and paused time; marks by kind), then immediate playback through the modeled speaker. |
| Review | **One cursor** across the audio, the sensor tracks, the vibration track and the marks; voice-activation gaps shown as explicit timing metadata, never compressed away; marks labelled "Marked by you" (MARK, Question asked here) with a distinct shape per kind; the flip to the inside at that moment. (1.1: a mark opens "Inside this moment", a card per stage printing that moment's engine numbers.) |
| Share | The **case file**: one zip document with its own extension: the processed audio as WAV, the original microphone audio only if opted in on each share, `tracks.csv`, `markers.json`, `notes.txt`, the stored recording, its processing record (section 4.9), `session.json` and a `README.txt`. Readable without the app. No hosted links, accounts or gallery in the first release. |

Elements and rules the design must carry:

- **Original microphone audio is kept for every recording in both modes** (24-bit lossless, about 250 – 350 MB/h, deleted with the recording; decision 2026-10-29). Where the user sees and deletes it, and the storage cost, are UI matters to design.
- **The vibration track**: the accelerometer's vibration level, recorded only while the phone lies still; while handled it shows "phone moving". Worded as a reading in g with a time window, as context next to a mark, never as a detection. It invites the user to put the phone down.
- **Readings** are named by what the sensor measures, with unit and status. The magnetometer is a steady-field meter in µT, not a mains or RF meter; the phone moves its own reading (haptics, speaker, charging, cases), which the app logs as context.
- Alignment is shown as a **window, never an instant**; "synchronized" and "precise" are not used until measured.
- **Atmosphere never animates from sensor values.** Measurements and theatre are visually distinct.
- The interface treats a coincidence as something to examine and never labels it proof of a paranormal cause, nor the opposite.
- Investigation mode offers **voice-activation presets labelled "units differ"**, including one that keeps recording in a quiet room, beside the factory default (decision 2026-10-29).
- Words never used in marks or readings: activity, spike, anomaly, hit, response, hotspot, presence, energy, detected, voice.

## 4. What the engine can do today (0.22.0)

Grouped the way a user meets them. The app embeds the engine (a Swift package wrapping an XCFramework; later an Android archive); a version check runs once at launch, and a mismatch is a build error of the app, never a user-facing condition.

### 4.1 Record, play, memory

- **States:** idle, recording or playing, never two at once; the speaker is muted while recording (**device**, S-002). The one-pass "monitor" path (microphone straight to the output, no memory) is refused while either is active.
- **Memory:** 60 minutes and 99 recordings, counted in stored device time (**device**, S-001). Recording **stops by itself at the exact sample the memory fills** and keeps the partial recording; a start is refused when no time or no slot remains. Recordings are numbered in the order made; **erase** returns a recording's time and renumbers the later ones; **erase all** empties the memory. A larger memory is a setting the device does not have (**emulator**; its tier is O-2).
- **Voice activation (VAS)** pauses the recording while the input stays below a threshold and resumes when sound returns (**device**, S-001; the values **modeled**, A-022 – A-025). The device has no VAS switch. Its character: pauses longer than the hang time (1 s default) vanish, the first 20 ms of each sound after a pause is lost, the remaining audio joins at abrupt splices. Each processing call reports whether the gate is paused and every splice (position and removed length), so a timeline can show the gaps.
- **Drop vs mute** (**emulator**): drop mode, the default and the device's behaviour, removes paused audio so the recording is shorter; mute mode keeps the timeline and stores silence instead (it costs memory). Both make the same decisions and report where they acted.
- **Playback** runs the recording, then a short settled-silence drain (about 90 ms); progress is available as the place reached in the recording and the total length for a constant speed. There is **no seek and no 10 SEC REVIEW**: the device's S11 control is not modeled; scrubbing a waveform is an app feature on the app's own copy of the audio, not an engine call.
- **Export and import:** a recording as an opaque, versioned, checksummed byte sequence; it imports into another engine at any host rate with bit-identical playback. Recordings made from now on stay importable after engine updates.
- **Latency** is fixed and reported per configuration (about 40 ms at 48 kHz with the speech coder on); the engine is deterministic (same input, settings and seed give bit-identical output on every platform) and real-time safe on the recording and playback calls.

### 4.2 Device controls (free)

| Control | Range, default | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Speaker or earphone** (route) | speaker / earphone; speaker | playback, live | Each route has its own sound (**modeled**: the small-speaker template A-010 is low confidence; the earphone's low-frequency cut A-032). The earphone route is quieter; the relative loudness of the two is left to the app. **device**: the earphone jack exists (S-001). |
| **Volume** | 0 … 1; the reference position ≈ 0.13 | playback, live | A knob position. Above the reference position, loud passages clip in the speaker amplifier (**modeled**, A-031). A change moves the knob over **20 ms**, starting and ending at rest (the continuity is the device's; the 20 ms is an **engine convention**, A-045). A dragged control should send at most one position per screen frame; a stream of updates makes the knob lag. The device has no default position. |
| **Playback speed** | levels 1 – 5; 3 = normal | playback only, live | Five levels (**device**, S-001 "SPEED CONTROL"; "+/-40%" from a product page, S-012). The ratios 0.6 / 0.8 / 1 / 1.2 / 1.4 and the **pitch moving with the speed** are **modeled** (A-054, low confidence; the device may keep pitch, which is the planned option before 0.90.0). A level change is a 20 ms crossfade (**engine convention**); a request during a crossfade lands when it ends. Step one level per gesture; no slider drag. The record path ignores it. |
| **Microphone sensitivity** | 1 … 5; 3 | record, live | Sets how quiet the input must be before recording pauses, 3 dB per level (**device** control, S-001; the step and threshold **modeled**, A-022). On the device it is set while stopped; changing it during a recording is an emulator feature. |

### 4.3 Unit conditions (free)

Audible differences between two real units or over one unit's life. Set by the user, never by the phone.

| Control | Range, default | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hiss level** | −18 … +12 dB; 0 | Raises or lowers the microphone capsule's steady hiss (about −62 dBFS by default, **modeled**, A-048, low confidence; unit-to-unit spread unknown). Lower it when the phone's own microphone adds its floor. Live during recording with a 20 ms glide. |
| **Battery voltage** | 2.2 … 3.0 V; 3.0 V | The played unit's cell voltage: lower cells lower the speaker amplifier's clip ceiling (**modeled** on the circuit and the amplifier's datasheet, A-056). Only loud speaker playback changes; the earphone route and recording do not. **Never read from the phone's battery, a sensor or a timer**; the engine never drains it. Preset data the app may use (each value an engine constant): "Fresh (as modeled)" 3.0 V, "Half used (4 h, 2.52 V)", "Nearly flat (10 h, 2.22 V)", "NiMH pair (2.45 V)". Never "dying", "drained" or anything tying the value to activity. |

### 4.4 Model internals (Workbench)

Values the device has that its user cannot reach. One line each, from the control manifest; all **modeled**, low confidence, live during recording through the schedule.

- **Limiter threshold** (−40 … +3 dBFS; −1): the level above which the limiter holds sound down; lowered, ordinary speech is limited and pumps.
- **Limiter attack** (1 … 50 ms; 3): how long a loud onset passes at full level before the limiter acts.
- **Limiter release** (200 … 10 000 ms; 1370): how long the level stays down after a loud sound.
- **Pause threshold** (−60 … 0 dBFS; −40): the pause threshold at the factory sensitivity level; the other levels step from it.
- **Pause delay** (50 … 10 000 ms; 1000): how long the input must stay quiet before recording pauses.
- **Resume delay** (0 … 200 ms; 20): how much of the first sound after a pause is lost before recording resumes.

### 4.5 Emulator options

Things the device cannot do. Each must be labelled as the emulator's, outside the device drawing or marked "(emulator)". Tier: Workbench, except where a curated free preset sets one (section 5) and except the host conveniences the app uses internally.

- **Stage switches**, each bypassing one modeled stage: microphone hiss (stage 1), microphone amplifier (2), limiter (3), codec input filter (4), converter clipping (4), voice-activated pause (5), speech coder (7 and 9, one switch for the pair; needs an empty memory), codec output filter (10), volume and power amplifier (11), speaker (12), earphone output (13). A bypassed engine is **not a device configuration**; a bypassed stage's branch is "stage N bypassed (emulator)", never "raw", "clean" or "the true signal".
- **Live playback-stage bypass** (stages 10 – 13): switchable **while a recording plays**, as a 20 ms blend with the stage kept running, so an A/B is seamless. Stages 1 – 9 are record-side: they change between recordings, and a "with / without" comparison of a past recording is an offline re-render from the kept microphone audio (section 4.9). Loudness rule: bypassing stage 11 removes the volume control, so the level jumps unless the volume sits at a neutral position (the reference position on the speaker route, position 1 on the earphone route); the app must move the volume first, compensate, or confirm. While stage 11 is bypassed the volume and battery controls change nothing audible: grey them.
- **Pause handling** drop / mute (section 4.1).
- **Memory length and maximum recordings** above the device's (section 4.1; tier O-2).
- **Listen point** (tap): in the one-pass monitor path only, which stage's output is heard; recording and playback ignore it. "Listen here" **during playback of a stored recording is not possible today** (the scope-copy request E-B is unbuilt); "Listen here" on a past recording is a re-render.
- **Output clamp** (off by default): bounds the returned audio to full scale; a host convenience the app uses when writing integer audio, shown to the user only as a count when on.
- **Hiss seed and host sample rate**: host parameters the app sets; not user controls.

### 4.6 Presets

The engine ships no presets; names live in the app, every value an engine constant or a control value. Presets and chains, whether free, purchased or restored from a session, are **declarative data validated against the control manifest**, never scripts (App Store guideline 2.5.2). Allowed names describe the signal ("Device as modeled", "Tired batteries (2.4 V)", "Noisy capsule (+6 dB hiss)", "Codec only", "No voice activation (emulator)"); never "EVP mode", "Spirit", "Clear voices", "Enhanced", "Sensitive to activity".

### 4.7 Meters: what the inside can show live

One read-out per processing call, thirteen figures computed from the same values the output is computed from, real-time safe, with no effect on the sound. Every moving element on the inside draws from these.

| Figure | Stage | What a card can show |
|---|---|---|
| Input peak | input | input level |
| Limiter-input peak | 2 | the level entering the limiter (can exceed full scale: the amplifier limits softly near +9 / +17 dBFS) |
| Gain reduction (dB) | 3 | the limiter's deepest attenuation in the call: a gain-reduction meter |
| Hold charge (0 – 1) | 3 | "limiter hold", with a capacitor glyph: how far the hold capacitor is charged; rises on loud sound, drains over about ten seconds. Landmarks: 0.13 unity floor, 0.73 one dB, 0.84 ten, 0.90 twenty |
| Record-filter peak | 4 | the level before the converter's clamp |
| Converter-clamp count | 4 | a clip indicator (device samples clipped this call) |
| Voice-activation state | 5 | "recording / paused", never "voice detected" |
| Hang remaining | 5 | a countdown to the pause (device samples ÷ 6000 = seconds) |
| Reconstruction peak | 10 | output level before the output chain |
| Route peak | 12 / 13 | the selected route's level; above 1.0 reads "above the amplifier's clip ceiling (modeled)", never "overload" |
| Output-clamp count | — | an emulator option's count: show outside the device drawing or only when on |
| Amplifier clip count | 11 | the speaker amplifier's knee or ceiling engaged (host samples this call); 0 while stage 11 is bypassed ("bypassed", never "no clipping") |
| Battery ceiling factor | 15 | the clip ceiling in force (1.0 at 3.0 V) |

Also readable per call: samples produced, whether the gate paused, codec frames completed and the frame being heard ("encoding" / "frames", never an "activity" indicator), the place reached in the recording, the memory status, the engine state. The record-side figures describe samples that reach the output up to the path's latency later; the inside should expect that lead.

### 4.8 The change schedule and the change log (free)

- Any live control (the four device and unit-condition controls, the eight record-side values, the four playback-stage switches) can be **scheduled at an exact sample position** in a stream's clock, and lands there whatever the audio buffer size (**emulator convention**: the device's knobs move when a hand moves them).
- **Every change a stream applies**, scheduled or set plainly, is reported as a **change entry**: position, control, value before, value after, and a **tag** the app chose (so the app matches entries to its own presets, marks and gestures). Entries chain: each one's "before" is the previous one's "after".
- Record-side entries sit in the **input clock** (microphone samples since the recording started), so they align with the kept microphone audio sample for sample; playback-side entries in the playback's output clock.
- Re-scheduling a log's entries reproduces the stream bit for bit, which is what session replay, presets and "what was applied when" rest on. The timeline's change marks come from the engine's log, not from what the app sent.

### 4.9 The processing record and the re-render (free)

- For every recording the engine writes a **processing record**: one plain-text document beside the exported recording (never inside it) with the engine's identity (library and per-stage model versions), the host rate, the seed and recording counter, every setting as it was at the recording's start, the run (the record-side change entries, the voice-activation gaps with the microphone-sample position each aligns with, the totals, how the recording ended) and a 64-bit digest tying the record to its stored form. It holds **no time, place, device identity, recording number or app data**; the case file holds those.
- **The re-render:** an engine built from the record, fed the kept microphone audio with the record's entries scheduled, reproduces the recording **bit for bit** at the record's library version. It describes how the recording was processed; it does not vouch for what the input contains. The published wording: "This recording re-renders identically from the included microphone audio with these settings (model 0.x)." The digest is "consistent, not signed".
- A reader reports differences as **flags, never refusals** ("written by library 0.20.0; this library is 0.22.0"; "stage 5's model version differs"; "the record and recording N do not belong together"), so a record stays readable for ever.
- **Timeline maps** place a kept sample on the microphone clock through every removed gap (a window of about −22 … +30 ms with the speech coder on, a convention, not a measured onset), a microphone position on the recording or the gap that removed it, and a playback output sample on the place in the recording under any speed schedule. Show an alignment as a window.
- A full-state re-render runs from the item's start: roughly 0.5 – 1 min per hour of audio on a phone (a guess; not measured). A per-frame meter track stored live (about 7 MB/h) is the recommended companion for the review timeline.

### 4.10 The stage manifest and the control manifest

Two constant, self-describing tables an app renders directly, needing no engine instance, so the inside's cards, the About view and every control's label are generated from the engine and cannot drift from its registers.

- **Stage manifest**, 15 entries under a header (library version, 6 kHz device rate, counts by status, one basis sentence, three links): per stage its number and stable identifier (the localization key), name, one sentence on what the stage does to sound **on the device**, status (not modeled / assumed / sourced / checked against a real unit), path (record / playback / both), confidence, source and assumption IDs, a **"not modeled here"** note (the gap; a card must label both sentences), the engine controls that touch it, and the model version that last changed it.
- **Control manifest**, 30 entries: identifier (the localization key), name, **kind** (device control, unit condition, model internal, emulator option, host parameter), **timing** (live; idle, applied between recordings; idle and memory-dependent; host-fixed), paths, stage, value type (switch, integer, continuous, choice with options), range, default, unit, S-/A- IDs, and one sentence describing what changing it does in the default configuration. Join a control to its stage's badge by stage number.
- The strings are short, neutral, English; the app localizes by identifier. The denylist the engine's tests enforce on its strings is the same vocabulary rule the UI copy follows (section 8).

### 4.11 What the engine does not do (so the UI does not assume it)

- No seek, scrub or 10 SEC REVIEW on a stored recording (section 4.1); no "Listen here" at a stage during playback (E-B unbuilt); no limiter ceiling event list (E-E unbuilt).
- No effects beyond the device (EQ, reverb, flanging): the pipeline is fixed; stages can be bypassed, not inserted, moved or removed. The dossier's workbench of added components (concept 03) needs an architecture decision not yet made; a UI for it is a proposal.
- No pitch-kept slow playback yet (the planned option before 0.90.0).
- Noise reduction (stage 6) is not modeled: no manufacturer document lists it and the circuit has none. The clock (14) is not modeled. The power supply (15) is modeled only as the battery's effect on the speaker amplifier; start-of-item transients, beeps and the low-battery cut-off are not modeled.
- The engine keeps no raw microphone audio, no time, no sensors and no files: those are the app's.
- Two threads never share an engine: UI gestures are queued into the audio thread.

## 5. Free tier versus Workbench

The rule is by **parameter kind** (decision 2026-10-29), an app rule on the control manifest's kind field:

| Kind | Tier |
|---|---|
| Device controls (route, volume, playback speed, sensitivity) | **free** |
| Unit conditions (hiss level, battery voltage) | **free** |
| Model internals (the six limiter and pause values) | **Workbench** |
| Emulator options (stage switches incl. the live playback bypass, pause handling, listen point, expanded memory) | **Workbench** (expanded memory's placement is O-2) |

Also free, for good: both modes, sensor sessions, watching the internals (meters, badges, cards' read-outs and sources), the device's limits (60 min / 99 items), the change schedule and log, the processing record and re-render, the speed crossfade, the kept microphone audio, sharing one recording and the case file. **Everything shipped free in 1.0 stays free; later purchases only add components.**

**Curated presets rule:** a curated free preset may set **any** value, model internals and emulator options included (for example Investigation mode's "units differ" voice-activation presets, one of which keeps recording in a quiet room). The Workbench sells the knobs, not the values.

The Workbench's commercial form is open (O-2): one unlock, or several packages of complementary components (EQ, reverb, flanging, unusual processing) with example chains and presets, presented in the same back-panel language. No ads, no consumables, no subscription at launch (O-6), no analytics or advertising SDK, privacy labels "data not collected". Old Workbench items whose home is open: expanded memory, stage taps and "Listen here", stem export, folders and search, review tools.

## 6. Table stakes, App Review and review tools

- **The category's table stakes, which 1.0 should meet** (O-17): raw microphone capture, voice activation, instant playback, a waveform to scrub, a session library with tags, trim, export. Silence removal is the device's own VAS.
- **App Review 4.2 (minimum functionality):** a record button with a filter is at risk; the app must feel like a full instrument. **4.3 (spam / lookalikes):** a reviewer should see the difference within about 30 seconds (the flip, the stage statuses). Common fixable rejections: crashes, a missing privacy policy, broken links, digital goods sold outside in-app purchase. First submission; review readiness is a design concern.
- **Permissions:** microphone; location optional, off by default; Motion & Fitness likely needed for the barometer (to confirm). Each permission string names what the app does with the data.
- **Review-tool candidates** (tier open, O-2): **reverse playback** and a **spectrogram**; earlier candidates an A-B loop, a band filter and a zoomable waveform. Slow playback is the device's own speed control, free. Name tools by what they do ("band filter"), never by what a user might hope they do.
- **Investigation marks:** MARK and "Question asked here" (both the user's, each its own shape); the vibration track as context (section 3.3). The engine's own marks on any timeline read "Emulated VAS resumed (threshold −40 dBFS)", "splice", "ceiling"; never "event", "voice", "activity".
- **Live monitoring while recording** is not a device behaviour (the speaker is muted); if offered it is a labelled Workbench extra through the earphone route.
- **Trademark:** "Panasonic", "RR-DR60" and "DR60" appear in no app name, icon or anything confusingly similar; the device is named once in the store description with a trademark notice.

## 7. Open decisions

Undecided as of 2026-10-31. The app's open questions (O-1 – O-6) are **reopened at the start of app work**, with the product specialist first. Present options; do not assume. Wording below is from brief.md, condensed where marked.

- **O-1 ★ Which controls are the free "intended user adjustments"?** Answered in principle by the four kinds (section 5: device controls plus unit conditions); the question's remaining part is whether any model internal joins the free set, and how the free presets are presented.
- **O-2 ★ The paid boundary and its shape.** One workbench unlock or several experience packs; future pack access; store presentation. Where each item of the old Workbench list now sits: expanded memory beyond 60 minutes and 99 recordings; stage taps and "Listen here"; stem export; folders and search; review tools (reverse playback and a spectrogram added 2026-10-21).
- **O-3 ★ Raw microphone audio.** *Decided 2026-10-29:* kept for every recording in both modes. Still open from the question: how it interacts with voice activation, and where the user sees and deletes it; the privacy labels follow from the answer.

## 8. The claims policy for every string

Neutral and technical. Describe what the signal chain does to sound: band-limiting, hiss, limiting, voice-activated pausing, codec artifacts, speaker response. Never claim the recorder or the app captures, enhances, reveals or communicates anything paranormal; never deny or debunk it either; the app's own view is not stated. Say **"modeled on"** until a stage is checked against a real unit; never "authentic", "identical", "measured", "closely matches", "validated", "verified" (use "checked against a real unit"). Never "capture", "reveal", "enhance", "communicate"; never "fake", "debunk", "entertainment only" (O-16 pending). No "activity", "detects", "senses", "listening for", "signal present", "anomaly", "spike", "event" as a label, "voice detected", "overload", "raw", "clean", "the true signal". Competitors' wording ("spirit communication", "responses from ghosts", "Holy Grail of EVP devices") is not reused. Marketing may say the RR-DR60 is the recorder many investigators used and that this app reproduces its sound.

Three strings that pass:

1. "Modeled on the Panasonic RR-DR60's signal chain, stage by stage, from its service manual and codec datasheets. 0 of 15 stages checked against a real unit."
2. "Voice activation (VAS): recording. 3 pauses removed, 12 s. Pauses while the input stays below −40 dBFS for longer than 1 s; the first 20 ms after each pause is lost. Modeled on the owner's manual (A-008, A-022)."
3. "Speaker amplifier at 2.4 V: loud passages clip 6 dB lower than at fresh cells. Quiet passages are unchanged. Modeled on the circuit and the amplifier's datasheet, not measured (A-056)."
