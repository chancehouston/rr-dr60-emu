# Assumption Register

When exact hardware specifications are unknown, we make **reasonable, documented assumptions**. This register is the single place they live.

**Rules:**
- Every assumption has a stable ID (`A-###`). Never reuse or renumber an ID. Mark superseded ones instead.
- Code, tests, and specs cite the ID wherever the assumed value is used (e.g. `// A-001`).
- **Confidence:** **High** (strongly implied by primary sources), **Medium** (typical for this class of device or era, consistent with sources), **Low** (an educated guess, a placeholder until evidence arrives).
- **Status:** `assumed` → `verified` (with documentary evidence, `S-###` only; never write `verified` for a measurement) or `measured` (confirmed by a measurement of a real unit; cite the capture) or `revised` (link the new value) or `superseded`; `adopted` marks an assumption the emulator took on in a spec. The stage manifest's cross-check ([spec 007](../../specs/007-stage-manifest/spec.md) FR-004, FR-006) reads this table as follows: a row has exactly seven cells and no `|` inside a cell; the Status cell's **first word** decides its class (`superseded`, `verified`, `measured`, otherwise open); a `verified` cell may name only `S-###` IDs, and any `A-###` in it makes the row read as **open** (part of the value is still assumed; put cross-references in the Assumption or Rationale column); the words `adopted` or `revised` **anywhere** in the Status cell mark the row as adopted, so it must be cited by a modeled stage; `superseded` anywhere but first, or a `verified` cell that names a unit, a capture or a measurement, is a parse failure; the first High/Medium/Low word in the Confidence cell is the headline confidence.
- Changing an assumed value changes emulator output. Note it in [CHANGELOG.md](../../CHANGELOG.md).

| A-028 | AGC | The AGC is a **peak-detecting limiter** at the codec input: Q6, a half-wave detector on the codec input-amp output (GSX) through the R13/R17 divider against the AIN+ reference, charges C6 (1 µF) through R12 (390 Ω); C6 discharges through R11 (10 MΩ) and drives, through the emitter follower Q5, the shunt transistor Q4 that attenuates the node after R7 (22 kΩ). **Knee −1 dBFS ± 2 dB** relative to the codec's full scale (A-030), about 1 dB wide (an exponential base–emitter law); **ratio effectively unlimited** (a hard ceiling at the knee); **unity gain below the knee** (no regulation, no noise pumping); the loop is closed (the detector senses after the attenuator). **Reinterpreted (spec 005)**: R12·C6 (0.4 ms) only limits the detector's peak charge current, and R11·C6 (10 s) is the time constant of the **arming decay** (a new loud onset is limited within about 0.5 ms for roughly 3 s after a loud sound, within about 1 ms for the next few seconds, and the dead time is back to about two thirds of its full value after 10 s), not the release; the audible attack is a charge dead time (A-034) and the audible release about 1.4 s (A-035). The emulator limits the attenuation to 40 dB (engineering target; the circuit can attenuate more, but the codec input amp's rail and then the mic amp clip first, A-029). **The detector is modeled with a hard threshold.** 0.6 V is Q6's base–emitter voltage at the attack's milliampere charge peaks; at the detector's steady-state current (about 0.14 µA average, what the discharge draws at 10 dB of attenuation) a small-signal PNP sits at 0.42–0.54 V, which puts the sustained ceiling about 2 dB lower (the post-implementation review of 2026-10-11, B1: −0.8 dBFS for the model's own saturation current, +0.2 at a tenth of it, −2.0 at ten times it). **The default knee is therefore −1 dBFS: sustained loud input is held just below full scale as a clean sine, and only the onset clips; the hard-threshold value (+1 dBFS, flat-topped by the codec for about 30 % of each cycle) is the upper end of the ±2 dB. Which branch a real unit shows is the most audible open question; the deciding capture is the waveform of a tone held at a fixed loud level.** A single hard threshold cannot be right for both phases; an exponential detector (a soft knee, where a tone 1–2 dB below the knee charges the capacitor slowly rather than never) is deferred until a capture shows one (a 30 s tone 1–2 dB below the knee: a slow sag means a soft knee).
