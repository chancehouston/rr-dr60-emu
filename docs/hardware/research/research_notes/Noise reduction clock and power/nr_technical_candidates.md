# Noise reduction in the RR-DR60's speech DSP (MN1931712BB): candidate mechanisms, period evidence and capture tests

Research notes, 2026-10-17. Scope: what "noise reduction" a mid-1990s Panasonic speech-coding DSP running a 4 kbit/s CELP at 6 kHz (after the VAS gate) could plausibly have run, how each candidate sounds, and how a capture from a real unit would tell them apart. ## 1. What is the MN1931712BB? (Panasonic / Matsushita speech DSPs, 1994–1999)

### Takeaway
No datasheet for the MN1931712 was found, but the **1997 Panasonic Semiconductor Selection Guide** (bitsavers) documents the **MN1930 series** of 3 V, 16-bit fixed-point DSPs (MN1932801, MN1933211) whose numbering, supply range, PLL clocking and TQFP-100 package match what S-002 shows for IC2; their figures (27 / 21.7 ns machine cycle, so about **37–46 MIPS**, a double-speed 16 × 16 MAC with 40-bit accumulation, 6K words data RAM, 30K words data ROM, 28–32K × 24-bit instruction ROM) are the best available bound on what the RR-DR60's DSP could compute. The same guide shows Panasonic's MN19xx DSPs as the "Speech Signal Processor" of its Japanese digital mobile phone (TDMA, 0.9/1.5 GHz band: PDC) reference designs. The guide lists **no noise-suppression or VAD function** for any MN19xx part (they are programmable DSPs; such functions would be firmware).

### Cited Findings

The MN1930 series rows are **MN1932801** (27 ns, 2.7–3.3 V, TQFP100-P-1414, "Double speed MAC built-in", 6K RAM, 30K data ROM) and **MN1933211** (marked "under development"; 32K × 24-bit ROM, 6K RAM, 30K data ROM, 21.7 ns, 2.7–3.3 V, TQFP100). ### Inferences
- By Panasonic's numbering (MN193xxxx alongside MN1932801 and MN1933211) the MN1931712 is very likely a **mask-ROM member of the 3 V MN1930 series**, i.e. a device of roughly the MN1932801's class: at the RR-DR60's 3.2–3.3 V, about 27–30 ns per instruction, **≈ 33–37 MIPS** with a single-cycle (double-speed) MAC. The 8 MHz resonator × PLL factor N would set the actual rate; N = 4 gives 32 MHz (31 ns), N = 5 gives 40 MHz (25 ns), within the MN1932801's 60/N–74/N MHz input window. This is an inference from family resemblance, not a documented fact.
- Budget implication: a G.723.1/G.729-class encoder plus decoder is commonly quoted at roughly 15–25 MIPS on such DSPs (not verified in this pass), so a 37-MIPS part leaves on the order of **10+ MIPS of headroom** — enough for a 256-point-FFT spectral-subtraction suppressor (estimated about 1–2 MIPS, section 3) — but only one of encode or decode runs at a time in a recorder, which loosens the budget further. Compute is therefore **not** a reason to rule out a spectral-domain suppressor; memory (6K words data RAM shared with the coder state and the flash buffering) is the tighter constraint, and a 256-point suppressor needs roughly 1–1.5K words.
- Bit-reverse addressing and the max/min instruction in the MN1930 feature list are exactly the primitives an FFT and a minimum-tracking noise estimator need; their presence proves capability, not use.
- The MN19xx parts' role in PDC phones means Matsushita's speech DSP firmware lineage was the PDC coders (VSELP 11.2 kbit/s full rate, PSI-CELP 5.6 kbit/s half rate), which is exactly what Morii's 1995 noise-reduction patent (section 2) names as its target.

### Gaps
- No MN1931712 datasheet, ROM-code description or Panasonic press release was found; its core clock, PLL factor in the RR-DR60, ROM contents and any firmware feature list are unknown. Leads: the PDF behind the SeekIC listing; the 1997 *Microcomputer Family* Selection Guide on bitsavers (a first download returned an error page and was not retried); Panasonic's 1998–1999 selection guides (not online); Nuvoton (Panasonic Semiconductor's successor) for legacy data.
- Whether the MN1931712 is a mask-ROM MN1932801 / MN1933211 derivative is inferred from the numbering only.

## 2. Matsushita / Panasonic patents and papers 1993–2000 on noise reduction, VAD and silence handling for speech coders

### Takeaway
Matsushita had **a fully specified pre-encoder spectral-subtraction "noise canceller" in its own CELP patent family**, contemporary with the device: Morii's 1994–95 filings (US 5,774,846; JP 3183104, priority 1995-07-14) and the **eighteenth embodiment of the Yasunaga / Morii / Ehara November-1996 CELP family** (published as US 6,799,160 "Noise canceller"), with a 256-point FFT on 160-sample (20 ms) frames at 8 kHz, a minimum-tracking noise estimate, over-subtraction, random-phase filling of over-subtracted bins, extra attenuation of noise-only frames and LPC-based spectral emphasis. Nothing found ties it to the RR-DR60 or to any recorder; it was framed for digital mobile phones. No Matsushita patent on recorder-specific silence compression, comfort noise or playback squelch of the era was found.

### Cited Findings
- **US 5,774,846** (Matsushita; inventor Toshiyuki Morii), "Speech coding apparatus, linear prediction coefficient analyzing apparatus and noise reducing apparatus"; earliest priority **1994-12-19** (JP), further priorities 1994-12-26 and **1995-07-14**; filed 1995-11-20; granted 1998-06-30. Its noise reducing apparatus (fourth embodiment, FIG. 17) is spectral subtraction: DFT of each frame, subtraction of an estimated noise spectrum, inverse transform with the original phase; the noise estimate per frequency is the **minimum of the current and a predetermined number of past input spectra** ("works whether or not speech is present"); a stabilizing coefficient and a noise-reducing coefficient set the subtraction amount; over-subtracted bins are compensated from the previous output spectrum; an LPC-based spectrum emphasizing unit and frame-to-frame waveform matching follow; the reducer works "before the speech information is coded". Applications named: digital portable telephones. — [Google Patents US5774846](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5774846)
- **JP 3183104 B2** (Morii; Matsushita), "Noise reduction device", filed **1995-07-14**, granted 2001-07-03: A/D, LPC analysis, DFT (**256-point FFT, 160-sample frames at 8 kHz plus 60-sample look-ahead**), noise spectrum tracked with **two candidate values per frequency and their persistence counts** (so it estimates noise during speech), subtraction scaled by a noise-reduction coefficient (example setting **20.0**, lowered when no noise is detected), negative bins replaced by the smaller of the input and the previous frame's spectrum, input phase for the inverse transform, then LPC pole emphasis, high-frequency emphasis, power emphasis and triangular-window overlap with the previous frame; an optional memory-saving mode shares one noise value across four adjacent bins. Background cites VSELP (11.2 kbit/s) and PSI-CELP (5.6 kbit/s); evaluated in front of a CELP encoder/decoder; applications: mobile phones, multimedia communication, voice guidance. No recorders mentioned. — [Google Patents JP3183104B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP3183104B2/en)
- **US 6,799,160 B2** "Noise canceller" (Matsushita; Yasunaga, Morii, Ehara), a continuation in the family with earliest priority **1996-11-07**, filed 2001-04-30 — the same family the project cites as S-010 (EP 1 136 985 etc.). Its **eighteenth mode (FIG. 27)** is a noise canceller placed ahead of the speech coder's buffer, with Table 10 settings: frame **160 samples (20 ms at 8 kHz)**, pre-read **80 samples (10 ms)**, **256-point FFT**, LPC order 10, "sustaining number" **30** frames for the noise-spectrum candidates, designated minimum power 20.0, noise reference power 20000.0, unvoiced-segment detection coefficient 0.05, designated noise-cancellation coefficient **1.5**, learning coefficient 0.8, **unvoiced-segment power reduction coefficient 0.3**, compensation power increase coefficient 2.0, **5** consecutive noise references, power enhancement coefficient 1.2; the cancellation coefficient storage starts at 20.0. Rules: noise estimation runs when input power < 0.05 × the maximum power, or the cancellation coefficient > 1.7, or input power < 1.6 × the mean noise power; a candidate older than 30 frames is replaced by the second candidate × 1.4; the mean noise is smoothed with 0.9 (input above half the mean noise) or 0.5; subtraction of mean noise × coefficient, negative bins replaced by the compensation spectrum × coefficient; noise-only/unvoiced frames get a stabilization step (Process 1 for the first 5 consecutive noise frames, Process 2 after, smoothing 0.8/0.2 and 0.5/0.5); **compensated bins are given random phase** (a random-phase table indexed by a counter stepping by 2, reset at 16); spectral enhancement uses three MA/AR/high-frequency coefficient sets for voiced (0.6/0.7/0.3), unvoiced (0.64/0.66/0) and noise-only (0.8/0.5/0.4) frames; output is cross-faded with the previous frame by a triangular window over the pre-read length. Claims 1–6 cover this structure. — [Google Patents US6799160B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6799160B2/en)
- The November-1996 family (EP 1 136 985 B1, application filed 1997-11-06, earliest priority JP 1996-11-07, further priorities 1996-11-21 and 1997-02-19) is a CELP coder/decoder patent; the noise canceller appears there as one embodiment among the coder embodiments (the fetched part of the page showed only the coder). — [Google Patents EP1136985B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP1136985B1)
- Negative / off-topic Matsushita hits: **EP 0 700 166** "Noise suppressing device" (Nohara, Kane; priority 1994-09-02) is a radio-tuner noise suppressor driven by carrier level and modulation degree, not speech-coder NR. — [EPO EP0700166 A3](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.2/patents/EP0700166NWA3/document.html). **US 7,054,808 B2** "Noise suppressing apparatus and noise suppressing method" (Matsushita; Koji Yoshida) was surfaced by search but not read; its priority date was not checked. — [Google Patents US7054808B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7054808B2)
- Later (flag: after the device): Ehara et al., IEICE Trans. - A Matsushita search hit for a "digital compressed sound recorder" with CELP (US 5,675,333) is actually **U.S. Philips** (priority 1994-08-31) and mentions no silence detection or NR (negative result). — [Google Patents US5675333](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5675333)

### Inferences
- The RR-DR60's service manual is © 1998 (S-002), so Morii's 1995 suppressor and the 1996-family noise canceller both **predate the device's firmware** and were in the hands of the same Matsushita speech-coding group whose CELP work S-010 already ties to the device's class. If Panasonic put "noise reduction" in an IC recorder in 1997–98, this design is the most plausible in-house candidate. That is a plausibility argument, not evidence that the device runs it.
- Adapted to the device clock (A-026, 6 kHz), the same structure would most likely keep the sample counts (160 / 80 / 256), stretching to a 26.7 ms frame and 13.3 ms look-ahead, or keep the milliseconds (120 / 60 samples, 256-point FFT with more zero padding). Either fits A-040's open frame-length question; the canceller's look-ahead (80 samples) matches a coder look-ahead, which suggests a shared input buffer.
- Interpreting the parameters: a subtraction factor of 1.5 is 1.76 dB of over-subtraction; the 0.3 "unvoiced-segment power reduction" is −5.2 dB if applied to power or −10.5 dB if applied to amplitude (the text read does not say which); 30 frames × 20 ms = **0.6 s** is the noise-candidate lifetime, i.e. the time scale on which a constant input is re-classified as noise; 5 frames = 100 ms is the switch from Process 1 to Process 2 in pauses.

- The full text of US 5,774,846's FIG. 17 embodiment and of the 1996-family Japanese original (to confirm the canceller was in the 1996 priority text and not added in the 2001 continuation) was not read past the summaries. The claims summary and Table 10 came from the continuation's text.
- J-PlatPat was not searched directly; Japanese-language searches for ICレコーダ / 雑音 / 松下 1996–1999 returned nothing specific. A search on J-PlatPat for Matsushita applications with FI codes G10L21/02 (noise reduction) and keywords 録音再生 / 半導体メモリ / 音声符号化, filed 1995–1998, is the next step.
- No Matsushita patent on a recorder playback squelch, comfort noise for recorders, or silence compression in IC recorders was found (negative result, not exhaustive).

## 3. Candidate mechanisms available to 1990s low-rate CELP firmware: parameters, cost, sound, detection

### Takeaway
Six candidates, in rough order of how much they change the sound: **(a)** a pre-encoder spectral-subtraction / gain-floored suppressor (Matsushita's own design, or an IS-127-like 16-channel one) — steady hiss drops by about 5–20 dB after a ~0.5 s adaptation, with "musical" or warbling residue and suppression of any sustained tone; **(b)** a downward expander / noise gate on low-level PCM — level-dependent, "breathing" floor that rides up under speech; **(c)** VAD + comfort-noise or zeroing — pauses become synthetic noise or silence (largely redundant with the VAS on this device); **(d)** the decoder postfilter — already modeled (A-041), lowers noise between formants and adds tilt, no adaptation transient; **(e)** a DC / rumble high-pass — nearly invisible behind the codec's 225 Hz band edge; **(f)** the codec's own rendering of noise — swirling, frame-rate modulated hiss, present whatever else exists and the main confound in every test.

### Cited Findings
**(a) Spectral-subtraction / Wiener-type suppressor**
- Matsushita design parameters: see section 2 (256-point FFT, 20 ms frames, 10 ms look-ahead, minimum/persistence noise tracking over 30 frames, over-subtraction 1.5, noise-frame power reduction 0.3, random phase in filled bins, LPC-based emphasis). — [US6799160B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6799160B2/en); [JP3183104B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP3183104B2/en)
- **IS-127 (EVRC) noise suppressor**, the first standardized one (TIA/EIA, dated 1995 by one source and 1996 by another): 64-point FFT energy bands grouped into **16 channels**; per-channel gain in dB = slope × (channel SNR − threshold) + a **minimum overall gain (e.g. −13 dB)**, clamped between that minimum and 0 dB; channel energies and a background-noise estimate updated in energy minima / non-voice frames; a "voice metric" from quantized channel SNRs classifies frames as noise or voice (with a spectral-deviation estimator). The 16-channel layout comes from Motorola's earlier US 4,811,404. These details are from patent descriptions of IS-127, not the standard text. — [US 7,555,075 "Adjustable noise suppression system"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7555075); [US 4,811,404 "Noise suppression system"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4811404); [US 7,912,567 "Noise suppressor"](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7912567); dating: [CouthIT EVRC-A page](https://couthit.com/portfolio/evrc/) (1995) vs. [US 6,397,177](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6397177) (1996)
- In TI's EVRC implementation, submodule 1 holds "the input signal preprocessing and all the subroutines associated with noise suppression"; a Hughes design put echo cancelling and noise suppression on a separate TMS320C31. No MIPS figure for the suppressor was found. — [TI SPRA566B, IS-127 EVRC on TMS320C6000](https://www.ti.com/lit/pdf/spra566b); [US 5,533,133 "Noise suppression in digital voice communications systems"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5533133)
- Noise estimation by **minimum statistics** (Martin, EUSIPCO 1994, pp. 1182–1185): noise power estimated from minima of a smoothed power estimate, with no speech-activity detector; minima are biased low and need compensation. Doblinger (Eurospeech 1995) gave a cheaper subband minimum-tracking variant. — [RWTH Aachen IKS publication page](https://www.iks.rwth-aachen.de/publikationen/publications-detail//martin94b/); [Ruhr-Uni Bochum noise power estimation](https://www.ruhr-uni-bochum.de/ika/forschung/forschungsbereich_martin/speech_audio_processing/noise_power.htm)
- **Musical noise**: "little islands of spectrum power … that appear randomly in different frequency bins from frame to frame"; Berouti, Schwartz and Makhoul (ICASSP 1979) introduced over-subtraction (factor ≥ 1, set from the frame SNR) and a **spectral floor**; over-subtraction distorts low-level speech, too little leaves musical noise, and the floor limits the achievable attenuation. Boll (IEEE Trans. ASSP 27(2), 1979) is the founding spectral-subtraction paper. — [US 7,133,824 "Noise reduction method"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7133824); [VOCAL spectral subtraction note](https://vocal.com/noise-reduction/spectral-subtraction/); [US 6,317,709 "Noise suppressor having weighted gain smoothing"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6317709)

**(b) Noise gate / downward expander on PCM**
- The 1995 Dallas DS2132A digital answering-machine processor (9.8 / 4.9 kbit/s) offers "Precise signal level detection capability" and record/playback gain control in 3 dB steps (−9 … +30 dB); its energy-level detector "allows the microcontroller to perform call progress detection and automatic gain control functions". Its feature list has **no noise suppression, VAD or comfort noise** — level decisions are left to the host controller. — [Dallas DS2132A datasheet (1995)](https://www.nyang-tech.com/datasheet/1761322853/Analog-Devices-Inc.-Maxim-Integrated/DS2132A.pdf)
- No period source specific to an expander in a speech DSP was found; parameters below are engineering estimates (see Inferences).

**(c) VAD + DTX + comfort noise / zeroing**
- G.729 (1996) Annex B: VAD, DTX (decides when to update background-noise parameters in non-speech frames) and CNG started by 2-byte (15/16-bit) SID frames; the decoder synthesizes noise from the last SID parameters when nothing is received. — [Wikipedia, G.729](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.729); [US 6,807,525 "SID frame detection with human auditory perception compensation"](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6807525)
- Annex B's SID decision uses the Itakura distance between LPC filters and energy change since the last SID; it is noted as over-sensitive to very-low-level background noise (frequent SID updates). — [US 6,807,525](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6807525)
- G.723.1 Annex A specifies the same VAD/CNG structure for G.723.1 (background level and spectral content); the SID size was not verified in this pass. — patents citing it, e.g. [US 7,092,875 "Speech transcoding method and apparatus for silence compression"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7092875)

**(d) Decoder postfilter**
- Chen and Gersho, "Adaptive Postfiltering for Quality Enhancement of Coded Speech", IEEE Trans. Speech Audio Process. 3(1), 1995, pp. 59–71: a pole-zero long-term postfilter cascaded with a short-term postfilter; the short-term part attenuates noise in the **spectral valleys**, where coding noise is most audible because the SNR is lowest. — [US 6,941,263 "Frequency domain postfiltering for quality enhancement of coded speech"](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6941263) (citing it); [US 7,124,077](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7124077B2)
- Conventional postfilters reduce energy in local spectral valleys proportionally more than at peaks. — [Google Patents US7103539B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7103539B2/en) (priority 2001)
- The emulator already models a short-term postfilter with tilt compensation and gain scaling, and no pitch postfilter (A-041; `crates/rr_dr60/src/codec/postfilter.rs`). — [project assumptions.md, A-041](../../../assumptions.md)

**(e) High-pass pre-filter**
- The codec's own transmit filter already rejects 60 Hz by ≥ 20 dB (26 typ) and the modeled record band is about 225–2550 Hz at 6 kHz (S-005, A-027). — [project sources.md, S-005](../../../sources.md)
- Sony's later IC recorders expose a "low-cut filter" (projector, wind rumble) and an "NCF (Noise Cut)" filter as user settings (flag: modern products, used only as an example of what recorder makers call "noise cut"). — [Sony ICD Help Guide](https://helpguide.sony.net/icd/b14/v1/en/contents/TP0000340730.html)

**(f) The codec's rendering of noise ("swirling")**
- "Swirling" is one of the most severe degradations of stationary background sounds in CELP-type coders, caused by unnatural temporal fluctuations of the decoded power and spectrum from coarse estimation/quantization of the LPC filter and excitation; it lessens as bit rate rises. Pre-1996 fixes include smoothing or bandwidth-expanding LPC parameters during inactivity (US 5,632,004), doing so only for stationary backgrounds (US 5,579,432), and reduced-fluctuation LPC/gain quantizers for inactive speech (US 5,487,087); a VSELP-era patent notes the coders "do a poor job of modelling the signal" with background noise present. — [US 8,457,953 "Method and arrangement for smoothing of stationary background noise"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8457953) (background section); [US 5,633,982 "Removal of swirl artifacts from CELP-based speech coders"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5633982)

| b | Downward expander / gate on PCM | threshold perhaps −50 … −40 dBFS, ratio 1:2 … 1:∞, attack 1–5 ms, release 50–300 ms, maybe hysteresis | < 0.1 MIPS | quiet hiss vanishes or drops; floor "breathes" up with each syllable and decays after; low-level speech tails clipped | **input/output level curve** with a knee (slope > 1 below threshold, 1 above); attenuation depends on absolute level, not on stationarity; a tone below threshold is cut immediately and stays cut, one above passes indefinitely |
| c | VAD + CNG / zeroing | 20 ms frame VAD; SID updates; CNG from a stored LPC + energy, or digital silence | VAD ≈ 0.2–0.5 MIPS; CNG decoder-side small | pauses replaced by smooth synthetic noise (different texture/spectrum from the real background, and possibly level-mismatched) or by dead silence; switches at frame boundaries | pause noise is **frame-stationary and statistically too smooth**, its spectrum frozen between SID updates; transitions at 20 ms multiples; on this device largely **redundant with the VAS** (which already drops quiet passages, A-008/A-022) |
| d | Decoder short-term (and pitch) postfilter | G.723.1-like γ_n/γ_d ≈ 0.65/0.75 + tilt; possibly a pitch postfilter | ≈ 0.5–1 MIPS | coding noise between formants lowered; slightly "muffled/sharpened" timbre; on noise input it shapes hiss into moving formant-like resonances | no adaptation transient; operates identically on any level; affects the spectral tilt of decoded noise; already in the model |
| e | DC / rumble high-pass | first- or second-order, 60–150 Hz (G.729 uses 140 Hz in its pre-processing; G.723.1 a DC-removal filter — from the standard texts, not re-verified here) | ≈ 0.05 MIPS | none audible behind the codec's band edge | extra loss at 150–300 Hz beyond the codec template (S-005: 300 Hz loss ≈ +0.07 dB) |
| f | The codec on noise | the coder itself (A-041) | — | swirling: hiss rendered as fluctuating, frame-rate (20 ms) modulated, formant-like coloured noise; low-level frames may be pulled down by the gain predictor | present on every unit whatever NR exists; must be subtracted out by comparing with the emulator's NR-off rendering |

- **Interaction with the emulator's VAS (spec 003):** the VAS threshold is −40 dBFS at level 3 and the modeled capsule hiss is about −62 dBFS (A-022, A-048), so in drop mode hiss-only passages are already removed before any NR would act; NR would matter mainly (i) inside kept passages (noise under and between words shorter than the VAS hold), (ii) with VAS off, and (iii) in noisy rooms where the background exceeds the VAS threshold, which is exactly where the VAS never pauses. If the firmware's NR ran **before** the VAS decision (the task places NR after it; S-002 cannot show the firmware order), an NR that removes 10 dB of stationary noise would raise the background level at which the VAS still pauses by about the same amount — a capture-visible interaction (test T7 below).
- **Interaction with the codec (spec 008):** an a1/a2 suppressor feeds the ACELP coder a signal whose noise floor carries musical-noise blips; a 4 kbit/s pulse codebook renders isolated narrowband bursts as short tonal chirps, and the gain predictor smooths frame energies, so the "musical" residue would come out softer and more warbled than in uncoded spectral subtraction. A suppressor sharing the coder's look-ahead adds little delay (≤ one frame overlap, ~10 ms); a separate one would add 10–20 ms to the 30 ms codec delay (A-040). The postfilter (d) and swirl (f) already make the model's decoded hiss lower in valleys and fluctuating; NR is distinguishable from them only by **adaptation in time** and **suppression of stationary tones**, which (d) and (f) do not produce.
- **Sound vs. "noise reduction" marketing:** any of (b)–(e) could be what a 1990s brochure or reviewer called "noise reduction"; S-004's claim does not discriminate. The coder's postfilter (d) alone is the cheapest explanation of a claim of "reduced noise" and requires no extra stage.
- Matsushita's own suppressor design contains an explicit anti-musical-noise measure (random phase in filled bins) and LPC-based spectral emphasis — so if the device ran it, the expected residue is more diffuse than textbook spectral subtraction and the output spectrum of speech would be slightly formant-sharpened even before the coder's postfilter.

### Gaps
- No MIPS or memory figures for IS-127's suppressor or for Matsushita's canceller were found; the cost column is my estimate from FFT operation counts.
- The IS-127 standard text itself was not read; the −13 dB minimum gain, the 16 channels and the update rules are from patents describing it.
- G.723.1 Annex A's SID size and G.729's 140 Hz pre-filter were not verified against the ITU texts in this pass (both are in the project's S-008/S-009 documents for a later check).
- No period description of a noise expander inside a speech DSP was found; parameters in row (b) are typical engineering values, not sourced.

## 4. What did other 1990s digital voice recorders and answering-machine chipsets do about noise?

### Takeaway
The period evidence found shows **level detection and gain control, not noise suppression**, in answering-machine DSPs (Dallas DS2132A, 1995); DSP Group's TAD chip family (D6000 "Xchip" sets, used in custom versions by Sanyo and PhoneMate) is documented only for compression and storage. No 1993–1999 IC recorder or TAD chipset with a documented spectral noise suppressor was found. Noise suppression in the era was a **cellular** feature (IS-127 EVRC; Matsushita's PDC-oriented patents), and "noise cut / noise cancel" as a named IC-recorder feature shows up in sources only much later (Sony NCF, Sanyo 2008).

### Cited Findings
- Dallas DS2132A/Q "Digital Answering Machine Processor" (© 1995): two compression algorithms (9.8 and 4.9 kbit/s; 7 or 14 min in 4 Mbit), DTMF and fax-tone detection, echo cancellation (for DTMF detection), "Precise signal level detection capability", "Record/Playback gain control"; the host performs AGC from the energy level. No noise suppression, VAD or comfort noise is listed. — [DS2132A datasheet](https://www.nyang-tech.com/datasheet/1761322853/Analog-Devices-Inc.-Maxim-Integrated/DS2132A.pdf)
- DSP Group developed the first TAD speech processor (1989); its D6000 family of "Xchip sets" were used in custom versions by Sanyo and PhoneMate; the D6005 combined a speech-compression DSP, a memory/host ASIC and a codec. No noise-suppression feature is mentioned. — [Wikipedia, DSP Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSP_Group); [Tech Monitor article on DSP Group's D6000 family](https://www.techmonitor.ai/?p=169971)
- Panasonic's own 1997 selection guide shows its answering-phone reference design with analog "Recording/playback ICs for answering phone" (AN6182K/S) and its digital phones with MN19xx speech processors; no recorder- or TAD-specific noise function is listed. — [Panasonic 1997 Semiconductor Selection Guide](http://www.bitsavers.org/components/panasonic/1997_Panasonic_Semiconductor_Selection_Guide.pdf)
- Searches for 1997–1998 IC recorders with a "noise cancel" feature (Olympus, Sony, Sanyo) returned only modern products: Sony's low-cut and "NCF (Noise Cut)" filters, and Sanyo's ICR-PS603RM "noise cancelation" (2008). — [Sony ICD Help Guide](https://helpguide.sony.net/icd/b14/v1/en/contents/TP0000340730.html); [TechCrunch, Sanyo IC recorders (2008)](https://techcrunch.com/2008/11/13/sanyo-unveils-three-super-advanced-ic-recorders/)
- The RR-DR60's operating-instruction table of contents (ManualsLib) shows "Changing Microphone Sensitivity" and "Troubleshooting" but no noise-reduction item (contents list only; manual body not read in this pass). — [ManualsLib RR-DR60 product page](https://www.manualslib.com/products/Panasonic-Rr-Dr60-619491.html)

### Inferences
- In the device's market segment, a plain energy-threshold design (the VAS, plus perhaps an expander) is the period norm; a spectral suppressor would have been unusual but within Matsushita's in-house capability (section 2). The absence of "noise reduction" from S-002's specification table and (apparently) from the manual's contents weighs against a marketed NR feature; it does not exclude an unadvertised one.
- S-004's "noise reduction" claim may conflate the VAS (which removes silent stretches), the codec postfilter, or the AGC limiter; none of these needs a dedicated NR stage.

### Gaps
- DSP Group, TI (e.g. TMS320C5x-based TAD solutions), OKI (MSM6xxx ADPCM recorders), Sanyo and Sharp TAD/recorder datasheets from 1995–1999 were not found online in this pass; whether any included noise suppression is open.
- The RR-DR60 operating instructions' body text (does it mention noise anywhere?) and any Panasonic Japan press release or catalog page for the RR-DR60 / its Japanese twin were not read.

## 5. How to tell from captures of a real unit whether noise reduction exists, and which kind

### Takeaway
Four properties separate the candidates: **adaptation in time** (spectral subtraction learns the noise over ~0.3–1 s; a gate, the postfilter and the codec do not), **stationarity selectivity** (a minimum-tracking suppressor attenuates a sustained tone; nothing else does), **level dependence** (an expander's attenuation depends on absolute level; a suppressor's on SNR), and **pause texture** (CNG gives frozen synthetic noise, zeroing gives silence, a gate gives breathing). Every test should run with the **VAS off** and be compared against the emulator's rendering of the same stimulus with NR bypassed, so the codec's swirl and postfilter (f, d) cancel out.

### Cited Findings
- Minimum-statistics and persistence-based estimators need a window of past frames (Martin 1994; Matsushita's 30-frame sustaining number), which sets the adaptation time; IS-127 updates its noise estimate in energy minima / non-voice frames. — [RWTH IKS, Martin 1994](https://www.iks.rwth-aachen.de/publikationen/publications-detail//martin94b/); [US6799160B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6799160B2/en); [US 7,555,075](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7555075)
- IS-127-type suppressors hold the gain between a minimum (about −13 dB in the cited example) and 0 dB as a function of channel SNR. — [US 7,555,075](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7555075)
- Musical noise is randomly placed, frame-to-frame isolated spectral peaks. — [US 7,133,824](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7133824)
- Swirl is a temporal fluctuation of decoded power and spectrum in stationary backgrounds, from the coder's parameter quantization. — [US 8,457,953](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8457953)
- The project's capture conventions: the A-029 anchor (full scale ≈ 90 dB SPL at 1 kHz, ±10 dB), the VAS on by default in drop mode (A-008), the frame-length and coder-structure tests already listed for A-040/A-041. — [project assumptions.md](../../../assumptions.md)

### Inferences
Proposed capture tests (stimuli played acoustically to the microphone from a calibrated speaker at a fixed distance, or through an injection lead into the mic node if a unit is opened; record → play back via the earphone jack into a clean interface; VAS **off** unless stated; repeat at two sensitivity settings; each takes under 5 minutes):

- **T1 — Idle hiss and start-up transient.** 60 s recording in a quiet room (and, if possible, with the capsule sealed). Analyse the decoded noise in 20 ms frames: (i) band power vs. time — a drop of several dB within the first 0.3–1.5 s after record start, then a plateau, is the signature of an adapting suppressor (a1/a2); a flat trace argues against it; (ii) time-frequency statistics — kurtosis / proportion of isolated bins > 10 dB above their neighbours in a 128- or 256-point STFT; compare with the emulator's NR-off rendering of a −62 dBFS hiss (A-048). Expected without NR: flat level, swirl-type fluctuation only.
- **T2 — Stepped broadband noise (level sweep).** White or pink noise held 10 s each at −60, −50, −40, −30, −20 dBFS-equivalent (A-029), with 3 s gaps. Plot decoded RMS (300–2500 Hz, last 5 s of each step) against input. No NR: slope 1 (the codec tracks level within ~0.4 dB, spec 008 calibration). Expander/gate (b): slope > 1 below a knee, 1 above. Suppressor (a): roughly a constant offset (≈ the floor, e.g. 5–15 dB) at all levels once adapted, possibly releasing at high absolute levels if an absolute "noise reference power" threshold exists (US 6,799,160's 20000.0). The first 1 s of each step separately: an adapting suppressor shows the step at full level, then decaying; a gate does not.
- **T3 — Sustained tone (stationarity test; the most discriminating).** 1 kHz at −20 dBFS for 20 s, then 500 Hz and 2 kHz. Read the AES17 tone amplitude in 0.25 s windows. A minimum-tracking spectral suppressor treats a constant tone as noise after roughly its window (~0.6 s for 30 × 20 ms) and **attenuates it progressively** (by up to its floor); a gate, the postfilter, the AGC (below its knee) and the codec do not (the codec's tone rendering is level-stable; check against the emulator). Also measure a tone that is gated on/off every 0.4 s: it should pass at full level if the effect in the steady case is adaptation.
- **T4 — Tone in noise (SNR-dependent gain).** 1 kHz at −20 dBFS over white noise at −50, −40 and −30 dBFS. Measure the tone-to-noise-floor ratio in the decoded output (noise read in 300–800 Hz and 1500–2500 Hz bands) vs. input. A suppressor improves the ratio by roughly its floor at low noise and less as noise rises; a gate improves nothing once the tone opens it (floor rises to full level under the tone); no-NR leaves the ratio unchanged (minus codec effects).
- **T5 — Noise onset / offset timing.** Silence → white noise at −35 dBFS for 5 s → silence, repeated. Measure decoded noise level in 20 ms frames across each onset: an adaptation curve (time constant 0.2–1 s) means an estimator; an immediate full level means none. At offset, look for a brief "pump" (CNG/expander release) or abrupt silence. Run once with the VAS on to see whether the VAS opens on the onset before any NR adapts (order of stages).
- **T6 — Speech in noise, pause texture.** A read passage (standard phonetically balanced sentences) over steady pink noise at SNR 20, 10 and 5 dB. Compare the noise floor *between* words with the floor *under* words (estimate under-word floor from 2–3 kHz bands between formants, or by subtraction of a clean take): (a) gives a floor that is lower in pauses and recovers under speech with a ~10–100 ms ramp (US 6,799,160's 5-frame Process 2 switch predicts extra quiet after 100 ms of pause); (b) gives breathing at syllable rate; (c) CNG gives pause noise with a frozen spectrum (frame-to-frame LPC distance near zero, unlike real noise) and transitions on 20 ms multiples; zeroing gives digital silence or the codec's idle output. Also check whether pause boundaries fall on frame multiples (shared with the A-041 frame-granular VAS test).
- **T7 — VAS / NR order.** With the VAS on at a fixed level, raise steady pink noise in 2 dB steps from 10 dB below to 10 dB above the expected VAS threshold (−40 dBFS at level 3, A-022) and note the noise level at which the unit stops pausing. Compare with the emulator's VAS-only prediction: a stop level about 5–15 dB **higher** than predicted means a suppressor acts before the VAS decision; agreement means the VAS sees un-suppressed audio (NR after it, or none).
- **T8 — Low-frequency region.** Tones at 100, 150, 200, 250, 300 Hz at −20 dBFS. Compare the loss relative to 1 kHz with the codec template (S-005; A-027's 225 Hz edge at 6 kHz). Extra loss of more than ~1 dB at 200–300 Hz suggests an additional firmware high-pass (e). Weak test: the template already falls steeply here.
- **T9 — Codec-noise control (separate swirl from NR).** White noise at −30 dBFS, 30 s, both on the real unit and through the emulator with NR bypassed. Compare: frame-to-frame spectral flux (LPC-envelope distance per 20 ms), the long-term spectrum, and the level. Swirl appears in both; any extra level drop, isolated-bin blips, or adaptation on the unit is attributable to a stage the model lacks. This control should accompany every test above.
- **T10 — Playback-side gate (if suspected).** A record-side test cannot isolate a decoder-side squelch, but a decoder gate would show as decoded hiss vanishing below a level in T2 even with the VAS off, with no adaptation and with a hysteresis loop when the noise level is ramped up then down in T2's steps (repeat T2 descending). An expander on the record side gives the same static curve; telling them apart would need access to the stored frames (not possible on this device without opening the flash).

Decision rule summary: adaptation (T1, T5) + tone suppression (T3) ⇒ spectral suppressor (a); a static knee in T2 without adaptation or tone suppression ⇒ expander/gate (b); frozen-spectrum pause noise or digital silence at frame boundaries in T6 ⇒ VAD/CNG or zeroing (c); none of these, with the unit matching the emulator in T9 ⇒ no NR beyond the postfilter and codec (d, f), which supports keeping A-009 at "optional, off by default" or retiring it.

- For the emulator: if an NR stage is ever modeled, the Matsushita design (US 6,799,160 Table 10) is a fully parameterized, era- and company-appropriate template to register as a low-confidence assumption, with the tone-suppression and adaptation-time figures above as the measurable properties to calibrate against captures.

### Gaps
- No captures from a real unit exist (project status); all predicted magnitudes are from patents and engineering estimates, not measurements of the RR-DR60.
- The acoustic SPL anchor (A-029, ±10 dB) makes absolute thresholds in T2 and T7 uncertain by up to ±10 dB; relative effects (adaptation, tone suppression, slopes) are robust to it.
